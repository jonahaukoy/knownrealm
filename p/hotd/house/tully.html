House Tully

Lords of Riverrun and Lords Paramount of the Trident, raised to rule the fertile, much-fought-over Riverlands after Aegon's Conquest. A silver trout leaping on a field of red and blue — though old Lord Grover's grip has gone soft, and it is his quarrelsome bannermen, Bracken and Blackwood first among them, who will decide which dragon the rivers follow.

The fuller tale

Riverrun is a young seat by the standards of the houses around it — raised to rule the Trident only after Aegon’s Conquest, rather than descending from any line of river kings — and Family, Duty, Honor reads less like an ancient boast than a founding instruction manual for a house that had to learn quickly how to hold ground everyone else in Westeros keeps marching across. The silver trout on red and blue guards the richest, most fertile, and most fought-over crossroads in the Seven Kingdoms, precisely because every army moving between the crownlands and anywhere else eventually has to cross a Tully ford to get there.

By the Dance, that guardianship rests in hands too old to hold it firmly. Lord Grover Tully is ancient and ailing, more a name invoked for legitimacy than a lord actually issuing commands, and the gap between his nominal authority and his actual capacity is exactly the kind of opening the riverlands’ more quarrelsome bannermen have been waiting for — a liege lord too soft-gripped to tell the Brackens and Blackwoods, or the Freys at the Twins, that their private arithmetic has to wait on his.

The Trident becomes, as a direct result, the single most fought-over stretch of ground in the entire war — not because Riverrun commands a great host into the field, but because everyone else’s host has to pass through, over, or beside its lands to reach anywhere that matters. Daemon Targaryen makes Harrenhal his own base of operations within the riverlands almost as soon as the war begins, and the Battle of the Red Fork, which costs Jason Lannister his life and the Westerlands its leadership, is fought on Tully soil between armies that barely bothered to ask Riverrun’s opinion first.

What actually holds the Tully banner to the Blacks through the fighting is not Grover but the grandson increasingly acting in his stead, Elmo Tully — a steadier hand than his grandfather’s failing one, and the man who eventually musters what the riverlands can still put in the field for the war’s final push, after two years of other people’s armies tearing up the ground he is supposed to be lording over.

That push arrives at King’s Landing in the war’s last act, a riverlands host under Elmo converging on the capital in the same narrow window as Cregan Stark’s northmen and Jeyne Arryn’s ships out of Gulltown — three loyalties, three separate roads south, meeting at almost the same hour outside a city that has, by then, run out of anyone left to defend it. Riverrun ends the Dance scarred by more marching feet than any other great seat in the war, on the winning side of a fight it spent most of two years merely absorbing rather than commanding.

The page and the screen

The show leans on Daemon’s occupation of Harrenhal, staged at length on screen, to carry most of the riverlands’ presence in the war, while giving Lord Grover Tully’s incapacity and Elmo Tully’s quiet rise to actual command comparatively little airtime by comparison — a chronicle-level detail Fire & Blood spells out plainly but that the screen version mostly leaves implied. As of its current run, the show has also not yet dramatized the war’s final convergence on the capital that the book credits jointly to Elmo Tully, Cregan Stark and Jeyne Arryn, so the riverlands’ part in that closing act remains, for now, a page-only chapter.
