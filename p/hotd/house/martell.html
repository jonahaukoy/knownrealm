House Martell

Princes of Dorne, who alone of all Westeros never bowed to the dragons. A red sun pierced by a golden spear, ruling the hot southern deserts from Sunspear by Rhoynish law and custom ÔÇö and wise enough, when the dragons dance, to watch from behind the Red Mountains.

The fuller tale

It is worth stating plainly what the era itself makes obvious and later centuries tend to blur: during the Dance, Dorne is not part of the Seven Kingdoms at all. The marriage that eventually binds Sunspear to the Iron Throne is still decades in the future, and the Princes of Dorne who watch this war unfold do so as rulers of a fully sovereign country, owing KingÔÇÖs Landing nothing ÔÇö not troops, not taxes, not so much as an opinion.

That sovereignty was purchased the hard way, against dragons rather than alongside them ÔÇö AegonÔÇÖs own attempts to add Dorne to his conquest broke against its deserts and its refusal to fight the war Targaryen strategy expected, costing the dynasty dragons of its own in the process. A Targaryen civil war two generations later reads, from Sunspear, less like a crisis to be weighed in than like a debt finally coming due on its own account, with no help required from the far side of the Red Mountains to collect it.

So Dorne does exactly what its history taught it to do when Targaryens turn violent among themselves: nothing, deliberately and at length. No Dornish banner marches for either crown, no Dornish port opens to either fleet, and every dragon spent burning a fellow dragon over the Gods Eye or Shipbreaker Bay is one more dragon that will never again threaten a Dornish garden ÔÇö an outcome Sunspear can only regard as, at minimum, no great loss.

The policy holds because it costs Dorne nothing to hold it. Unbowed, Unbent, Unbroken is not a wartime slogan improvised for this crisis; it is simply the settled, generations-old Martell answer to any Targaryen quarrel, and the Dance changes that answer not at all ÔÇö the union with the Iron Throne that finally arrives will come from a marriage bed a lifetime later, not from anything decided in this war.
