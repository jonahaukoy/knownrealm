S1·E2 — The Rogue Prince

Viserys I Targaryen holds the throne Hand: Ser Otto Hightower

The hour retold

An hour about a chair sitting empty and everyone in the realm quietly measuring themselves against it. Daemon, sulking on Dragonstone, seizes the island with his gold cloaks and steals a dragon's egg — the one meant for the cradle of the son Viserys just buried — less because he wants it than because he wants his brother to come and take it back. What arrives instead is a bridge full of drawn steel and a fifteen-year-old princess landing Syrax between the swords, ending the standoff by daring her uncle to kill her in front of a dragon. It is the first time the show lets Rhaenyra be the most dangerous person in a room, and it will not be the last.

The episode's real damage is done in a council chamber and a garden. The Sea Snake offers his twelve-year-old daughter Laena as the new queen, a match that would bind the realm's fire to its fleet and give the crown the sea power it is about to badly need. Viserys chooses Alicent Hightower instead — companionship over strategy, the girl who reads to him over the alliance that might have saved him — and Corlys Velaryon leaves court cold, twice insulted. The Stepstones burn in the background, the Crabfeeder nailing Westerosi sailors to posts while the crown does nothing, and a spurned Sea Snake brings his ships to Daemon to fight a war the king refused to.

What happens

  • Daemon takes Dragonstone with his gold cloaks and steals a dragon's egg — the one meant for Baelon's cradle.
  • Otto leads a party to the island; steel is bared on the bridge until Rhaenyra lands Syrax and dares her uncle to kill her.
  • Corlys and Rhaenys offer Viserys their daughter Laena, aged twelve; the king chooses Alicent Hightower instead, and the Sea Snake leaves court cold.
  • The Crabfeeder, backed by the Triarchy, nails Westerosi sailors to posts in the Stepstones — and the crown does nothing.
  • Spurned by the throne, Corlys brings his fleet to Daemon: a war for the Stepstones, fought without the king's leave.
