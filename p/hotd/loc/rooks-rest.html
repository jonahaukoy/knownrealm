Rook's Rest

Seat of House Staunton

A modest castle on the bay's north coast between Duskendale and Crackclaw Point — two days from King's Landing by road, far less by wing.

The fuller tale

Rook’s Rest, a modest Staunton castle two days from King’s Landing by road and far less by wing, hosts one of the Dance’s few genuinely decisive battles — and it is a trap from the start. Aegon II’s faction lets word reach Rhaenyra that the castle is lightly held and ripe for the taking, hoping to draw a black dragon within reach of two green ones waiting in ambush; Rhaenys Targaryen, unwilling to let her queen fly into a snare meant for her, answers the bait herself, astride old Meleys.

The battle that follows is the Dance’s first true dragon-on-dragon slaughter. Meleys, ancient and canny, very nearly wins outright — Aegon II is grievously burned and Sunfyre crippled before Aemond arrives on Vhagar, the largest living dragon in the world, and the arithmetic finally turns against the Queen Who Never Was. Rhaenys and Meleys fall together over Rook’s Rest, taking with them any illusion that this war might stay a war of armies rather than a war of dragons.

Rook’s Rest itself is almost incidental to the battle fought in its sky — a small castle that gives its name to the moment the Dance stops being political and starts being an extermination, one dragon at a time, of the very creatures that built the dynasty fighting over them.

The chronicle here

  • Criston Cole takes Duskendale — Lord Darklyn keeps his loyalty and loses his head — then marches on Rook's Rest by night. (S2·E4)
  • Rhaenys and Meleys answer Rook's Rest; the trap closes: Aegon comes half-drunk on Sunfyre, and Aemond brings Vhagar for both dragons. (S2·E4)
  • Cole takes Duskendale and Rook's Rest by march and treachery; the trap at Rook's Rest is the war's first dragonfall. Rhaenys Targaryen, the Queen Who Never Was, answers the castle's ravens and finds two dragons waiting: Aegon on Sunfyre the Golden and Aemond on Vhagar. Meleys the Red Queen dies with her teeth in Sunfyre's throat; the king falls with her, burned half to nothing, and the realm passes to the regent Aemond — one eye, one dragon, no restraint. (F&B 4)
