The City Watch

The watchmen of King's Landing, two thousand strong — given their gold cloaks and their teeth by Prince Daemon, and loyal to his memory long after the crown forgot it.

The fuller tale

The gold cloaks exist, in this era, largely because Daemon Targaryen decided King’s Landing’s law needed teeth rather than a ledger — two thousand men recruited, armed and paid to actually enforce the crown’s law in a city that had mostly been policing itself by neglect, a project Daemon treated as seriously as any military command he ever held and considerably more personally than most.

Command passes, after Daemon, to Harwin Strong — Breakbones, whose nickname the Watch takes rather literally as a statement of intent — and under Strong the gold cloaks become as much a political instrument as a police force, an army inside the capital’s walls that answers, in practice, to whoever currently holds its captain’s loyalty rather than to the crown in the abstract.

That is precisely the vulnerability the Dance exposes. Once Daemon is gone from the city and Harwin Strong is sent away from it, the Watch’s loyalty stops being a settled question and becomes a running negotiation — two thousand armed men in a capital that changes hands more than once during the war, and a force whose willingness to hold the streets, or open them, ends up mattering as much to who actually controls King’s Landing at any given hour as any army approaching from outside it.

The page and the screen

The show dramatizes the Watch’s founding directly, with Daemon personally arming and drilling the recruits in a sequence built for the screen; Fire & Blood treats the same reform more as an administrative fact of Daemon’s early career, worth a paragraph rather than a set piece, consistent with a source that generally prefers to summarize institutions rather than watch them being built.
