How it ends for them

On the screen. Fell at Rook's Rest, refusing to run (S2┬ÀE4)

The Queen Who Never Was answered Rook's Rest's ravens and flew into a trap set for any dragon that came. Clear of the fight, she turned Meleys back into Vhagar's teeth rather than leave the sky to them ÔÇö and fell with her Red Queen, refusing to the last to run.

On the page. Fell at Rook's Rest with Meleys (F&B, ch. 4)

At Rook's Rest the Queen Who Never Was met two dragons laid in ambush. Meleys the Red Queen locked jaws with Sunfyre and near killed the king before Vhagar broke them both. Rhaenys died as she had lived ÔÇö owed a crown, paying its price anyway.

Both tellings put Rhaenys's death at Rook's Rest, and both agree on its essential shape even where they differ on detail. In the show, Rhaenys and Meleys engage Sunfyre directly, wounding Aegon's dragon badly and Aegon himself nearly fatally, before Vhagar ÔÇö vastly larger, ancient beyond any dragon then flying ÔÇö closes on the Red Queen from above. Rhaenys has a clear window to flee; she turns to fight instead, and Vhagar's jaws take Meleys out of the sky in a single, devastating strike, rider and dragon falling together into the battlefield below.

Fire & Blood's account agrees on the outcome and disputes some of the choreography ÔÇö Mushroom's version has Rhaenys actively pressing the attack out of fury rather than tactical necessity, while Eustace frames her engagement as reluctant and honorable. Both sources agree that her death, whatever the exact sequence, took Sunfyre and Aegon effectively out of the war for its middle stretch, and both sources agree that she died owed a crown the realm never gave her, and paid its price regardless.

Where the page and the screen part

The show stages Rhaenys's Dragonpit restraint as a deliberate, almost mythic beat ÔÇö Rhaenys and Meleys looming over Aegon's coronation with the power to end the war in its first hour and visibly, consciously choosing otherwise, a scene invented for the series to dramatize what the book only implies was possible. Fire & Blood is considerably more matter-of-fact about the same morning, treating her presence and restraint as one detail among many rather than as a staged confrontation, and gives Rook's Rest itself in sparer, more contested terms ÔÇö Mushroom and Eustace disagree on tactical particulars the show resolves cleanly on screen.
