The fuller tale

Called Breakbones for his strength, Harwin Strong is by wide consensus the finest knight in the realm during his years of service — captain of the City Watch's gold cloaks and, less officially but more consequentially, sworn protector of Princess Rhaenyra's household through precisely the years her three sons are born with unmistakably brown hair in a family of silver.

The court's whispers about the boys' true parentage settle, more than on anyone else, on Harwin — a suspicion neither confirmed nor denied by anyone with actual knowledge, but persistent enough that it eventually costs him his position, his standing, and finally his safety.

When the scandal grows too large to manage quietly, Harwin is dismissed from the gold cloaks and sent home to Harrenhal to his father — ostensibly a retreat from court politics, though both tellings make clear the timing is anything but coincidental, arriving just before the fire that kills him.

The page and the screen

The show makes Larys's role in Harwin's death explicit and deliberate — engineered specifically to sever Rhaenyra's household from the man whose paternity of her sons was becoming impossible to keep quiet. Fire & Blood, characteristically, keeps the cause of the Harrenhal fire disputed among its own sources, refusing to confirm what the show stages as clear premeditated murder.

The books: Burned at Harrenhal (F&B, ch. 1)

Breakbones — the strongest knight in the realm, and the true father of the princess's sons — was dismissed from the gold cloaks and sent home to Harrenhal. His brother's fire found him there the same night, locked in with his father. What Larys wanted buried burned instead.

In the show, Harwin is dismissed and sent home the same night his brother's fire finds him — locked into Harrenhal alongside his father, the blaze arranged, the series makes clear, by Larys himself, disposing of the one man whose knowledge of the princess's sons' true parentage threatened the crown's stability.

Fire & Blood records his death in the same fire in 120 AC, in the same disputed circumstances as his father's — whether Larys's ambition, Harrenhal's old curse, or a king's quiet housekeeping caused it, the histories cannot agree, though the book notes dryly that whoever benefited most from the timing is not hard to identify.
