How it ends for them

On the page. Given to Sunfyre in Dragonstone's yard (F&B, ch. 7)

Fleeing her fallen city, the queen was sold by Dragonstone's own garrison to the brother she'd danced against. Aegon fed her to Sunfyre in the castle yard while her son watched ÔÇö and made a martyr with a half-year's reign and three hundred years of argument.

The show has not yet reached this chapter of the war; Rhaenyra's end remains, as of the series' current seasons, unfilmed. Fire & Blood does not extend her the same mercy of delay.

Driven from King's Landing as her own capital turns on her, Rhaenyra flees to Dragonstone ÔÇö her birthplace, her first refuge, and finally her trap. The book's account has the castle's own garrison, no longer willing to die for a losing queen, seize her and hand her to her half-brother Aegon II in exchange for their lives. Aegon has her brought to the yard where Sunfyre still lives, broken and dying from his own wounds, and orders the dragon to finish what the war could not: Rhaenyra is fed to Sunfyre before her surviving son's eyes, a public execution staged specifically for its cruelty. Even Aegon's own council calls it a mistake ÔÇö a martyrdom instead of a punishment, and the last image the Dance leaves the realm to argue over for the next three hundred years.

Where the page and the screen part

The show, still working its way toward the book's darkest chapters, has not yet dramatized Rhaenyra's final defeat and death ÔÇö its aired seasons leave her a hunted queen mid-war, her ending still to be filmed. Fire & Blood, by contrast, carries her all the way through, and does so with a bluntness the show's slower pacing hasn't caught up to: where the series lingers on Rhaenyra's interiority and grief, Gyldayn's sources reduce her final weeks to a fugitive's itinerary, judged by hostile chroniclers who had every reason to make her downfall sound deserved.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
