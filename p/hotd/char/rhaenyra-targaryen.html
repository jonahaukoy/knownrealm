Rhaenyra Targaryen

House TargaryenThe Blacks — the Queen's Party

The Realm's Delight: Viserys's firstborn, named Princess of Dragonstone and heir to the Iron Throne over every precedent in the realm's memory. Rider of Syrax, wed first to Laenor Velaryon and then to her uncle Daemon — a woman raised to a crown half the realm swore she could never wear.

A soul of House Targaryen, born at King's Landing, sworn to The Blacks — the Queen's Party.

The fuller tale

Season One

In The Heirs of the Dragon — Cupbearer, dragonrider, and — by episode's end — heir to the Iron Throne. Viserys tells Rhaenyra the secret every king carries — Aegon's dream, the Song of Ice and Fire — and names her heir. The lords of the realm, Corlys and Rhaenys among them, kneel and swear to Rhaenyra Targaryen, first of her name. In The Rogue Prince — Ends a standoff by putting a dragon between the swords.

Otto leads a party to the island; steel is bared on the bridge until Rhaenyra lands Syrax and dares her uncle to kill her. In Second of His Name — Blood to the elbow at the hunt — and still not son enough for the court. Rhaenyra rides out angry, kills a boar in the dark with Ser Criston, and watches a white hart — the king's omen — walk away unkinged. In King of the Narrow Sea — One night in the city costs a Hand his badge and a knight his vows.

Rhaenyra tires of suitors counting her castles; Daemon shows her the city instead — Flea Bottom, a mummer's farce about her succession, a pleasure house. What starts in the pleasure house Daemon cannot finish; what Rhaenyra starts that night with Ser Criston, she does. In We Light the Way — Weds the realm's most eligible man on the realm's saddest wedding day. Viserys sails to Driftmark to mend the realm's two dragon houses: Rhaenyra will wed Laenor Velaryon, and their sons will sit the throne.

Rhaenyra and Laenor strike a private bargain — a marriage of duty, with room for what each of them actually wants. In The Princess and the Queen — Offers the queen a marriage pact for peace; gets a decade of cold war instead. Ten years pass. Rhaenyra births her third son, Joffrey — and carries him, still bloody, across the castle because the queen commanded a look at him. Rhaenyra sees the court for what it is and removes her family to Dragonstone.

In Driftmark — Buries a friend, bleeds for her sons, and finally marries the man she chose at ten. Laena's daughters and Rhaenyra's sons fall on him in the yard; knives and fists in the dark, and Lucerys cuts out Aemond's eye. Alicent demands an eye for an eye; when the king refuses, she takes the blade from his own belt and cuts Rhaenyra's arm reaching for Luke's face. In The Lord of the Tides — Wins Driftmark for her son and loses her father by morning.

Rhaenyra and Daemon come back to a changed Red Keep — Otto restored as Hand, Alicent ruling a king kept swimming in milk of the poppy. Vaemond names Rhaenyra a whore and her sons bastards before the throne — Daemon takes his head off mid-sentence. 'He can keep his tongue.' In The Black Queen — Crowned, bereaved twice over, and — in the last shot of the season — done with restraint. Word of the usurpation reaches Dragonstone; the shock sends Rhaenyra into early labor, and she buries a daughter, Visenya, the same day.

Ser Erryk Cargyll arrives with a stolen crown; Daemon sets Viserys's crown on Rhaenyra's head on the shore of Dragonstone.

Where they stand. King's Landing and Dragonstone.

Season Two

In A Son for a Son — Searches the wreck-strewn coast for what Vhagar left of her son. Rhaenyra combs the shores of Shipbreaker Bay until she finds a wing of Arrax — and speaks her first words in an episode of grief: 'I want Aemond Targaryen.' In Rhaenyra the Cruel — Wakes to steel in her own chamber and two dead men who shared one face. Otto turns Jaehaerys's funeral into a procession through the streets — grief as statecraft, 'Rhaenyra the Cruel' on every crier's lips.

Ser Arryk Cargyll is sent to Dragonstone wearing his twin's face; Ser Erryk finds him at Rhaenyra's door. In The Burning Mill — Risks everything on one last conversation in the sept. It buys nothing. Rhaenyra slips into King's Landing in a septa's robes to ask Alicent, face to face, if war is truly past stopping. It is. Rhaenyra sends Rhaena to the Vale with the youngest children and a clutch of dragon eggs; Jace chafes at being an heir kept from the war.

In Smallfolk — Discovers her sharpest weapon is bread with a queen's seal on it. Hunger bites King's Landing; Rhaenyra and Mysaria feed the city from black ships, and the smallfolk begin to love the queen who feeds them. In The Red Sowing — Trades pure blood for airpower and wins the arithmetic of the war. Addam's claiming shows the way: Rhaenyra summons Targaryen bastards and dragon-blooded smallfolk from every brothel and shipyard — the Red Sowing.

Jace watches smallfolk mount dragons and feels his own claim thin; Rhaenyra now commands more dragons than the greens can ever answer. In The Queen Who Ever Was — Hears her enemy's mother offer her King's Landing — and knows the price will be paid in fire anyway. At Harrenhal, Daemon's visions end in the one that matters — the Song of Ice and Fire — and when Rhaenyra lands, he kneels: her king consort, her army, hers. Larys Strong smuggles the broken Aegon out of King's Landing before Rhaenyra's dragons can find him.

Where they stand. Storm's End, Dragonstone and King's Landing.

Their story

Rhaenyra is born the Realm's Delight in the story's telling and, more precisely, the accidental heir — her birth uneventful, her position secure only because her father has, for the first years of her life, no son to prefer over her. She grows up cupbearer to Viserys's small council, present at the table where the realm's business is done, absorbing statecraft the way other princesses absorb needlework. When her mother Aemma dies delivering the son who does not survive the day, Viserys does the thing no Targaryen king has ever done: he names his living daughter, not some cousin or infant half-brother, his heir, and marches every lord in the Seven Kingdoms through an oath to honor it.

Being named heir and being treated as one prove to be different educations. Rhaenyra spends her father's reign watching the court test the boundaries of an oath sworn under duress — courted by lords who would rather marry her than kneel to her, shadowed by a Kingsguard sworn to protect her and, in Ser Criston Cole's case, briefly loved by her in a way neither of them can afford. Her father's second marriage to Alicent Hightower, once her closest friend, turns the court into two waiting rooms, and every year Alicent bears another son sharpens the question everyone is too polite to ask aloud: will the old king's word actually hold.

Her own marriage is arranged for policy — to Laenor Velaryon, heir to Driftmark, a match that binds two fleets and asks nothing of either party's heart, since both have already given it elsewhere. Three sons follow, Jacaerys, Lucerys and Joffrey, and the court's cruelest ongoing joke is the boys' brown hair in a family of silver — a paternity question Rhaenyra never answers and the realm never stops asking. When Laenor is done being useful to the story, she marries her uncle Daemon in secret and in defiance of half her father's court, binding the dynasty's two sharpest branches into one household and one cause.

The show gives this life a particular shape — a guarded princess maturing into a watchful queen-in-waiting, a friendship with Alicent that curdles in real time across a decade of small betrayals, a Driftmark council where Rhaenyra defends her sons' legitimacy with a fury that convinces no one and satisfies everyone who loves her. Fire & Blood tells a colder, more contested version of the same years, filtered through Septon Eustace's sympathy and Mushroom's scandal-mongering, in which Rhaenyra's private life is less knowable and the paternity question is treated, by half the sources, as settled fact rather than rumor.

Her father's death is the hinge the whole story turns on. While Rhaenyra is confined to childbed on Dragonstone, the green council in King's Landing crowns Aegon before she can be told Viserys is even dead, and the crown she was sworn a kingdom's oaths to inherit is simply taken while she is occupied with the business of extending the family line. She receives the news of the usurpation and, within the same terrible span of days, the death of her second son Lucerys over Shipbreaker Bay and the loss of a stillborn daughter — three griefs compressed into one household, and the queen who wanted a peaceful coronation is remade into the one who wants a war.

As queen on Dragonstone, and later in King's Landing itself, Rhaenyra runs the black council with a hardness her father never had to develop — authorizing Blood and Cheese's contract in answer to her son's murder, sending her remaining sons and dragons into a war that consumes them one by one, and finally taking the throne her father promised her for a reign measured in months rather than the decades she waited for it. Fire & Blood's account of her rule is unsparing about how quickly popularity curdles into hatred once the crown actually sits on a woman's head — the city that once loved the Realm's Delight turns on Queen Rhaenyra with a speed that shocks even her enemies.

By the time the Dance is done, Rhaenyra has spent every currency the crown was supposed to be worth — sons, dragons, a capital, her own safety — and gained a throne she can hold only by dragonfire and only for a season. The books' unsentimental accounting gives her half a year as queen and three centuries as the losing side's version of history, told by the winners; the fuller record insists on the plainer fact underneath the propaganda: she was named heir by a king and every lord in the realm swore to it, and the realm broke its word first.

How it ends for them

On the page. Given to Sunfyre in Dragonstone's yard (F&B, ch. 7)

Fleeing her fallen city, the queen was sold by Dragonstone's own garrison to the brother she'd danced against. Aegon fed her to Sunfyre in the castle yard while her son watched — and made a martyr with a half-year's reign and three hundred years of argument.

The show has not yet reached this chapter of the war; Rhaenyra's end remains, as of the series' current seasons, unfilmed. Fire & Blood does not extend her the same mercy of delay.

Driven from King's Landing as her own capital turns on her, Rhaenyra flees to Dragonstone — her birthplace, her first refuge, and finally her trap. The book's account has the castle's own garrison, no longer willing to die for a losing queen, seize her and hand her to her half-brother Aegon II in exchange for their lives. Aegon has her brought to the yard where Sunfyre still lives, broken and dying from his own wounds, and orders the dragon to finish what the war could not: Rhaenyra is fed to Sunfyre before her surviving son's eyes, a public execution staged specifically for its cruelty. Even Aegon's own council calls it a mistake — a martyrdom instead of a punishment, and the last image the Dance leaves the realm to argue over for the next three hundred years.

Their chapters, book by book

Fire & Blood — The Dance of the Dragons 6 chapters that name them

A Question of Succession The Old King's council at Harrenhal passes over Rhaenys for Viserys; a generation later Viserys passes over precedent for Rhaenyra. Aemma dies of the birthing bed, Alicent weds the king, and two courts grow inside one castle — the blacks and the greens, named for the gowns worn at a tourney. Daemon wins the Stepstones, weds Laena, loses her, and weds Rhaenyra over Laenor's convenient corpse — though the book's Laenor is truly murdered, cut down at Spicetown by his own favorite, Qarl Correy. Harrenhal burns its lord and his heir; the king rots slowly on his throne and dies whispering to no one the maesters record. The Blacks and the Greens The king is a day dead before the green council has crowned a new one: Aegon, the Conqueror's crown on his head, anointed before the mob in the Dragonpit while his sister labors on Dragonstone, unknowing. Grand Maester Orwyle is sent with terms; Rhaenyra answers with her father's crown on her own head. The realm chooses sides — the Vale, the North and the rivers for the queen, Oldtown, the Rock and Storm's End for the king. And at Storm's End, in a storm, Vhagar falls upon a boy flying home with an answer. Rhaenyra Triumphant The queen's arithmetic: the Red Sowing gives dragons to bastards and boasters — Hugh Hammer on Vermithor, Ulf White on Silverwing, Addam of Hull on Seasmoke — and the sky belongs to the blacks. The price is paid in the Gullet, where the Triarchy's ninety ships break the blockade and Prince Jacaerys falls with Vermax into the sea. But King's Landing cannot be held against six dragons: the gates open, Rhaenyra takes her father's throne — and the Hightower host coming up the roseroad meets betrayal at Tumbleton, where the Two Betrayers burn the town they were sworn to. Rhaenyra Overthrown The city that opened its gates learns what a queen's taxes and a queen's fears cost. Helaena Targaryen falls from Maegor's Holdfast onto the spikes below; the smallfolk name Rhaenyra kinslayer and rise behind the Shepherd. The Dragonpit is stormed by ten thousand hands — five dragons die in it, and Prince Joffrey with them, falling from Syrax's back. Rhaenyra flees a burning city as, above the Gods Eye, the uncles end each other: Daemon leaps from Caraxes onto Vhagar's back and puts Dark Sister through Aemond's remaining eye as both dragons fall. The Short, Sad Reign of Aegon II Rhaenyra, sold by the garrison of Dragonstone, is given to Sunfyre in front of her son: the king watches his sister eaten, and thinks it justice. It buys him half a year. Second Tumbleton kills a Betrayer and the young Prince Daeron; Addam of Hull dies proving a bastard's honor at Second Tumbleton; and with the Wolf of the North marching south and no dragons left worth the name, Aegon II drinks poisoned wine poured by his own council. 'Let there be an end,' the lords agreed — and made one. The Hour of the Wolf Cregan Stark comes south too late for the war and just in time for the reckoning: for six days the Wolf of the North rules King's Landing in all but name, trying poisoners and turncloaks, taking heads where the new king's councillors would take pardons. Aegon the Younger — Rhaenyra's son, Aegon the Third — is crowned and wed to Aegon II's daughter Jaehaera, joining the broken halves. The dragons are done: a boy king who flinches from them, a realm of graves, and a peace made of exhaustion. The Dance is over. What it was for, the maesters still argue.

Where the page and the screen part

The show, still working its way toward the book's darkest chapters, has not yet dramatized Rhaenyra's final defeat and death — its aired seasons leave her a hunted queen mid-war, her ending still to be filmed. Fire & Blood, by contrast, carries her all the way through, and does so with a bluntness the show's slower pacing hasn't caught up to: where the series lingers on Rhaenyra's interiority and grief, Gyldayn's sources reduce her final weeks to a fugitive's itinerary, judged by hostile chroniclers who had every reason to make her downfall sound deserved.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
