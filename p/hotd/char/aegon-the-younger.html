How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Their chapters, book by book

Fire & Blood — The Dance of the Dragons 1 chapter that name them

The Hour of the Wolf Cregan Stark comes south too late for the war and just in time for the reckoning: for six days the Wolf of the North rules King's Landing in all but name, trying poisoners and turncloaks, taking heads where the new king's councillors would take pardons. Aegon the Younger — Rhaenyra's son, Aegon the Third — is crowned and wed to Aegon II's daughter Jaehaera, joining the broken halves. The dragons are done: a boy king who flinches from them, a realm of graves, and a peace made of exhaustion. The Dance is over. What it was for, the maesters still argue.

Where the page and the screen part

Aegon the Younger's role in both tellings is largely confined to his parentage and his dragon rather than any independent action, and the two versions track closely on the facts available — the differences that exist are chiefly matters of how much screen time and narrative attention the ongoing show chooses to give a character who, in the book, remains a minor figure throughout the war's main action.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
