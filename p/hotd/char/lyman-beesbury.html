Lyman Beesbury

Master of coin to three kings, an honest old man in a council of knives — the arithmetic of treason never added up for him.

The fuller tale

Master of coin to three successive kings, Lyman Beesbury is, by the time the succession crisis breaks open, one of the few genuinely honest men left on a small council increasingly staffed by people with a personal stake in the outcome — an old man whose loyalty is to the oath he swore rather than to whichever faction currently holds the room.

When the green council convenes to crown Aegon in the hours after Viserys's death, Lyman is the single voice willing to name what is actually happening — not a smooth succession but a violation of oaths every lord in the realm swore to Rhaenyra — and he says so plainly, in a room that has already decided it does not want to hear it.

The page and the screen

Both tellings agree Beesbury dies at that council table for objecting too loudly, though the exact manner differs by source even within Fire & Blood itself, let alone between book and show. The show settles the matter cleanly for its own narrative purposes; the book preserves the disagreement as a feature of its own unreliable-history conceit.

Where they stand, season by season

S1·E1 — King's Landing
Their fate — if death has found them, it is written here. Open at your peril.

The show: Opened at the green council's table (S1·E9)

The books: Died objecting — the accounts differ on how (F&B, ch. 2)

Master of coin to three kings, he alone at the green council refused the word 'succession' for what it was — treason against the named heir. Ser Criston opened his throat on the table, and the council continued around the body.

In the show, Ser Criston Cole opens his throat across the table in front of the assembled council, and the meeting continues around the body as though a chair has simply been vacated rather than a man murdered — the moment that announces, more clearly than anything before it, what kind of council now runs the realm.

Fire & Blood cannot even settle on a single account: Mushroom insists Ser Criston cut his throat exactly as the show depicts; Grand Maester Orwyle's version has him dying of a sudden chill in a black cell days later; Septon Eustace records a fall. The book's own verdict is that the sources agree only on one thing — the honest man at that table died first, however it actually happened.
