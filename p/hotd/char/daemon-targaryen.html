Daemon Targaryen

House TargaryenThe Blacks — the Queen's PartyThe City Watch

The Rogue Prince: rider of Caraxes and bearer of Dark Sister, commander of the City Watch, King of the Narrow Sea for a fortnight, and the most dangerous man in Westeros by broad consent. His brother can neither keep him nor be rid of him.

A soul of House Targaryen, born at King's Landing, sworn to The Blacks — the Queen's Party, The City Watch.

The fuller tale

Season One

In The Heirs of the Dragon — Gold cloaks at his back, a crown in his eye, and a toast he will never live down. At the heir's tourney, Daemon fights like the war the realm hasn't had in a lifetime — while Queen Aemma dies in the birthing bed, and baby Baelon a day after her. Daemon toasts 'the heir for a day' in a pleasure house; Viserys strips him of the succession and sends him from court. In The Rogue Prince — Steals an island, an egg, and every scene.

Daemon takes Dragonstone with his gold cloaks and steals a dragon's egg — the one meant for Baelon's cradle. Spurned by the throne, Corlys brings his fleet to Daemon: a war for the Stepstones, fought without the king's leave. In Second of His Name — Wins a three-year war with one unforgivable, unbeatable lie. Viserys finally sends aid; the letter's arrival shames Daemon into fury — he beats the messenger with his helm.

Daemon rows to the Crabfeeder's line under a peace banner, feigns surrender, and butchers a path until Craghas is cut in half beneath Caraxes. In King of the Narrow Sea — Gives back a crown to keep a grievance. Crowned 'King of the Narrow Sea' by his victorious army, Daemon sails home — and lays the driftwood crown at his brother's feet. Rhaenyra tires of suitors counting her castles; Daemon shows her the city instead — Flea Bottom, a mummer's farce about her succession, a pleasure house.

In We Light the Way — Widowed by his own hand, and refused a Velaryon bride for his trouble. Daemon calls on his wife at Runestone. Lady Rhea Royce falls from her horse; the stone that ends her is in her husband's hand. In The Princess and the Queen — A husband and father in Pentos, almost at peace — until the pyre. In Driftmark — Grieves a wife, frees a 'dead' man, and weds the heir to the throne.

Rhaenyra and Daemon wed on Dragonstone's shore in the old Valyrian way — over a fresh corpse in Laenor's clothes, while Laenor rows for Essos, alive and free. In The Lord of the Tides — Crowns his brother with his own hands; uncrowns Vaemond with Dark Sister. Rhaenyra and Daemon come back to a changed Red Keep — Otto restored as Hand, Alicent ruling a king kept swimming in milk of the poppy. Viserys drags himself to the Iron Throne, half a face and half a body, to hold the succession for his daughter one last time; Daemon picks up the fallen crown.

In The Black Queen — Chokes his queen over a dream he was never told, then brings her the worst news in the world. Ser Erryk Cargyll arrives with a stolen crown; Daemon sets Viserys's crown on Rhaenyra's head on the shore of Dragonstone. The painted table counts its strength: the Vale, the North, the riverlands to win; thirteen dragons to the greens' four — and Daemon wants them flying now.

Where they stand. King's Landing, Dragonstone, The Stepstones, Runestone, Pentos and Driftmark.

Season Two

In A Son for a Son — Buys vengeance in the city he once policed — and doesn't specify enough. Daemon buys two ratcatchers — Blood and Cheese — and sends them into the Red Keep for 'a son for a son.' In The Burning Mill — Wins Harrenhal with a landing, then starts losing to the castle itself. Daemon flies Caraxes to Harrenhal and takes the greatest castle in Westeros without a blow; Ser Simon Strong yields it with supper.

In The Red Dragon and the Gold — Fights the only enemy Caraxes can't burn: Harrenhal, and himself. Daemon holds a ghost-ridden Harrenhal, dosed by Alys Rivers's draughts, dreaming of everything he broke. In Regent — Crowns himself in dreams while the rivers wait for him to wake. Daemon's Harrenhal dreams deepen; the riverlords test him, and Willem Blackwood's brutality against the Brackens does his recruiting.

In the riverlands, Daemon lets the Blackwoods loose on Bracken lands — and learns that terror recruits enemies faster than banners. In The Queen Who Ever Was — Sees the dream at last, and kneels to it — the rogue prince, done running. At Harrenhal, Daemon's visions end in the one that matters — the Song of Ice and Fire — and when Rhaenyra lands, he kneels: her king consort, her army, hers.

Where they stand. King's Landing, Dragonstone and Harrenhal.

How it ends for them

On the page. Fell with Caraxes and Vhagar above the Gods Eye (F&B, ch. 6)

The Rogue Prince's last flight ended the Battle Above the Gods Eye: he leapt from his own dragon to kill Vhagar's rider, and went into the lake with both. His body was never found — which the singers, and one witch of Harrenhal, have made much of since.

The series has not yet flown Daemon to his ending; as of its current seasons he remains alive and at war. Fire & Blood does not spare him that wait.

Above the Gods Eye, in the war's most storied single combat, Daemon on Caraxes meets his nephew Aemond on Vhagar — the collision the whole Dance has been building toward. The dragons lock together in the air; Daemon, in the boldest and most reckless act even he ever attempted, leaps from his own dragon's back onto Vhagar's to reach Aemond directly, driving Dark Sister through the prince's remaining eye as both riders fall. Caraxes and Vhagar go into the lake locked together, and Daemon goes with them. Fishermen and singers alike swear the bodies were never recovered — a silence the book treats less as mystery than as the kind of ending Daemon Targaryen would have chosen for himself, unrecoverable and endlessly argued over.

Their chapters, book by book

Fire & Blood — The Dance of the Dragons 3 chapters that name them

A Question of Succession The Old King's council at Harrenhal passes over Rhaenys for Viserys; a generation later Viserys passes over precedent for Rhaenyra. Aemma dies of the birthing bed, Alicent weds the king, and two courts grow inside one castle — the blacks and the greens, named for the gowns worn at a tourney. Daemon wins the Stepstones, weds Laena, loses her, and weds Rhaenyra over Laenor's convenient corpse — though the book's Laenor is truly murdered, cut down at Spicetown by his own favorite, Qarl Correy. Harrenhal burns its lord and his heir; the king rots slowly on his throne and dies whispering to no one the maesters record. A Son for a Son Blood and Cheese come over the walls of the Red Keep with a purse of Daemon's gold and instructions that fit in five words. Queen Helaena is made to choose among her sons; they take Jaehaerys's head regardless. The greens answer with a mummer's Cargyll: twin meets twin at Dragonstone's gates and both die of it. Daemon takes Harrenhal without a blow; Bracken and Blackwood open the war of the rivers at the Burning Mill; and Ser Criston Cole, Hand and kingmaker, marches the green host into the crownlands. Rhaenyra Overthrown The city that opened its gates learns what a queen's taxes and a queen's fears cost. Helaena Targaryen falls from Maegor's Holdfast onto the spikes below; the smallfolk name Rhaenyra kinslayer and rise behind the Shepherd. The Dragonpit is stormed by ten thousand hands — five dragons die in it, and Prince Joffrey with them, falling from Syrax's back. Rhaenyra flees a burning city as, above the Gods Eye, the uncles end each other: Daemon leaps from Caraxes onto Vhagar's back and puts Dark Sister through Aemond's remaining eye as both dragons fall.

Where the page and the screen part

The show compresses Daemon's decades of pre-Dance restlessness — the City Watch years, three marriages, a driftwood crown — into a tight character study of a man perpetually one grievance from treason, while giving him invented interiority Fire & Blood never bothers with: visions and hauntings at Harrenhal, a fraught, half-poisoned marriage to Rhaenyra played out in real time, a much more overt hand in commissioning Blood and Cheese. The book, narrated at a chronicler's distance and stitched from conflicting testimony, keeps Daemon more opaque and more mythic — a man whose true motives at Harrenhal, in the Stepstones, and in his final flight above the Gods Eye are relayed secondhand, disputed by Mushroom and Eustace alike, and never fully resolved even by Archmaester Gyldayn.
