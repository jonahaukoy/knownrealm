How it ends for them

On the screen. Burned at Harrenhal (S1┬ÀE6)

The Hand of the King asked leave to resign twice, knowing what the court whispered about his son and the princess. He died in his own castle's flames beside that son ÔÇö a fire his other son arranged, and billed to the queen as a kindness.

On the page. Burned at Harrenhal (F&B, ch. 1)

The Hand of the King burned in his bed at Harrenhal beside his heir. Every account agrees on the fire; none agree on the hand that lit it. His clubfooted younger son inherited the castle, the title, and the benefit of the doubt.

The show has Larys arrange the fire that kills both his father and his brother at Harrenhal, framing it as a service performed for the crown's convenience as much as for his own inheritance ÔÇö Lyonel, who had already asked twice to resign the Handship rather than remain entangled in the paternity scandal, dies in the flames before he can complete his own quiet exit from court.

Fire & Blood records the fire and the deaths without confirming the cause: whether Harrenhal's own ancient curse claimed another lord, whether Larys set the blaze deliberately, or whether the fire was simple accident, the maesters' sources disagree, and Gyldayn declines to adjudicate between them ÔÇö noting only that the timing, for Larys, could not have been more convenient.

Their chapters, book by book

Where the page and the screen part

Both tellings put Lyonel and Harwin's deaths in the same fire at Harrenhal, though the show is considerably more explicit about who set it and why ÔÇö attributing the arson directly to Larys, acting to both protect the crown's reputation and clear his own path to his father's title. Fire & Blood is deliberately less certain, preserving multiple competing explanations ÔÇö the castle's old curse, Larys's ambition, or a king quietly cleaning up his daughter's scandal ÔÇö without settling on one.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
