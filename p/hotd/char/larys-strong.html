How it ends for them

On the page. Beheaded in the Hour of the Wolf (F&B, ch. 8)

The Clubfoot outlived every master he served and every fire he set, until the Hour of the Wolf. Cregan Stark offered the Wall; Larys chose the block with a shrug, and House Strong's strange line ended where its castle's curse said it would.

The series has not yet reached the postwar reckoning where Larys's story ends; his fate remains ahead of the current seasons.

Fire & Blood has him outlive nearly every master he ever served and every fire he is suspected of setting, only to be caught out at last in the purge later chroniclers call the Hour of the Wolf — a reckoning conducted by Cregan Stark as the new regency asserts control. Offered the comparatively merciful sentence of exile to the Night's Watch, Larys instead accepts the block with what the book describes as an indifferent shrug, and House Strong's strange, quietly murderous line ends at Harrenhal roughly where its old curse always promised it eventually would.

Their chapters, book by book

Where the page and the screen part

The show, in its aired seasons, has made Larys's manipulations considerably more explicit and visible to the audience than Fire & Blood's chronicle allows — viewers witness his scheming directly, while the book's sources can only infer his hand in events after the fact, from the pattern of who benefits. This is one of the more significant divergences in how much certainty either version grants the audience about a deliberately opaque character.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
