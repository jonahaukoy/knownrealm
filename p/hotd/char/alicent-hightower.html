How it ends for them

On the page. Outlived her war, and died of a winter fever (F&B, ch. 8)

The dowager queen outlived her father, her sons, her war and her enemy, kept to her chambers in a court of ghosts. Fire & Blood gives her a winter fever and last words for no one the maesters could name ÔÇö the green queen, dying in grey.

The series has not yet carried its story to Alicent's final years; her fate on screen remains ahead of the show's current seasons.

Fire & Blood gives her the rarest ending in the entire war: she survives it. Outliving her father, both of her crowned sons, and the political power she spent a lifetime accumulating, Alicent lingers on in a King's Landing full of ghosts, and dies not by blade or dragonfire but of an ordinary winter fever years after the fighting stops ÔÇö the queen who helped light the war getting the one death almost no one else in her family is permitted, quiet, private, and entirely her own.

Where the page and the screen part

The show has, in its aired seasons, spent considerably more time inside Alicent's head than Fire & Blood ever affords her ÔÇö entire sequences of doubt, grief and private reckoning that the book, narrated at a chronicler's remove through hostile or half-informed sources, simply does not grant any of its women. Where Gyldayn's account treats the 'prince that was promised' misunderstanding as a plausible but unconfirmed rumor, weighed against other versions in which Alicent acted with full and cynical intent, the show commits to a specific, sympathetic reading: a woman who genuinely misheard a dying man and then could not walk the mistake back once her father and the realm had already acted on it.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
