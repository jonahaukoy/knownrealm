Corlys Velaryon

House VelaryonThe Blacks — the Queen's Party

The Sea Snake: nine great voyages, a fleet larger than the crown's, and High Tide built from the profits. Lord of Driftmark, wed to the Queen Who Never Was — the richest man in Westeros, and the most stubborn about what his blood is owed.

A soul of House Velaryon, born at Driftmark, sworn to The Blacks — the Queen's Party.

The fuller tale

Season One

In The Heirs of the Dragon — The Sea Snake — richest man in the realm, and married to the queen who should have been. The lords of the realm, Corlys and Rhaenys among them, kneel and swear to Rhaenyra Targaryen, first of her name. In The Rogue Prince — Twice insulted — his daughter refused, his sea ignored. Corlys and Rhaenys offer Viserys their daughter Laena, aged twelve; the king chooses Alicent Hightower instead, and the Sea Snake leaves court cold.

Spurned by the throne, Corlys brings his fleet to Daemon: a war for the Stepstones, fought without the king's leave. In Second of His Name — Pays in Velaryon blood for a war the crown disowned. In Driftmark — Buries a daughter and holds his tongue about his grandsons' hair. Six years on: Corlys takes a wound in the Stepstones that festers; his brother Vaemond sails to court to claim Driftmark over the 'strong boys.'

In The Black Queen — Rises from his sickbed to lay the Velaryon fleet at the black queen's feet.

Where they stand. King's Landing, Driftmark, The Stepstones and Dragonstone.

Season Two

Corlys's blockade closes the Gullet; King's Landing begins to feel the first pinch of hunger. In The Red Dragon and the Gold — Waits at the heart of the blockade for a wife who doesn't come home. In Regent — Made Rhaenyra's Hand — and faced at the docks by his unclaimed blood. On Driftmark, Alyn of Hull — the sailor who pulled the Sea Snake from the waves — is owed more than wages, and Corlys knows it.

In Smallfolk — Watches a bastard son rise on a dragon he never dared acknowledge.

Where they stand. Driftmark and Dragonstone.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Their chapters, book by book

Where the page and the screen part

Corlys is one of the few major figures the show and the book keep in close alignment, though the series gives him a specific and painful wound Fire & Blood only implies — the death of his son Laenor is handled on screen as a fully staged escape Corlys is complicit in covering up, whereas the book leaves that cover story to Rhaenyra and Daemon's convenience and Corlys's own knowledge of it ambiguous. The show also compresses his nine voyages into backstory and dialogue rather than dramatizing them, where the book treats his career as history in its own right, worth pages of its own before the Dance even begins.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
