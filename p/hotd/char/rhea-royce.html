How it ends for them

On the screen. A fall from her horse ÔÇö and a stone (S1┬ÀE5)

The Lady of Runestone rode out hawking and met her husband on the high road ÔÇö years after he'd left her, hours before she died. Her horse threw her; the fall broke her; the stone that finished it was in Daemon's hand. The Vale ruled it a riding accident, in the way of rulings no one believes.

On the page. A fall while hawking, nine days after her husband's visit (F&B, ch. 1)

The Lady of Runestone fell from her horse while hawking and cracked her skull upon a stone, lingering nine days. Her husband, the histories note, paid his only visit to Runestone in years just before ÔÇö and claimed her lands just after. The Vale refused him both.

In the show, Rhea is thrown from her horse while riding in the mountains near Runestone, and the fall is finished, ambiguously but suggestively, by a stone in Daemon's own hand ÔÇö the Vale's official ruling calls it a riding accident, in the specific tone of a ruling nobody involved actually believes.

Fire & Blood records her death as a fall while hawking, her skull cracked on a stone, lingering nine days before she dies. The histories note, pointedly, that her husband paid his first visit to Runestone in years just before the accident and moved to claim her lands just after ÔÇö the Vale's lords refused him both the visit's innocence and the inheritance.

Their chapters, book by book

Where the page and the screen part

Both tellings treat Rhea's death as suspicious without quite committing to calling it murder outright, though the show stages Daemon's involvement with somewhat more visual suggestion than the book's careful hedging allows.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
