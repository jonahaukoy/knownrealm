Steely Pate

Smallfolk & Showfolk

Master armorer of Ashford Meadow: gruff, fair-dealing, and proud of his steel. He arms the tall hedge knight properly — and stands by his work when it matters.

The fuller tale

Master armorer of Ashford Meadow’s tourney grounds, Steely Pate runs his forge with the gruff efficiency of a craftsman who has heard every excuse a poor knight has ever offered for why his gear doesn’t fit, and has stopped being impressed by any of them. What he offers Dunk — proper armor, fitted and functional rather than scavenged and ill-matched — is a small mercy dressed up as a business transaction.

His fairness matters more than his gruffness might initially suggest. In a tourney increasingly defined by whose favor a knight can claim and whose birth commands automatic respect, Pate deals in steel and workmanship alone, standing by the quality of what he makes regardless of who is wearing it — a craftsman’s honesty operating, quietly, as its own small counterweight to the tale’s grander injustices.

The page and the screen

Youssef Kerkour brings Steely Pate to the screen in the show’s opening season, a compact but well-remembered supporting turn in a story largely populated by knights and princes rather than tradesmen — a reminder, consistent with the novella’s own instincts, that Ashford Meadow runs on more than noble ambition alone.
