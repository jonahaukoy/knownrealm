Lady Rohanne Webber

House OsgreyHouse Webber

The Red Widow of Coldmoat: small, freckled, red-braided, and sharper than half the swords in the Reach. Four husbands buried — the whispers say she helped — a castle held in her own right, and a father's will with a deadline in it.

The fuller tale

Small, freckled, and red-braided, Lady Rohanne Webber holds Coldmoat in her own right at a moment when very few women in the Seven Kingdoms hold anything at all — and holds it, by every practical measure the tale offers, better than most of the men complaining about her doing so. Four husbands have died under her roof by the time Dunk’s story crosses hers, and the whispers that she poisoned her way to widowhood say considerably more about what a competent, unmarried woman’s success looks like to a suspicious age than they say about Rohanne herself.

Her competence is not incidental to the plot; it is the plot. She manages Coldmoat’s accounts, levies, and defenses with a shrewdness that would draw open admiration if she were a man and draws whispered accusation because she is not — and hanging over all of it is her late father’s will, which strips her of the castle entirely unless she remarries within a year of his death, a legal trap that makes her competence and her vulnerability inseparable from each other.

The feud that drives Sworn Sword’s plot — a dammed stream, a burned wood, tenants caught between Coldmoat and neighboring Standfast — brings her into direct conflict with old Ser Eustace Osgrey and, through him, with the hedge knight sworn to his service. Rohanne proves herself, across that conflict, a genuinely formidable opponent: sharp in negotiation, ruthless in leverage, and entirely capable of settling a dispute by trial in the very water she claimed rights to.

What resolves the crisis is not conquest but marriage — an ending achieved the old way, wedding the ancient enemy at his own gate rather than continuing to bleed both households over a stream. It is a settlement built on exactly what both sides actually wanted, which turns out to be an end to counting graves rather than any further victory.

Her significance outlasts the tale that introduces her. Through the marriages and generations that follow — genealogies the later histories trace with evident fascination — the Red Widow’s bloodline runs, by the reckoning some chroniclers give, all the way to Casterly Rock itself, red hair and sharp accounting sense alike finding their way, eventually, into the Lannister family the main saga makes so much of.

The page and the screen

Lucy Boynton was cast as Lady Rohanne Webber in March 2026 for the show’s second season, adapting The Sworn Sword — casting that generated considerable excitement given the character’s reputation among readers as one of the novellas’ sharpest, most quietly formidable figures. The season has not yet aired as of this writing, so the performance itself remains to be seen, but the character’s central tension — competence read as suspicion, in a world built to punish women for holding power alone — is exactly the kind of material well suited to a strong central performance.
