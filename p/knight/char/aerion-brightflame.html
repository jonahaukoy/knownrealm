Aerion Brightflame

House Targaryen

Maekar's second son, beautiful as a dream and cruel as a nightmare, who half believes he is a dragon in human skin. Wine, vanity, and viciousness — and a prince's immunity, almost always.

A soul of House Targaryen, born at Summerhall.

The fuller tale

Season One

In Ashford Meadow — Beautiful, cruel, and sure he is a dragon wearing a man. The princes of the realm arrive in force — Baelor, Maekar and his sons: Aerion the Bright Flame, Daeron the Drunken, and somewhere unseen, a missing fourth son. In The Lists — Breaks a girl's fingers over a wooden dragon; meets a real one in a hedge knight. Ser Humfrey Hardyng unhorses Aerion Brightflame — and Aerion's answer is a lance through his horse's neck, leaving Hardyng broken-legged under a dying animal.

That night Aerion finds the puppet show: the wooden dragon offends the Bright Flame, and he breaks Tanselle's fingers for it. In A Trial of Seven — Assembles his seven from Kingsguard white and bought apples. Aerion demands a trial by combat — and names it a trial of seven, the old Andal way: seven against seven, and Dunk must find six men to die for a hedge knight's honor. Raymun Fossoway shames his cousin Steffon into promising help; Steffon sells his promise to Aerion instead by morning — and Raymun is knighted on the spot to take his place.

In The Judgment of the Gods — Recants his accusation with Dunk's fist as his conscience. Aerion drives Dunk to the river's edge — and Dunk, remembering he was a boy of Flea Bottom before he was a knight, drops his sword and beats the yield out of him with his hands. Aerion recants. The trial is won — but across the field Prince Maekar's mace has found his brother Baelor's helm, and the Hand of the King is swaying in the saddle. In A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms — Exiled to Lys, where the wine is stronger and the patience shorter.

Aerion is packed off to Lys, Daeron to a watch he'll drink through. And a hedge knight rides south for Dorne with a prince shining his kettle helm — Ser Duncan the Tall, of no house, of the Seven Kingdoms.

Where they stand. Ashford Meadow and Lys.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Dunk VI Ser Humfrey Hardyng unseats Aerion Brightflame fair and clean — and Aerion answers by driving his lance through the neck of Hardyng's horse, leaving the champion crushed and broken-legged beneath the carcass. Dunk VIII In his cell Dunk learns the price of striking a dragon: a hand and a foot. Aerion demands a trial of seven. Dunk begs the meadow for champions; Steffon Fossoway sells his promise to the prince, and his cousin Raymun is knighted at dawn to stand in his place. Dunk IX The trial of seven, before gods and men: Baelor Breakspear takes up a plain shield to make Dunk's seven. Ser Humfrey Beesbury dies in the press; Aerion yields under Dunk's bare hands — and across the field, Maekar's mace glances off his brother's helm. Dunk X The blow that seemed nothing was everything: Baelor Breakspear dies of a broken skull. Maekar comes to Dunk with a question and an offer, Aerion goes to Lys, and a hedge knight rides for Dorne with a prince polishing his kettle helm.

Where the page and the screen part

Finn Bennett’s take on Aerion drew some of the loudest praise in the show’s first-season reviews — critics called the performance genuinely unsettling rather than cartoonish, crediting Bennett with finding the wounded vanity underneath the character’s casual sadism. The puppet-show sequence, adapted essentially beat for beat from the novella, became one of the season’s most discussed scenes precisely because the show resisted softening it; both tellings agree that Aerion’s cruelty needs to be shown plainly, without excuse, for the trial of seven to carry its full weight.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
