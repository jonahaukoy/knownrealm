Ser Humfrey Beesbury

House Beesbury

A bold young knight of Honeyholt, three-and-twenty, with the black-and-yellow stripes of his house on his shield and no patience for injustice.

The fuller tale

Three-and-twenty and already carrying himself with the confidence of a man who has never seriously doubted his own courage, Ser Humfrey Beesbury of Honeyholt rides at Ashford Meadow beneath his house’s black-and-yellow stripes, one more accomplished young knight among the tourney’s crowded field of hopefuls. He has, by any practical measure, no personal stake whatsoever in a hedge knight’s quarrel with a prince of the blood.

He joins Dunk’s trial of seven anyway, moved by what appears to be nothing more complicated than a conviction that what was done to the puppeteer’s boy, and to Dunk afterward, was plainly wrong — the kind of uncomplicated moral clarity that costs a young knight nothing until the moment it costs him everything. He fights as one of Dunk’s chosen champions with the same easy confidence he brought to the tourney’s earlier rounds.

He fights in the crush of the melee alongside Dunk, Robyn Rhysling, and the rest of the borrowed seven, against three princes of the blood and the white cloaks bound to defend them — a mismatch on paper that the trial’s whole premise insists must be settled by something other than simple numbers. What happens to him once the swords are actually crossed is a stark reminder that the trial’s outcome was never guaranteed, and that the men who stood up for a hedge knight’s honor did so knowing, or at least ought to have known, exactly what they were risking.

The page and the screen

As with several of the tourney’s champions, casting details for Ser Humfrey Beesbury’s role in the show have not been widely publicized, and the character’s function in the first season — a young knight lending his sword to the trial on conviction alone — mirrors the novella’s own brief but pointed treatment of him.

The books: Slain in the trial of seven (THK, ch. 9)

The bold young knight of Honeyholt stood with a hedge knight in the trial of seven at Ashford Meadow, against three princes of the blood and the white cloaks of the Kingsguard. He died in the press of the melee — one of the two men the gods took to prove Dunk's innocence.

In the crush of the trial of seven, Ser Humfrey Beesbury is struck down and killed, one of the melee’s earliest casualties and one of the two deaths — his own and Ser Humfrey Hardyng’s — that the gods take from among Dunk’s champions even as the trial’s ultimate verdict falls in the hedge knight’s favor. His willingness to fight for a stranger’s cause, with nothing to gain from it personally, becomes part of the price the trial’s outcome is measured against.
