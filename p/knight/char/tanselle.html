Tanselle

Smallfolk & Showfolk

Tanselle Too-Tall, a Dornish puppeteer with a quick brush and quicker strings, who paints an elm and a shooting star on a hedge knight's shield. Not too tall by half, in Dunk's considered opinion.

A soul born at Dorne, sworn to Smallfolk & Showfolk.

The fuller tale

Season One

In Ashford Meadow — Tanselle Too-Tall, the Dornish puppeteer — not too tall by half, says Dunk. Dunk pitches his camp beneath an elm and walks the meadow: banners, merchants, puppeteers — and a Dornish girl named Tanselle painting a wooden dragon. Steely Pate the armorer takes Dunk's measure and most of his coin; the shield gets new paint from Tanselle's brush — a shooting star above an elm. In The Lists — Her scream is the hinge the whole tale turns on.

That night Aerion finds the puppet show: the wooden dragon offends the Bright Flame, and he breaks Tanselle's fingers for it. In The Judgment of the Gods — Gone from the meadow before the trial ends — Dunk never finds her to say farewell.

Where they stand. Ashford Meadow.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Their chapters, book by book

The Hedge Knight 3 chapters that name them

Dunk III Ashford Meadow: a hundred pavilions on the banks of the Cockleswhent. Dunk camps beneath a great elm, meets the Dornish puppeteers and the tall girl Tanselle, and watches her wooden dragon die to a wooden hero's lance. Dunk IV Steely Pate takes Dunk's coin for good armor; Tanselle paints his shield — a shooting star above an elm. At the steward's table Dunk learns a hedge knight without a famous name must beg a place in the lists. Dunk VII Night. Tanselle's puppet dragon offends the Bright Flame, and he breaks her fingers for it. Dunk hears the scream, knocks a prince of the blood into the dirt — and a hooded stableboy cries out that he is Aegon Targaryen.

Where the page and the screen part

Tanzyn Crawford plays Tanselle in the show’s first season, and the character’s warm, quietly flirtatious rapport with Dunk survives the adaptation largely intact — the shield-painting scene, small as it is on the page, gives the show one of its gentler character beats amid a season otherwise building toward violence.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
