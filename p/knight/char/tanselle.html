Tanselle

Smallfolk & Showfolk

Tanselle Too-Tall, a Dornish puppeteer with a quick brush and quicker strings, who paints an elm and a shooting star on a hedge knight's shield. Not too tall by half, in Dunk's considered opinion.

The fuller tale

Tanselle Too-Tall arrives in the tale as a Dornish puppeteer with quick hands and quicker opinions, traveling the tourney circuit with a company whose shows mix genuine artistry with a satirist’s willingness to mock whoever’s watching — including, at Ashford Meadow, the royal family itself, in a puppet performance that reads as playful commentary right up until Prince Aerion decides otherwise. It is her fellow puppeteer’s assistant whose fingers Aerion breaks in the fallout, an act of cruelty that pulls Dunk directly into the tourney’s central conflict.

Before any of that, though, Tanselle gives Dunk something that matters just as much to the shape of his story: the elm tree and falling star she paints on his plain shield, transforming an unmarked hedge knight’s gear into something with an actual identity behind it. It is a small act of craft with outsized consequences — the device becomes, over the tales that follow, recognizably his, and hers is the hand that put it there.

The page and the screen

Tanzyn Crawford plays Tanselle in the show’s first season, and the character’s warm, quietly flirtatious rapport with Dunk survives the adaptation largely intact — the shield-painting scene, small as it is on the page, gives the show one of its gentler character beats amid a season otherwise building toward violence.

In the show, season by season

Season One. In Ashford Meadow — Tanselle Too-Tall, the Dornish puppeteer — not too tall by half, says Dunk. In The Lists — Her scream is the hinge the whole tale turns on. In The Judgment of the Gods — Gone from the meadow before the trial ends — Dunk never finds her to say farewell.

In the books

The Hedge Knight. In The Road to Ashford — Tanselle Too-Tall, who paints dragons on shields and works them on strings.

  • Steely Pate takes Dunk's coin for good armor; Tanselle paints his shield — a shooting star above an elm. At the steward's table Dunk learns a hedge knight without a famous name must beg a place in the lists. (THK 4)
