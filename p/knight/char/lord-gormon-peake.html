Lord Gormon Peake

House PeakeThe Black Dragon's Men

Lord of Starpike — and, he would tell you, rightfully of Dunstonbury and Whitegrove too, forfeited when the Peakes backed the black dragon. Sour, scheming, and convinced one more throw of the dice will win his castles back.

The fuller tale

Lord of Starpike by right and, in his own aggrieved accounting, rightful lord of Dunstonbury and Whitegrove as well — castles his house forfeited when its banners flew for the black dragon in the first Blackfyre Rebellion and never got back. Decades on, Gormon Peake has not so much moved past that loss as organized the rest of his life around reversing it, sour and calculating in roughly equal measure, the sort of man who treats every gathering as an opportunity to recruit for a cause he never actually stopped fighting.

At Lord Butterwell’s wedding he finds his opportunity dressed as a celebration — old Blackfyre sympathizers converging under cover of festivities, a dragon’s egg offered as an outlandishly rich tourney prize, and a charming mystery knight whose dreams of crowns give Peake’s scheming something to organize itself around. He is less the rebellion’s figurehead than its engineer, the man doing the patient, unglamorous work of assembling a plot out of grudges, old loyalties, and one lord’s timid ambition.

That lord is Ambrose Butterwell himself, whom Peake manipulates with the practiced ease of a man who has spent a career reading exactly how far a nervous host can be pushed toward treason before his nerve fails him. Whether Peake ever gets his forfeited castles back, and what becomes of him once Bloodraven’s forces close around Whitewalls without the battle he was presumably hoping for, the tale leaves for the reader to wonder about.

The page and the screen

As a figure from The Mystery Knight, Lord Peake has not yet appeared in any aired season of the show, and casting for the Whitewalls conspirators generally remains unannounced. The character’s function — the plot’s patient, embittered architect, working through other men’s ambitions rather than his own visible authority — is exactly the kind of role that tends to be filled by a recognizable character actor once a production reaches it.
