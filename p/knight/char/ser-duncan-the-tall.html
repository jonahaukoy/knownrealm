Ser Duncan the Tall

The Hedge Knights

Dunk: a boy of Flea Bottom who grew nearly seven feet tall, squire to old Ser Arlan of Pennytree since childhood. Slow to anger, honest to a fault, and armed with the old man's sword, shield, and lessons — 'sworn shield of the weak, protector of the innocent,' whether he ever heard the words said over him or not.

A soul born at Flea Bottom, King's Landing, sworn to The Hedge Knights.

Where the page and the screen part

A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms premiered on HBO in January 2026 as a direct, largely faithful adaptation of The Hedge Knight, with Peter Claffey’s Dunk drawing the kind of notices — critics cited his stillness as much as his size — that suggested the production understood the character’s central trick: he is compelling precisely because he underreacts to everything except cruelty. The season kept the trial of seven as its emotional spine, and the show’s decision to let the fight play out at length, rather than compress it into a montage, matched the novella’s own patience with violence and its cost.

Where the season necessarily expands is atmosphere — Flea Bottom, Ashford Meadow’s tent city, the texture of hedge-knight poverty — since a novella’s compression has to become six hours of television somehow. The bones of the story, though, arrived largely untouched: readers of The Hedge Knight found their trial of seven, their puppet show, and their reluctant new squire essentially where they left them, which is more than most adaptations of beloved short fiction manage.
