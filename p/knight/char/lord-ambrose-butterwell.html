Lord Ambrose Butterwell

House ButterwellThe Black Dragon's Men

Lord of the Milkhouse, briefly and badly Hand of the King under Daeron II. Rich as cream, timid as milk — a man who wants the rewards of treason without the risks, and hosts a wedding that serves him neither.

The fuller tale

Lord of the Milkhouse at Whitewalls, Ambrose Butterwell has built both his fortune and his self-image around dairy wealth and the pale, cream-colored walls his family raised to advertise it — a lord whose castle looks, from a distance, considerably grander than his political judgment ever proves to be up close. A brief and undistinguished turn as Hand of the King under Daeron II left him with the taste for influence without the stomach or the skill to hold onto it responsibly.

That combination — ambition without nerve — makes him precisely the sort of host sharper men like Lord Gormon Peake know how to use. His second wedding, staged at his own seat with all the extravagance his dairy fortune can buy, becomes the cover under which a rebellion’s remnants quietly regroup, a dragon’s egg offered as a tourney prize the kind of gesture calculated to attract exactly the old loyalists Peake is hoping to draw out.

Butterwell himself seems to want the rewards treason might bring — restored influence, a return to relevance — while flinching from every actual risk the word implies, a timidity that leaves him vulnerable to being managed by guests with considerably clearer purposes than his own. He hosts the plot more than he leads it, a lord whose castle becomes the stage for a drama he can neither fully control nor fully escape.

When Bloodraven’s forces surround Whitewalls and the conspiracy collapses without a battle, it is Butterwell’s own household that pays the price for his indecision. The crown’s response is architectural as much as judicial: his house attainted, his seat emptied and eventually pulled down stone by stone, so that no gathering place remains for whatever ambition might otherwise have regrouped there again.

The page and the screen

As a figure central to The Mystery Knight’s plot, Lord Butterwell has not appeared in any season of the show aired to date. His character offers an appealing role for adaptation whenever the production reaches it — a host caught out by his own guests’ ambitions, equal parts victim and enabler of the conspiracy unfolding under his roof.
