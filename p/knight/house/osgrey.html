House Osgrey

The chequy lions of Standfast, Marshals of the Northmarch a thousand years ago, lords of dozens of villages and keeps ÔÇö and now masters of one old towerhouse, three fields, and a dry wood. Old Ser Eustace remembers every inch of what his house has lost, including what it lost at Redgrass Field.

The fuller tale

The Osgreys were Marshals of the Northmarch a thousand years before anyone now living could name their current holdings, lords once of dozens of villages and keeps under the chequy lion ÔÇö a house whose motto, The Lion Still Stands, has aged from confident boast into something closer to stubborn insistence, since almost everything the lion once stood over has long since been lost.

The losing was not gradual. Ser Eustace Osgrey rode for the black dragon at Redgrass Field in 196 AC, and whatever the fighting itself cost him, the peace afterward cost him more ÔÇö attainted lands, diminished standing, a house reduced from a marcher power to a single old towerhouse called Standfast, three fields, and a stretch of dry wood that Ser Eustace can, and will, describe acre by acre to anyone who stands still long enough.

That precision is the man in miniature: an old knight who has turned the geography of his own decline into a kind of scripture, reciting what Standfast used to command with the same care other men use to recite their titles. When Dunk and Egg take service under him, they are not joining a great household so much as helping one old soldier keep a very small kingdom from shrinking any further.

The war he actually fights in his final years is proportioned exactly to what remains of him ÔÇö not armies, but a diverted stream, a burned mill, a dispute with his neighbor at Coldmoat that would be beneath any great house's notice and is, to Ser Eustace, the last campaign he has left to lose.

He does not lose it. The feud ends the old way, with a marriage rather than a funeral ÔÇö Ser Eustace to Lady Rohanne Webber herself, decades his junior and considerably his match, a peace neither side particularly expected and both, in the event, seem glad enough to take. The lion, against every reasonable expectation the house's own recent history had set, does still stand.

The page and the screen

This is Sworn Sword material, and while HBO cast Peter Mullan as Ser Eustace Osgrey for a second season announced ahead of the first season's premiere, that season is not expected before 2027 ÔÇö Standfast, the Coldmoat feud, and the marriage that ends it remain, for now, a page-only story.
