Summerhall

Summer palace of House Targaryen

The dragons' pleasure palace in the Dornish Marches, lightly walled and full of music — Prince Maekar's seat, and the home Egg slipped away from. Its sad fire is still fifty years away.

The fuller tale

Summerhall is the dragons’ holiday house — a lightly walled palace in the Dornish Marches built for music and warm evenings rather than defense, the sort of seat a family builds only once it has stopped expecting to need walls at all. In this era it belongs to Prince Maekar Targaryen, third son of Daeron the Good, a hard and joyless man who nonetheless keeps his household in a palace built for gaiety — and it is from Summerhall’s stables and side doors that his fourth son, a bald boy with no patience for princely tedium, slips away disguised as nobody in particular to squire for a hedge knight he has only just met.

That runaway prince is, of course, Egg, and Summerhall’s significance to the tale is entirely retrospective: a comfortable palace that a future king once found so stifling he ran from it in a servant’s castoffs. The castle’s own famous catastrophe — the fire that will one day gut it on the night a grandson is born beneath a bad omen — is still decades off in this telling, a shadow the story is careful not to let readers see falling yet, though it is already there to be noticed if one knows to look.
