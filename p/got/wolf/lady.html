Lady

Sansa's wolf

The best-behaved of the litter, killed for something another wolf did.

The wolf itself

Lady is grey and silver, lighter in the coat than her brothers, with a paler ruff at the throat and yellow eyes that are somehow the mildest yellow eyes in the litter. She is the smallest of the six after the runt, and the prettiest, and she carries herself the way well-bred dogs in a warm hall carry themselves: head up, tail level, unhurried, perfectly aware that she is being looked at. Even half-grown she was already too big for the rooms she was kept in, and she managed it gracefully, folding herself down beside chairs and under tables and never once knocking anything over.

Her temperament is her girlÔÇÖs, and both have been unfairly judged for it. Lady is courteous. She was the easiest of the six to train and the only one who never had to be corrected twice; she came when called, walked to heel without a lead, took food without snatching and did not bark. She never bit anyone in her life. Her voice is a soft whine at a closed door rather than a howl, and her instinct when a room turns tense is to press herself against her girlÔÇÖs leg rather than to move forward. That is not weakness ÔÇö she is still a direwolf, and a hundred and fifty pounds of one ÔÇö but she was raised to be pleasant among people, by a child who wanted very badly to be pleasant among people, and she succeeded at it completely.

The bond

Sansa named her Lady, which everyone remembers, usually while smiling at how very Sansa it is. It is also, quietly, the only name in the litter that describes an ambition rather than the animal ÔÇö the others named what they saw, and she named what she hoped to be.

The bond between them is the least examined of the six, and that is not an accident of the storytelling. Sansa is the Stark child who never learns what the connection is for. Where the others slip into their wolves in sleep and come back knowing things, she has almost no time at all with hers before the two are separated, and what she is left with afterwards is an absence rather than a skill. There are moments ÔÇö a dream that seems to belong to a different set of senses, a moment of certainty about someone that arrives from nowhere ÔÇö and then nothing, for years.

It is the cruellest of the six arrangements. Every other Stark child gets an animal that carries them through the worst part of their story. She gets manners, a court, and no instinct she can trust.

Lady was chained at the time, some distance away, and had done nothing whatsoever. That is the entire point of her.
