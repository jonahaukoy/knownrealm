Where the page and the screen part

The show’s Illyrio exists for a handful of first-season scenes — the marriage broker, the manse, the eggs — plus one overheard conspiracy with Varys in the Red Keep’s cellars, and then vanishes from the series entirely. The books keep him central long after: his partnership with Varys is given a full history, his manse hosts Tyrion’s flight east, and he stands revealed as co-architect of the Young Griff plot — the hidden claimant, the Golden Company, and the invasion of Westeros that the novels stage and the show replaced with Daenerys’s own. The adaptation, having cut Aegon, quietly reassigned the conspiracy’s fruits: the Golden Company sails for Cersei instead, and Varys’s restorationist scheming is simplified into service to Daenerys. Book readers also carry the Serra question — the dead wife, the blue-haired boy, and the possibility that Illyrio’s grand design is partly a father’s — which the show never needed and never mentions. He is alive in both tellings, rich beyond counting, and safely offstage.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
