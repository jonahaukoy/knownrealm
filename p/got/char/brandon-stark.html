The fuller tale

How it ends for them

Died before the story — before this story begins.

Brandon's ride to King's Landing ended in a treason charge — 'come out and die' being poor legal strategy under Aerys II — and his father's summons south ended in the throne room. The Mad King's idea of a trial gave Lord Rickard to the fire and gave Brandon a strangler's cord and a sword placed just beyond his fingertips. He died reaching for it. The books put the account in Jaime Lannister's mouth, a Kingsguard made to stand and watch; the show leaves the scene as a line of Ned's, which is roughly how Ned would have wanted it.

Their chapters, book by book

Where the page and the screen part

The show grants Brandon perhaps two sentences across eight seasons; viewers could be forgiven for forgetting Ned had a brother between Lyanna and Benjen. The books make him a small but load-bearing ghost: Catelyn's chapters measure Ned against him, Barbrey Dustin's grudge against the Starks begins in his bed, and the rumors around Ashara Dayne at Harrenhal attach to him in some tellings rather than to Ned. He is the books' first demonstration of a recurring thesis — that the dashing elder brother dies and the dutiful second son inherits the story.
