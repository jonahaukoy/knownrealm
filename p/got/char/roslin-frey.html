Roslin Frey

House TullyHouse Frey

One of Lord Walder's many daughters, small, pretty, and gentle-natured, given in marriage to Lord Edmure Tully.

The fuller tale

Out of Lord Walder’s crowded nursery of daughters and granddaughters, Roslin is the one the old man keeps in reserve — small, pretty, gentle-mannered, everything the Frey name is not — and when the moment comes he deploys her precisely. Robb Stark arrives at the Twins braced to see his uncle chained to some pinch-faced stranger as punishment for the broken pact; instead Edmure Tully is presented with a lovely, soft-spoken bride, and the Stark party relaxes. That is what Roslin is for. Her father spent her beauty the way he spends everything: as bait.

The books make her role quietly heartbreaking, because Roslin knows. She weeps through her own wedding — steadily, helplessly, in a way the guests read as maiden’s nerves — because she alone at the altar knows what the music will turn into, and can warn no one. The marriage that begins that night is nonetheless, by every account, a real one: she and Edmure, two gentle people used as instruments by monsters, find genuine affection in the wreckage, and she carries his child while her husband sits in a Frey cell — a Tully heir conceived at the Red Wedding, which is the saga’s way with hope.

She survives both tellings, a footnote to an atrocity she never chose, and ends the show’s story reunited with a freed Edmure at Riverrun — the one Frey the story permits a quiet ending, chiefly because she was as much a hostage of her father’s scheme as any Stark at the feast.

The page and the screen

The show keeps her wedding scenes nearly beat-for-beat but drops her knowledge of the plot — screen-Roslin is simply a sweet girl at the center of a horror, while book-Roslin’s tears are the tell no one reads in time. The books also keep her on the page after the massacre: pregnant at the Twins, praying for a daughter, by her own account, because a son would make her too useful to her family. It is a single line, and it carries the whole weight of being born a Frey.
