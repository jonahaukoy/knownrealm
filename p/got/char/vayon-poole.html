The fuller tale

How it ends for them

On the screen. Killed in the purge of the Stark household (S1·E9)

Winterfell's steward followed Lord Stark to King's Landing and died with the rest of the household when the Lannisters struck, cut down in the purge that spared almost no one wearing grey.

On the page. Killed with the Stark men in King's Landing (AGOT, ch. 49)

Winterfell's steward died with the rest of the household when the gold cloaks and Lannister men turned on Lord Eddard — one name in the purge that left his daughter Jeyne an orphan in Littlefinger's keeping.

When Ned Stark moved against Joffrey's succession and Littlefinger's gold cloaks turned, the Lannister answer was total: every man, woman and servant of the Hand's household was put to the sword in the purge, and Winterfell's steward died with the rest, cut down in the tower he kept. The books linger on the completeness of it — guardsmen, septa, stable hands, all of it done in an hour — and on the single omission that matters: his daughter Jeyne, taken alive into Littlefinger's keeping, which readers come to understand was not mercy. Vayon Poole's death has no scene of its own in either telling; he is one name in the bill for his lord's honor, which is precisely the kind of accounting he would have kept perfectly.

Their chapters, book by book

Where the page and the screen part

Both tellings dispose of Vayon identically and differ only in what his death sets up. In the show he is a face in the purge and little more. In the books his death detonates slowly through his daughter: orphaned Jeyne is handed to Littlefinger, and the steward's girl resurfaces volumes later at the center of the Boltons' great fraud — a plotline the show reassigned wholesale to Sansa Stark. Vayon himself is thus that rare figure whose adaptation footprint is measured almost entirely in consequences he never lives to see.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
