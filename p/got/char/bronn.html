Bronn

Sellswords & Free Companies

A sellsword of quick wit and quicker blade, loyal for exactly as long as the coin holds out — and surprisingly good company besides.

A soul born at The Riverlands, sworn to Sellswords & Free Companies.

The fuller tale

Season One

A sellsword with no house and no scruples worth mentioning, hired by Tyrion Lannister as a bodyguard on the road and immediately worth every coin of it. When Catelyn Stark hauls Tyrion before Lysa Arryn's court at the Eyrie and demands trial by combat, Bronn steps up as his champion against one of Lysa's household knights and wins by the simple expedient of not fighting fair, buying Tyrion's freedom and Bronn himself a permanent place in his service.

Season Two

Commands the wildfire ambush that breaks Stannis's fleet in the bay during the siege of King's Landing, a piece of tactical nastiness that turns the battle before it's properly begun. For services rendered he's knighted afterward, Ser Bronn of the Blackwater, a title he wears with visible amusement at how far a good sellsword can rise.

Season Three

Still Tyrion's man in a court that trusts neither of them, drinking, sparring with Podrick, and generally getting more use out of King's Landing's underworld than the Lannisters get out of their name. When Tyrion, on trial for a murder he didn't commit, asks him to fight as his champion again, Bronn — offered a match to a wealthy heiress by Cersei instead — chooses the marriage and the money over the sword, with no pretense of guilt about it.

Season Four

The marriage to Lollys keeps him comfortably out of the one fight Tyrion actually needs him for. He visits the cell before the trial to explain himself man to man, no pretense of guilt in it — a sellsword takes the safer money, that's the whole trade, and Tyrion is furious in a way that doesn't quite manage to be surprise. There is no version of that conversation that ends with Bronn on the sand risking his neck for a friendship, however real, that never once came with a salary attached.

Season Five

Reassigned to Jaime Lannister for a quiet mission to Dorne to retrieve Myrcella, which turns considerably less quiet once the Sand Snakes of House Martell get involved — Bronn fights one of them to a standstill and comes away with a poisoned scratch that spends several uncomfortable days deciding whether to kill him.

Season Six

Rides with Jaime to the siege of Riverrun, trading insults with the Blackfish's men from outside the walls while Jaime tries diplomacy first and threats second, comfortable as ever playing the practical half of whatever partnership he's currently in. When word arrives that the Freys have taken the castle by treachery instead, Bronn's just as happy to collect his soldier's pay for a siege nobody in Lannister colors actually had to fight.

Season Seven

Fights for the Lannister column at the Loot Train Battle, nearly burned alive when Daenerys's dragon comes in low over the wagons, and manages — barely — to wrestle one of Qyburn's giant crossbows into position and put a bolt into Drogon before the beast wheels off. It is the closest anyone has come all war to hurting a dragon.

Season Eight

Sent by Cersei with orders to kill Tyrion and Jaime both, a job he never quite gets around to finishing, and after the war is won and the throne changes hands for good, he trades his sword for a seat at the table — walking into the new king's court to claim the lordship and the council post he decided, sensibly, he was always owed.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Where the page and the screen part

The books send Bronn up a different ladder. After declining Tyrion's second trial he marries Lollys Stokeworth — the dim, ruined daughter of a minor crownlands house — and sets about acquiring the seat itself with unnerving efficiency: the sister ahead of his wife in the succession is soon widowed, castellans die conveniently, and by the fifth book he is Lord Stokeworth in all but patience, having named his newborn stepson Tyrion in a gesture Cersei reads, correctly, as a taunt aimed at the crown. Her attempt to have him quietly removed fails when the knight sent to do it dies of Bronn instead. The show's Dorne trip, loot train heroics, crossbow contract and Highgarden are all its own inventions — book Bronn has never met Jaime — but both tellings agree on trajectory: the sellsword is climbing, and no one has yet found the price of stopping him. In the published books he is alive and thriving.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
