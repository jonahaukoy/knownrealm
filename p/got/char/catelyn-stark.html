Catelyn Stark

House StarkHouse Tully

Born a Tully of Riverrun, Lady of Winterfell by marriage: fierce in defense of her children and never quite at peace with the bastard raised beneath her roof.

A soul of House Stark, born at Riverrun.

The fuller tale

Season One

In Winter Is Coming — Begs Ned to refuse — the letter changes everything. In The Kingsroad — Finds a strand of blond hair in the broken tower. A catspaw comes for the comatose Bran with Valyrian steel — Catelyn and the direwolf Summer stop him. Catelyn resolves to ride south in secret with the assassin's dagger.

In Lord Snow — Leaves with a name: Tyrion Lannister. Catelyn arrives in secret; Littlefinger tells her the assassin's dagger belonged to Tyrion Lannister. In Cripples, Bastards and Broken Things — "In the name of Catelyn Stark — seize him." At the Crossroads Inn, Catelyn calls on her father's bannermen and arrests Tyrion Lannister.

In The Wolf and the Lion — Learns the Eyrie's justice is her sister's madness. Catelyn climbs to the Eyrie and finds her sister half-mad, her nephew unweaned, her prisoner unwelcome. In A Golden Crown — Watches her sister's justice fail her family. In The Pointy End — Rides for her son's war camp at the Neck.

In Baelor — Walks alone into Walder Frey's hall and wins the war a bridge. Catelyn buys the Twins' crossing from Walder Frey with marriage promises — one of them Robb's. In Fire and Blood — "We will kill them all." First: get the girls back.

Where they stand. Winterfell, King's Landing, The Eyrie, Moat Cailin, The Twins and Riverrun.

Season Two

In What Is Dead May Never Die — Watches a summer king play at war in painted armor. Catelyn treats with Renly's hundred thousand at his tourney camp — and meets Brienne of Tarth. Brienne, blamed and hunted, swears her sword to Catelyn Stark. In The Prince of Winterfell — Traded the Kingslayer for a hope of her daughters.

Where they stand. Riverrun, Storm's End and The Crag.

Season Three

In The Rains of Castamere — One hostage, one plea, one scream — then silence. Talisa, Robb, Catelyn, Grey Wind — the King in the North falls with his kingdom.

Where they stand. Harrenhal, Riverrun and The Twins.

How it ends for them

On the screen. Slain at the Red Wedding (S3·E9)

She watched her firstborn murdered under a roof that had sworn them safety, with the Frey wife she'd bargained for standing witness. She opened Lady Frey's throat as her last act, and Black Walder opened hers.

On the page. Killed at the Red Wedding (ASOS, ch. 52)

She watched her son die at his uncle's wedding feast and opened old Jinglebell's throat for a vengeance Walder Frey only laughed at. They cut her throat and threw her in the Green Fork naked — and what the river gave back three days later does not forgive.

She died at the Red Wedding, under a roof whose bread and salt she had eaten, watching everything she had bargained for turn to slaughter. When the Rains of Castamere began and the quarrels flew, Catelyn's last play was pure Tully: she seized a hostage — Walder Frey's own kin — and offered a trade, her son's life for his. Frey let her cut the man's throat for nothing. She had watched Robb die by then; the Freys opened her throat a moment later. The show gives the hostage as Frey's young wife; the books give her the lackwit Jinglebell, and add that the Freys threw her body naked into the Green Fork as a mockery of Tully funeral rites.

And in the books, the river gives her back. Three days dead, fished from the water, she is granted the kiss of life by Beric Dondarrion — who passes his last resurrection to her and stays dead to pay for it. What rises is Lady Stoneheart: throat-scarred, half-voiced, unforgiving, hanging Freys and fair-weather friends from riverland trees at the head of the Brotherhood without Banners. The show cut her entirely — the single most debated omission of the whole adaptation. Readers are still waiting to learn what the mother's ghost does to the sons' war; viewers were never told she existed.

Catelyn I In the godswood Catelyn brings Ned hard news: Jon Arryn is dead of a sudden fever, and King Robert rides north with his court. Catelyn II Lysa's secret letter arrives accusing the Lannisters of murdering Jon Arryn; Ned resolves to go south, and Jon will go to the Wall. Catelyn III Fire in the library tower — and a catspaw with a Valyrian steel dagger at Bran's throat; Catelyn's bare hands and Summer's teeth end him. Catelyn IV Catelyn reaches King's Landing in secret and is fetched to Littlefinger, who names the dagger's owner: Tyrion Lannister — a lie with a long tail. Catelyn V At the crossroads inn Tyrion Lannister walks in on Catelyn Stark; she calls on her father's bannermen and takes him — but not toward Winterfell. Catelyn VI Up the mountain road to the Eyrie, through the Bloody Gate — where Lysa's welcome is madness, and Tyrion is given to the sky cells. Catelyn VII The Eyrie's champion Ser Vardis Egen fights Bronn for the gods' verdict — and follows his shield through the Moon Door. Catelyn VIII Catelyn meets Robb's host at Moat Cailin and does not send him home to Winterfell — she teaches him how to spend his one throw of the dice. Catelyn IX At the Twins Catelyn crosses Walder Frey's bridge with a bargain: the crossing for marriage promises — one of them Robb's. Catelyn X In the Whispering Wood Robb springs his trap on Jaime Lannister; the Kingslayer is taken, and the price is paid in Karstark sons. Catelyn XI In Riverrun's great hall the lords debate distant kings until the Greatjon has had enough: “The King in the North!” — and every sword comes out.

A Clash of Kings 7 chapters of their own

Catelyn I At Riverrun the war council divides; Robb sends his mother — over her protests — as envoy to the newly crowned Renly. Catelyn II At Bitterbridge Renly plays at war amid tourneys and peaches; a woman in blue armor wins the melee and asks only to guard her king — Brienne of Tarth. Catelyn III Under the walls of Storm's End the brother kings meet to trade insults; Stannis offers Renly a night to reconsider, and a peach outlives the peace. Catelyn IV In Renly's tent at dawn a shadow with Stannis's face slides through the king's gorget; Brienne is blamed, and Catelyn flees with her. Catelyn V News reaches Riverrun that Winterfell is taken; Catelyn, ordered to keep from the war, keeps instead a vigil over her father and her fears. Catelyn VI Edmure musters every sword to hold the fords of the Trident against Tywin's host — a victory, Catelyn fears, that wins the wrong battle. Catelyn VII Alone with the Kingslayer and a flagon of wine, Catelyn takes Jaime's confessions one by one — then calls for Brienne's sword.

A Storm of Swords 7 chapters of their own, and 1 more that name them

Catelyn I Catelyn waits at Riverrun for judgment for freeing the Kingslayer, sitting at her father's deathbed as he calls a name no one knows: Tansy. Catelyn II Robb returns from the west healed of an arrow and wed to Jeyne Westerling — a bandaged heart that has cost him the Freys and perhaps the war. Catelyn III Rickard Karstark butchers two captive squires in their cell for vengeance; Robb answers as a king must and takes his head — and half his host rides home. Catelyn IV Robb plans his way back north through the Twins: a pardon for the Freys, Edmure for a bride, and a wedding to mend what a wedding broke. Catelyn V Lord Hoster Tully is given to the river that raised him; Edmure's arrows shame him thrice before the Blackfish takes the bow and lights the boat with one. Catelyn VI The northern host crosses under the Twins' gates through grey rain; bread and salt are taken, and Grey Wind will not stop howling at the Freys. Catelyn VII The Red Wedding: the music turns to crossbows under Walder Frey's roof; Robb dies with his mother's screams in his ears, and Catelyn's throat is opened last. Epilogue · Merrett Frey Merrett Frey rides to ransom a captive from outlaws and finds a hooded judge instead: dead men hang for the Red Wedding, and the woman under the hood was once Catelyn Stark.

Where the page and the screen part

Catelyn is one of the books' central point-of-view voices — the entire northern war arrives through her eyes, and her interior monologue of grief, calculation and guilt is among the saga's best sustained writing. The show necessarily flattens that into Michelle Fairley's performance, which critics rightly praised, but it also hands her material the books never wrote: the invented confession about praying for baby Jon's life is the show's, and her marriage's arithmetic shifts once Talisa of Volantis replaces the books' Jeyne Westerling as the queen her son should not have married.

The structural difference is where each telling leaves her weight. In the books she is the reader's window on Robb — he has no chapters of his own — so his kingship, his mistakes and his charm all reach us filtered through a mother's fear. Show viewers meet Robb directly and lose that doubled vision, which quietly makes show-Catelyn seem more peripheral to a war the books insist she co-authored.
