Asha Greyjoy

Balon's daughter and true heir in all but law: a ship's captain with an axe in her belt and a crew that would follow her into the Drowned God's halls. The show renamed her Yara.

A soul of House Greyjoy, born at Pyke.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Their chapters, book by book

A Feast for Crows 1 chapter of their own, and 2 more that name them

Asha I At Ten Towers Asha musters her uncles and captains for the kingsmoot: a woman's claim to the Seastone Chair, argued with wit, salt, and an axe. Victarion I The Iron Captain comes to Old Wyk among longships beyond counting; the captains weigh Asha's wit against Victarion's iron — until a sail with a red eye appears. Aeron II The kingsmoot on Nagga's hill: Asha offers peace, Victarion strength — and Euron Crow's Eye offers dragons, with a hellhorn from the smoking ruins of Valyria. The ironmen choose madness.

A Dance with Dragons 3 chapters of their own

Asha I At Deepwood Motte the kraken's daughter reads letters from a broken home — then Stannis's host comes out of the trees, and Asha Greyjoy is the king's prize. Asha II The King's Prize: Stannis's march on Winterfell drowns in snow three days out of Deepwood; horses die, men freeze, and the northern clans mutter of the Night's King. Asha III At a crofters' village three days from Winterfell, Stannis's starving host burns its cannibals; out of the storm come a broken man and a girl in a dead girl's name — Theon and Jeyne.

Where the page and the screen part

Asha’s book road and Yara’s show road part ways almost completely after the kingsmoot. The books send her not to Daenerys but into Stannis Baratheon’s hands at Deepwood Motte, then on the death-march toward Winterfell as prisoner and point-of-view — snowbound chapters the show never adapted, including her forced proxy marriage and her reunion with the ruin of Theon in the enemy camp. The show’s counterpart storylines — the Targaryen pact, Euron’s prow, the brother’s rescue — have no book equivalent at all. She is also, in the books, half a Harlaw: her mother’s kin at Ten Towers, above all her uncle Rodrik the Reader and his famous library, give her a hinterland of allies and irony the screen never had time to visit.
