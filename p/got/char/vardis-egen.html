Vardis Egen

The captain of House Arryn's household guard, a seasoned knight sworn to Lady Lysa and the little lord of the Eyrie.

The fuller tale

Ser Vardis Egen is the Vale in miniature: correct, seasoned, loyal to the letter, and fatally sure that the old forms will hold. Captain of House Arryn's household guard, he served Lord Jon Arryn honorably for years and passed with the household into the keeping of the widowed Lady Lysa — which means, in practice, that a decent knight's last commission is enforcing the whims of the Eyrie's least stable court. The story gives him no vices and no illusions of grandeur; he is the professional soldier as furniture of the realm, the man who keeps the gates and asks no questions a vassal shouldn't, right up until the day the questions arrive armed.

That day comes with Tyrion Lannister's demand for trial by combat. When the Imp, imprisoned in the sky cells on Lysa's charges, invokes the old right and the sellsword Bronn steps forward to fight for him, Lysa names Ser Vardis her champion — over his own reluctance, the books note, for Vardis grasps immediately what his lady does not: that there is no honor to be won against a hired blade in a fight the gods are supposedly judging. He arms in full plate, takes up shield and longsword beneath the Moon Door, and fights exactly as he was trained to — advancing, squaring, offering the formal battle his whole tradition promises will prevail. His opponent declines to attend that battle, and the Eyrie's court learns, at the price of its champion, the lesson the entire saga keeps invoicing: the forms are not the fight.

The page and the screen

The duel is one of the saga's cleanest page-to-screen translations — armored propriety worn down by unarmored patience — though the settings differ instructively: the show stages it on an open terrace with the sky for a fourth wall, while the books fight it in the High Hall around a mocking statue of the weeping Alyssa Arryn, whose marble Bronn uses for cover in a detail adaptation traded for cliff-edge spectacle. Book Vardis is given slightly more interiority — his visible reluctance to fight, his awareness that Lysa's pride has purchased his risk — and slightly more history, as a remembered fixture of Jon Arryn's King's Landing household. His function is identical in both: he is the honest retainer spent by a foolish liege, and the first demonstration in the story that Bronn's arithmetic beats chivalry's poetry every time the two are left alone together.

The show: Fell in a trial by combat (S1·E6)

The books: Fell in the Eyrie's trial by combat (AGOT, ch. 40)

Lysa Arryn's household knight was named her champion in Tyrion's trial by combat at the Eyrie. Bronn wore him down in his heavy plate, opened his throat, and sent him out the Moon Door.

He dies at the Moon Door, in the duel that frees Tyrion Lannister. Both tellings run the same cruel experiment: Ser Vardis in full plate against Bronn in boiled leather, formal skill against professional survival. Bronn gives ground, circles, uses statue and stair and open floor, and lets sixty pounds of armor fight the fight for him; Vardis chases, slows, staggers — and when the weight has finally hollowed him out, the sellsword closes and opens his throat. In the show his body is then tipped through the Moon Door itself, six hundred feet of the Vale's own justice swallowing the Vale's own champion, while Lysa protests that the fight lacked honor and Bronn supplies the duel's epitaph: the dead man had it. The books leave the corpse on the High Hall's floor by the weeping statue, which amounts to the same verdict delivered indoors. Either way, the gods' judgment goes to the man who never asked for it, and the Eyrie's captain becomes the price of his lady's tantrum.
