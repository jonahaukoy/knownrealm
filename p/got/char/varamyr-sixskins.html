How it ends for them

(book only) — before this story begins.

On the page. Died in the snows south of the Wall (ADWD, ch. 1)

The great skinchanger of the free folk, who wore a shadowcat and a bear and men's fears, died a beggar in the snows after the Wall broke Mance's host. His second life in his wolf One Eye opens the fifth book — and shows what waits inside the storm.

His death opens the fifth book, and it is a masterwork of poetic justice conducted in a snowstorm. Broken by the eagle’s burning and the host’s shattering, Varamyr staggers south among the wildling refugees, robbed, wounded and dying in a nameless village — and at the end, in terror of the true death, he commits the abomination one last time: he hurls his spirit at the spearwife Thistle, the one human being who had shown him kindness, to take her body for his own. She fights him from inside — screaming, biting off her tongue, clawing at her own eyes to force him out — and both of them die in the struggle, her ruined body rising minutes later with blue eyes among the village’s new wights. Varamyr’s spirit escapes into his wolf One Eye for his second life: a fading passenger in a beast in a world filling with the dead, the great skinchanger reduced to exactly what his teacher warned him all men become who love the skin too much. The show, having no Varamyr, never owed him an ending; the books gave him a perfect one.

Where the page and the screen part

Varamyr never appears in the show, but his estate is everywhere in it. His warg lore — the second life, the beast that carries a dead man’s grudge — was handed to Orell, whose expanded screen role absorbs both Varamyr’s teaching function and his stolen eagle; his cautionary shadow over Bran’s gift survives in the Three-Eyed Raven’s warnings; and his prologue’s vision of wights rising among the wildling dead was folded into Hardhome. The books also give him a small, crucial on-page role before his prologue: at the battle beneath the Wall it is Varamyr’s beasts and Orell’s inherited eagle that serve Mance as the host’s reconnaissance, and when Melisandre’s fires burn the eagle out of the sky with Varamyr riding it, the agony breaks him — the beginning of the ruin his prologue completes. A book-only figure, and the richest single deposit of skinchanger doctrine in the saga.
