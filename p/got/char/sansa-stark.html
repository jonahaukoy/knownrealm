Sansa Stark

House Stark

The elder Stark daughter, courteous and dreaming of songs and southern knights, betrothed young to a prince. Her direwolf was named Lady.

A soul of House Stark, born at Winterfell.

The fuller tale

Season One

The realm's best student of a curriculum that turns out to be a lie — songs, courtesy, tourneys, and a golden prince. Her betrothal to Joffrey delivers her into the truth at the worst possible address, by way of the Trident, a butcher's boy, and her own direwolf killed in her father's place. She begs for Ned's life and is given his execution instead, watched from a platform in a crowd.

Season Two

A hostage in silk with the manners of a hostage. She is beaten in open court on the king's word and saved from worse by the Hound; she learns from Cersei that tears are a woman's weapon and there is a better one between her legs; and she learns, on her own, that a perfect curtsy is armour.

Season Three

Married to Tyrion Lannister on Tywin's order, to keep the North's claim inside Lannister hands. It is the second time in a year that she is handed to a family as an instrument, and she has by now stopped expecting anybody to ask.

Season Four

Out of the city in the confusion of a poisoning, into Littlefinger's boat and then the Vale — and there, at Lysa Arryn's inquest, she tells the first lie she has ever told for her own reasons, and it works. It is the moment the apprenticeship turns into a trade.

Season Five

Married to Ramsay Bolton in her own home, in her mother's sept, and everything the North was supposed to mean to her is used against her in her own bedchamber. She gets out over the wall of Winterfell in the snow with Theon Greyjoy, who is the last person on earth she would have chosen and the only one there.

Season Six

She finds Jon at Castle Black and pushes him into a war he does not think he can win — then raises the Vale's knights herself, tells nobody, and brings them onto the field at the exact hour. Afterwards she leaves Ramsay in his own kennels with his own dogs and does not raise her voice once.

Season Seven

Rules Winterfell while her brother is south, competently and to some resentment, while Littlefinger spends the autumn whispering that her sister is a threat. She ends it in the great hall by turning the trial around on the man who taught her how trials work.

Season Eight

Holds the North through the Long Night and the peace after it, and refuses at the last council to hand her country back to a king in the south. She is crowned Queen in the North in the castle she survived — the girl who wanted to marry a king, corrected by experience into the person the kings should have been.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

A Storm of Swords 7 chapters of their own, and 3 more that name them

Sansa I The Queen of Thorns asks Sansa plainly what Joffrey is; the truth — “a monster” — buys her a Highgarden dream: gentle Willas, and roses instead of lions. Sansa III Cloaked in Lannister crimson before she understands the joke, Sansa is wed to Tyrion; he spares her the bedding, and they share a marriage of two prisoners. Sansa IV Courtesies before the storm: the Tyrells fuss over Sansa's hair and hopes while a hairnet of dark amethysts waits, one stone short of innocent. Sansa V While the court screams, Ser Dontos spirits Sansa to a ship in the fog — where Littlefinger pays the fool his promised reward in crossbow bolts. Where the page and the screen part

The deepest divergence in all the Stark children is here: book Sansa has never set foot in Winterfell since the first volume. Her published-books story ends in the Vale, still Alayne Stone, managing Littlefinger's game among the lords declarant and a sickly little lord cousin — the entire Ramsay marriage belongs to the show, which reassigned the books' 'false Arya' plot (poor Jeyne Poole's) to Sansa to keep a beloved actress central. The change remains the adaptation's most argued-over decision: it bought the Battle of the Bastards its emotional engine at the cost of subjecting Sansa to horrors her book counterpart has so far been spared.

The books also age her down — eleven when the story begins — and give her one of the saga's great unreliable-narrator threads: the memory of the Hound's kiss that never happened, growing more real each time she recalls it. Small inventions ripple outward too: her snow-castle scene in the Eyrie, rebuilding Winterfell in miniature, is book-only and beloved; her dyed hair and false name make espionage of chapters the show compressed into a single season of black dresses. Two versions, one direction of travel — but readers genuinely do not know yet whether the books will walk her the show's road home.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
