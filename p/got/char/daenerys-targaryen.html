Daenerys Targaryen

House Targaryen

The last Targaryen princess, born in exile and sold in marriage by her brother to a Dothraki khal ÔÇö a frightened girl who begins to discover she is anything but.

A soul of House Targaryen, born at Dragonstone.

The fuller tale

Season One

Sold to a Dothraki khal by her brother for the promise of an army, and everything that follows is her refusing, one step at a time, to be a thing that is traded. She learns command in Drogo's khalasar the hard way and finds she is good at it. She watches Viserys get the crown he asked for, poured molten over his head. Then a wound goes bad, a maegi's bargain takes her husband and her son together, and she walks into Drogo's funeral pyre with three petrified eggs and walks out of the ashes unburnt with the first dragons in a century and a half.

Season Two

The red waste nearly finishes what the pyre started. Qarth takes her in and then robs her: her dragons stolen, her people murdered in her rooms, and a long unpleasant lesson in the House of the Undying about what a queen with three lizards and forty followers is actually worth. She comes out of it with a stolen ship and a much shorter list of people she trusts.

Season Three

Astapor states her thesis out loud. She offers a dragon for eight thousand Unsullied, takes the city instead the moment the chain changes hands, and then does the thing nobody expected ÔÇö tells the slave soldiers they are free and asks them to choose whether to follow her. They do. Yunkai falls next, and she is carried out of it on the shoulders of people who have no other word for her than mother.

Season Four

Meereen, and the discovery that breaking chains is a morning's work and building what comes after is a life's. She crucifies a hundred and sixty-three masters for a hundred and sixty-three children on the road, and finds out afterwards how that looks. She decides not to sail for Westeros ÔÇö she will learn to rule here first ÔÇö and then chains two of her own dragons in the catacombs after the third burns a farmer's daughter.

Season Five

The Sons of the Harpy make the city ungovernable and she answers with everything she has: an execution against her own council's advice, the fighting pits reopened, and a marriage of state to a man she does not want. It all comes apart at Daznak's Pit with knives closing in from every side, and ends with Drogon coming out of the sky, taking the arrows meant for her, and carrying her out over the grass.

Season Six

Taken by a khalasar and marched to Vaes Dothrak to spend her life among the widows of dead khals. She burns the temple down with every khal inside it and walks out of the fire a second time, and forty thousand riders kneel in the ashes. Then she goes back to Meereen, wins it properly, and finally sails ÔÇö Dothraki, Unsullied, the Greyjoy fleet, and alliances in the Reach and in Dorne.

Season Seven

Dragonstone, her family's first seat, which she has never seen. Her advisers talk her into a careful war and Cersei takes it apart piece by piece ÔÇö the fleet, Dorne, Highgarden ÔÇö until she stops listening and takes a dragon and a khalasar to the Roseroad herself, which works completely. Then she flies north to save people who will not thank her for it, and loses Viserion doing it, and watches him get back up with blue eyes.

Season Eight

Winterfell receives her the way the North receives anybody: coldly. In a fortnight she loses Jorah, Rhaegal and Missandei, learns that the man she loves has a better claim than hers, and understands that this country will never love her the way Meereen did. The bells of King's Landing ring surrender and she burns the city anyway, and the argument about that hour has not finished yet ÔÇö it was seeded in every promise about the blood of the dragon and every counsellor who taught her that fear works where love will not.

How it ends for them

On the screen. Slain in the throne room (S8┬ÀE6)

She broke the wheel and burned a city, and stood at last before the throne she'd chased across the world. Jon Snow kissed her and put a dagger in her heart in the same breath. Drogon melted the Iron Throne and carried her east, toward Valyria.

Where the page and the screen part

The published books leave Daenerys mid-arc and alive: A Dance with Dragons ends with Meereen under siege, her marriage to Hizdahr collapsing into poison and treachery, and Daenerys herself carried off by Drogon from the fighting pit to the Dothraki sea, where the last chapter finds her fevered, walking, and met by Khal JhaqoÔÇÖs riders ÔÇö her ending unwritten. Everything the show did afterward, from the burning of the khals to the bells of KingÔÇÖs Landing, is its own invention or extrapolation. The books also surround her with material the show cut whole: the prophecies of the House of the Undying in their full riddling form, the masked shadowbinder QuaitheÔÇÖs recurring warnings, the Meereenese knot of politics compressed on screen into a fraction of its factions, and two entire rival claimants ÔÇö Quentyn Martell, who dies reaching for her dragons, and Aegon, the boy said to be RhaegarÔÇÖs living son ÔÇö whose absence from the show reshapes her Westeros entirely.

The character reads differently in each medium, too. Book Daenerys is younger, more inward, and narrated from inside ÔÇö her chapters are a running argument between the girl who wants a house with a red door and the queen who must be fire and blood, with the phrase if I look back I am lost tolling through them. The show, without her interior voice, externalizes the same argument into advisers and consequences, which is partly why its final turn landed for some viewers as a swerve and for book readers as a long-foreshadowed arrival. On the page, the pieces of the ending ÔÇö whatever ending Martin writes ÔÇö are all still in motion.
