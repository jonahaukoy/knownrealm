Tobho Mott

A master armorer of King's Landing, trained in the mysteries of Qohor, whose shop turns out the finest steel in the city — and takes on the most promising apprentices.

A soul born at Qohor.

The fuller tale

Their story

Tobho Mott's shop at the top of the Street of Steel is where King's Landing keeps its one genuine trade secret. A master armorer out of Qohor — the Free City whose smiths guard the old arts closer than any septon guards scripture — he came west young, built the finest forge in the capital, and lets his prices do his advertising: lords who want mass-produced plate go elsewhere, and lords who want the exceptional pay Tobho what Tobho says. His singular boast, delivered to every wealthy visitor, is the one that matters to the saga: he learned in Qohor the working of Valyrian steel — the reforging of the old blades, a craft perhaps three living smiths in Westeros can honestly claim — and unlike most boasts on the Street of Steel, his is true.

The story visits him for two reasons, and both are load-bearing. The first is his apprentice: the strong, dark-haired bull of a boy whose fees were quietly paid by lords who never explained themselves — Gendry, King Robert's unacknowledged bastard, hidden in plain sight at Tobho's anvil, whose helmet-work Ned Stark admires while conducting the investigation that kills him. Tobho trained the boy well, asked no questions about the gold, and sold his contract to the Night's Watch when men with better reasons than his came asking — a master's protection, rendered in the only currency a tradesman safely can. The second reason is grimmer: when Tywin Lannister wants Ice, the ancestral greatsword of the Starks, melted down and remade into two blades for his own house, the commission goes to the one man in the city who can do it — and the books give the work its due elegy, the smith unable to keep the crimson from running smoke-dark in the steel, as if the North itself refused the dye.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Where the page and the screen part

The books credit him by name with the saga's most symbolically loaded metalwork — the destruction of Ice and the forging of the two swords that become Oathkeeper and Widow's Wail, plus the gilding of the Hound's helm for its grim later career on another man's head — where the show stages the reforging as an anonymous smith under Tywin's supervision in a wordless season-opening sequence, and Tobho himself appears only in the early seasons as Gendry's master. His shop's fate diverges accordingly: book Tobho is alive and working as of the published novels, his forge busy and his secrets intact, while show Tobho simply vanishes from the story once Gendry rows away — an armorer the series no longer needed, left unmentioned and presumably hammering on. In both tellings he is that rarest of King's Landing figures, a man of genuine excellence who survives by selling to all sides and belonging to none.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
