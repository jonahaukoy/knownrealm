Black Walder Rivers

House Frey

A grim, dangerous Frey grandson, baseborn and ambitious, feared even among his own enormous, quarrelsome family.

The fuller tale

In a family the size of a small army, you need a modifier to be remembered, and Walder-called-Black earned his: the darkest-tempered of the old lord’s sprawling line, a hard-handed, cold-eyed man whom even Freys take care not to cross. The show marks him baseborn — a Rivers, carrying the bastard name — and uses him as the family’s blunt instrument, half of the double act with smooth Lame Lothar: one brother to phrase the offer, the other to stand behind it looking like the consequences.

His place in the tale’s memory was fixed in a single moment at the Red Wedding. When the slaughter is done and Catelyn Stark stands alone amid her son’s dead, throat bared, mad with grief, it is Black Walder in the show who steps up behind her and draws the knife across — the last cut of the massacre, delivered without a word. Other men planned the wedding; Black Walder is the one the audience remembers ending it.

The books keep him nastier and subtler: trueborn there, a great-grandson high in the line of succession, feared within the family less for his blade than his appetites — rumor gives him a habit of his kinsmen’s wives — and positioned, as the war grinds on and the heirs ahead of him die badly, uncomfortably close to the Twins themselves. He is the Frey the other Freys whisper about, which in that family is a genuine distinction.

The page and the screen

The two tellings disagree about his very birth: the show’s baseborn “Rivers” is, on the page, trueborn Black Walder Frey, son of Ser Ryman and great-grandson of the old lord. They disagree about the famous killing too — in the books it is Raymund Frey, a different kinsman entirely, who cuts Catelyn’s throat, a deed the show moved to Black Walder to concentrate the audience’s hatred. And they disagree about his ending: the books leave him alive, dangerous, and rising through a thinning succession as the Brotherhood hangs his relatives one by one — including his own father.

The show: Killed at the Twins (S7·E1)

The hardest of Walder's brood, who cut Catelyn Stark's throat, raised his cup with the rest when 'Lord Walder' called the toast. Arya's poison left the women standing and House Frey's swords on the floor.

The show settles his account alongside his brother’s: gathered to the Twins for the great toast, he drinks with the rest of the male line and dies choking in the hall where Catelyn Stark bled — poisoned by the girl whose mother’s throat he cut, wearing his own lord’s face. In the published books he is alive, and if anything more prominent than ever; whatever the novels intend for Black Walder, the rope-and-wine accounting of the screen has not yet reached him.
