How it ends for them

On the screen. Poisoned in Dorne (S5·E10)

Ellaria Sand kissed the princess farewell at Sunspear's docks with poison on her lips. Myrcella died at sea in her father's arms — moments after telling Jaime she knew, and was glad.

In the show she dies of a kiss. Ellaria Sand, unappeased by peace with the Lannisters, bids the princess farewell at Sunspear's docks with poison on her lips — the long farewell, an antidote taken by the poisoner alone — and Myrcella sails for home apparently well. The venom works on the water: moments after telling Jaime she knows he is her father and is glad of it, she bleeds from the nose and dies in his arms, the second golden shroud of Maggy's prophecy delivered mid-embrace. Ellaria's price is paid later, when Cersei returns the exact kiss to her daughter Tyene in the black cells. In the published books Myrcella is alive — scarred by Darkstar's blade during the collapse of Arianne's plot, short an ear, but breathing, and on her way back into the story's center as Dorne weighs its next move.

Their chapters, book by book

A Clash of Kings 2 chapters that name them

Tyrion III Tyrion feeds three councillors three different marriage plots — Myrcella to Dorne among them — and waits to see which secret walks to Cersei. Tyrion IX Myrcella sails for Dorne and the city's rage boils over: the mob tears the High Septon apart, nearly takes Sansa, and the Hound earns his pay.

A Feast for Crows 2 chapters that name them

Arys I In the shade of Dorne, Ser Arys Oakheart lets Arianne Martell talk him out of his white cloak and into a plot: crown Myrcella, and let Dorne rise. Arianne I The queenmaker rides: Arianne spirits Myrcella toward a crown until Darkstar's blade and Areo Hotah's axe end it in blood on the Greenblood.

Where the page and the screen part

The books send her to Dorne on the same logic and into a much more tangled story. There she becomes the centerpiece of Arianne Martell's queenmaking plot — a Dornish scheme to crown Myrcella under Dornish law, which recognizes the eldest child regardless of sex — and when the plot unravels, the knight called Darkstar turns his blade on the child herself; book Myrcella survives him, but maimed, losing an ear and carrying the scar across her cheek. None of it — Arianne, the queenmaking, Darkstar — reaches the show, whose slimmed-down Dorne plot substitutes the Sand Snakes' vengeance for the books' constitutional crisis. She also gets a different confidence: the books have not given her the paternity scene with Jaime, which is the show's own invention and arguably its best addition to her.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
