Jorah Mormont

House Mormont

An exiled knight of Bear Island, the disgraced son of the Old Bear, serving as sword and counsel to a young Targaryen princess across the sea — and falling under her spell.

A soul born at Bear Island, sworn to House Mormont.

The fuller tale

Season Three

Dany offers a dragon for eight thousand Unsullied; Jorah and Barristan protest in unison.

Where they stand. Astapor and Yunkai.

Season Four

In The Mountain and the Viper — One old parchment undoes years of penance. Ser Barristan hands Jorah a royal pardon signed by Robert Baratheon: the spying is out; Dany exiles him.

Where they stand. Meereen.

Season Five

In Kill the Boy — Pulls Tyrion from the water; hides a grey patch on his wrist. Tyrion and Jorah sail through ruined Valyria, past stone men — and one grabs the dwarf. Jorah's 'gift' to the queen in a Meereen pit is Tyrion Lannister, alive and bowing. Dany takes Tyrion as advisor and sends Jorah away a second time.

Where they stand. Volantis, Valyria and Meereen.

Season Seven

Sam risks his chains to cut the greyscale from Ser Jorah in a locked cell. Jorah returns cured; Gendry returns rowed; Jon plans madness: capture a wight to convince two queens.

Where they stand. Oldtown, Dragonstone and King's Landing.

Season Eight

In The Long Night — Fell shielding his queen, sword in hand, at last content.

Where they stand. Winterfell.

How it ends for them

On the screen. Fell defending his queen (S8·E3)

The exile who betrayed his queen, was banished twice, and came back both times died in the snow at Winterfell with his sword in his hand, taking blow after blow meant for Daenerys. He fell at her feet; a dragon mourned over them both.

In the show Jorah dies at the Battle of Winterfell, in the Long Night of the eighth season, exactly where every viewer always knew he would die: between Daenerys and the swords coming for her. When Drogon is driven from the sky and the queen is left standing in the open among the dead, Jorah cuts his way to her and plants himself, taking wound after wound through the last hour of the battle, Heartsbane — Sam Tarly’s family blade, passed to him in the smithy of the man whose family judged him — in his hands. He falls only when the battle is already won, dying in her arms in the snow while Drogon lands and curls a wing around them both: the bear, the dragon, and the queen, the whole hopeless devotion granted one perfect image.

In the published books Jorah Mormont is alive — branded, exiled, unransomed and unredeemed, riding with the Second Sons in the war for Meereen, still carrying his ruin toward whatever ending Martin intends. Book readers note the elder Mormont’s dying wish at Craster’s Keep — find my son, tell him to take the black — as a road sign the novels may yet honor; the show, having given Jorah a better death than the Wall could, let the old bear’s wish go unkept.

Where the page and the screen part

The largest divergence is medical: Jorah’s greyscale is a show invention, transplanted from the books’ Jon Connington, who contracts it saving Tyrion from the stone men of the Sorrows. With Connington cut from the show entirely, his disease, his devotion, and something of his doomed-loyalist flavor were folded into Jorah — an elegant compression that also handed the show its Sam-and-Jorah Citadel sequence, which has no book counterpart. Book Jorah’s road is grimmer and less romantic: sold at auction to the Yunkish lord Yezzan zo Qaggaz alongside Tyrion and the dwarf girl Penny, branded on the cheek with a demon’s mask, escaping in the chaos of the pale mare to join the Second Sons as the siege of Meereen tightens.

The books’ Jorah is also a rougher character — older, blunter, and pushier in his affections, including an unasked-for kiss the show declined to keep — and he remains alive in the published novels, a sellsword with a slave’s brand waiting on a battle that has not yet been fought. His show ending, dying at his queen’s feet in the Long Night, is the series’ own composition from start to finish.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
