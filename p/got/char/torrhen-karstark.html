How it ends for them

On the screen. Fell in the Whispering Wood (S1·E10)

Rode in Robb's van into the Whispering Wood, where the Kingslayer was taken. Jaime Lannister cut him down sword in hand, a Karstark son spent for a Lannister prisoner — a price his father never forgave.

On the page. Fell in the Whispering Wood (AGOT, ch. 64)

He rode in Robb's guard into the Whispering Wood and the Kingslayer's sword found him in the press. A Karstark son traded for a Lannister captive — the beginning of his father's long unraveling.

Torrhen Karstark fell in the Whispering Wood, in the night battle where Robb's trap closed on Jaime Lannister's column. When the Kingslayer, seeing the battle lost, made his last try — cutting through Robb's personal guard to reach the boy king and end the war at a stroke — Torrhen was one of the men who stood in the way, and Jaime's sword killed him there, sword in hand, a pace or two from the most valuable prisoner of the war. The books kill his brother Eddard beside him in the same moments. It was, by every military accounting, a good death in a great victory; his father ran the accounting differently — a son traded for a captive kept politely in chains — and the difference between those two ledgers eventually cost Robb Stark the Karstark army and Lord Rickard his head.

Bran IV Word comes that Torrhen's Square is besieged; Ser Rodrik strips Winterfell's garrison to answer it — precisely as the ironmen intended.

Where the page and the screen part

The tellings differ only in arithmetic and staging: the books kill two Karstark brothers in the Whispering Wood — Torrhen and Eddard both, in Robb's bodyguard, dying within reach of Jaime — while the show economizes to Torrhen alone, dispatched in Jaime's failed lunge for Robb himself. The books also leave a brother standing: Harrion Karstark, captive of the Lannisters, whose survival keeps Karhold's succession alive as a plot thread the show never needed. In both versions Torrhen's real narrative life is posthumous, lived in his father's mouth.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
