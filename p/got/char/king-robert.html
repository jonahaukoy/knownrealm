How it ends for them

On the screen. Gored by a boar (S1┬ÀE7)

The king hunted boar in the kingswood, sodden with wine his squire kept pouring, and the boar won. He died in the Red Keep naming Ned Stark Protector of the Realm ÔÇö a testament altered and betrayed before the body cooled.

On the page. Gored on a hunt (AGOT, ch. 50)

Wine, a boar, and a squire named Lancel who kept the king's cup full ÔÇö the hunt in the kingswood opened him from navel to nipple. He died in the Red Keep amending his will at Ned's gentle dishonesty, easier in his mind than he'd been in years.

The king died as he lived ÔÇö hunting, drunk, and surrounded by people who profited from him being both. In the kingswood, chasing a boar with a skin of strongwine his squire Lancel Lannister dutifully kept full, Robert took the tusks low across the belly. He was days dying in the Red Keep, long enough to make his last mistakes generous ones: he named Ned Stark Protector of the Realm, dictated a will for his 'son Joffrey,' and never saw Ned quietly amend the words to 'my rightful heir' ÔÇö a mercy and a hedge in four words. He asked pardon for the Targaryen girl, too late for the order already sailing. Both tellings agree on the essentials and on the epitaph Robert would have chosen himself: killed by a pig, undone by wine, and betrayed by nearly everyone at the bedside ÔÇö though the wine, as Varys observes, was the assassin that mattered, and Cersei never even had to raise her voice.

Where the page and the screen part

Show and book Robert are unusually close ÔÇö Mark Addy's performance lifts whole scenes from the page, including the crypt scene at Winterfell and the terrible marital post-mortem with Cersei (a show invention, but one that book readers tend to adopt as canon on the spot). The books deepen the edges: Ned's memories give us the young Robert of the Vale in loving detail, the bastards multiply (Mya in the Vale and Edric at Storm's End matter to later plots the show never runs), and his brothers' resentments are given more room ÔÇö Stannis nursing the grudge of Storm's End, Renly noting acidly how little of the old Robert was left. The show also lets Cersei claim their marriage held one good night; the books' version of the ledger is grimmer on both sides.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
