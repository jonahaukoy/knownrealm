How it ends for them

On the screen. Fell beyond the Wall (S2·E10)

Captured with Jon in the Skirling Pass, he ordered the boy to do the one thing that would buy the free folk's trust. He goaded Jon into the fight and died on his sword — a ranger spending his own life like a coin, for the Watch.

On the page. Died beyond the Wall (ACOK, ch. 69)

Hunted through the Skirling Pass, the great ranger ordered Jon to turn his cloak and buy the free folk's trust with a death. He came at Jon like a storm and died on Longclaw as Ghost's teeth closed — exactly as he intended.

He dies on Jon Snow’s sword, exactly as he planned. In the books, cornered in the Skirling Pass with the Rattleshirt’s hunters closing, Qhorin names Jon his brother one last time, then comes at him like a storm — giving the boy no choice and the watching wildlings no doubt — and falls to Longclaw with Ghost at his leg, murmuring that sharp steel is a better death than the ones on offer. The show stages the same transaction inside the wildling camp: a picked quarrel, a seized blade, a fight Qhorin conducts like a lesson until Jon runs him through before Rattleshirt’s whole band. His last words bless the mission — and the free folk, believing they have watched a crow kill a crow, open their world to the spy the Halfhand died to plant. The Watch never gives him a pyre; his monument is everything Jon does after.

Where the page and the screen part

The screen compresses the Skirling Pass — the long hunt through the heights, the dwindling of the scouting party man by man, and Qhorin’s methodical grooming of Jon for the role all shrink to a few scenes of captivity, with the Halfhand sowing Jon’s cover story from inside the wildling column by picking loud fights with him. The books keep the two free until the end and give the final fight a witness the show omits: Ghost, whose teeth close on Qhorin’s calf in the last exchange, letting the ranger die knowing the tale the wildlings will tell is airtight. His great line — “our honor means no more than our lives, so long as the realm is safe” — is the books’ cleanest statement of the Watch’s creed, and the show wisely keeps its substance.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
