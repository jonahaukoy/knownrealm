Arya Stark

House StarkThe Faceless Men

The younger Stark daughter — wilful, quick, happier with a blade than a needle. She named her direwolf Nymeria, for a warrior queen.

A soul of House Stark, born at Winterfell, sworn to The Faceless Men.

The fuller tale

Season One

The Stark who never fitted the portrait: better with a bow than her brothers, hopeless with a needle, and a collector of the names of people nobody else notices. Jon gives her a sword thin enough to use and her father gives her a fencing master, and Syrio Forel's water dance becomes the frame of her whole life. She is in the crowd at Baelor's sept when her father's head comes off, and she leaves the city that night with a shorn head among Yoren's recruits.

Season Two

The Gold Cloaks catch the column on the kingsroad and she buries a boy's name in the mud along with her own. Harrenhal makes her a Lannister cupbearer, close enough to Tywin Lannister to pour his wine and listen to him talk about legacies — and Jaqen H'ghar offers her three deaths for the three lives she saved, which she spends badly and then brilliantly.

Season Three

The Brotherhood Without Banners sell her to the Hound, which turns out to be an education. He takes her to the Twins to be ransomed and they arrive minutes late, in time to hear the music change and see her brother's army butchered from the outside of it.

Season Four

North with the Hound through a country that has stopped pretending: the Vale, a fight at an inn, and a man she once knew from a list. When the Hound is beaten and asks her for the mercy of a quick end she takes his coin instead and leaves him under a tree, and buys passage to Braavos with a piece of iron.

Season Five

The House of Black and White wants everything she has in exchange for the power to be no one. She gives up her clothes, her possessions and eventually her name, and hides Needle in the rocks rather than surrender it, which loses her the argument before it starts. She steals a face to kill Meryn Trant, which was not hers to steal, and is struck blind for it.

Season Six

Blind and beaten in the streets until she is not. Offered the last of the bargain, she refuses it, takes Needle back out of the rocks, and puts out the candle in the dark. She comes home the long way, by way of a pie at the Twins and a hall of Freys with a cup in their hands.

Season Seven

Winterfell, and a sister she has not seen since the sept. Littlefinger works on the two of them all autumn with letters and hints and very nearly succeeds — and then does not, in the great hall, where he learns which one of them was actually listening.

Season Eight

She is the smallest soldier in the war and the tale hands her the most consequential act of violence in it, in the godswood, out of the dark. Afterwards she walks out of a burning city on a white horse, tells her family what she has decided, and sails west of Westeros, where the maps stop — not a lady, not a killer, not no one.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Their chapters, book by book

A Game of Thrones 5 chapters of their own, and 2 more that name them

Arya I Arya botches her stitches and flees the sewing circle to watch the boys spar, counting all the ways the world is unfair to girls with skinny arms. Jon II Farewells at Winterfell: Jon gives Arya a sword called Needle and says goodbye to a Bran who cannot hear him. Sansa I By the Trident, Joffrey's cruelty and Arya's temper collide; Nymeria bites the prince and is driven off with stones, and the lie begins. Arya II Ned finds Needle — and instead of taking it away, hires Syrio Forel, the First Sword of Braavos, to teach his daughter water dancing. Arya III Chasing cats through the Red Keep, Arya overhears two men in the cellars plotting delay and war — Varys and Illyrio, though she cannot name them. Arya IV The purge reaches the Tower of the Hand: Syrio Forel holds the door with a wooden sword; Arya finds Needle and learns what swords are for. Arya V On Baelor's steps Ned Stark confesses to treason to save his daughters; Joffrey wants a head anyway. Yoren grabs Arya in the crowd: “boy.”

A Clash of Kings 10 chapters of their own

Arya I Yoren's ragged column creaks out of King's Landing: Arya is “Arry” now, an orphan boy among orphan boys, with Gendry, Hot Pie, and three chained men in a wagon. Arya II Gold cloaks ride out from the city with a warrant — for Gendry; Yoren's dirk and thirty ragged brothers send them back empty-handed. Arya III North along the gods eye the column crawls through a riverlands already burning; Arya keeps Needle close and her true name closer. Arya IV Ser Amory Lorch storms the holdfast by night: Yoren dies at the barn door, and Arya frees the three chained men from the fire before crawling out the tunnel. Arya V The captives are marched to Harrenhal; when Lommy Greenhands cannot walk, Raff the Sweetling answers his plea for carrying with a spear. Arya VI A drudge in Harrenhal, Arya whispers her list of names; Jaqen H'ghar owes the Red God three lives, and Chiswyck falls from a wall to pay the first. Arya VII Weasel soup: Jaqen, Rorge and Biter serve Harrenhal's garrison to the northmen, and the castle changes hands to Roose Bolton without a battle. Arya VIII Harrenhal under Bolton: Arya becomes the lord's cupbearer; Jaqen pays his last debt, offers her the iron coin, and changes his face. Valar morghulis. Arya IX Roose Bolton's Harrenhal is no kinder than the Mountain's; Arya hears the northmen's plans and weighs Jaqen's lesson — a girl can free herself. Arya X Arya takes a map, a knife, and two companions out of Harrenhal's postern; the Bolton guard at the gate learns what Needle is for.

A Storm of Swords 13 chapters of their own

Arya I Loose in the riverlands, Arya, Gendry and Hot Pie ride for Riverrun — until singers with swords find them at an inn: Tom o' Sevens and the brotherhood without banners. Arya II Blindfolded and singing, the brotherhood leads Arya through the riverlands; Harwin — her father's man — tells her of the lightning lord who will not stay dead. Arya III Through fought-over towns the outlaws collect coin, news and prisoners; Arya measures the distance to Riverrun and finds it growing instead of shrinking. Arya IV At Acorn Hall Lady Smallwood puts Arya in a dress with acorns on it; Tom's songs and Gendry's forge make the war feel briefly far away. Arya V In the hollow hill the brotherhood tries the Hound before Beric Dondarrion: trial by combat against a flaming sword — Sandor walks free, and a dead man is kissed back to life for the sixth time. Arya VI The brotherhood turns bounty-keeper: Arya will be ransomed to her family — but the lightning lord's war of hedges and hollows keeps carrying her the wrong way. Arya VII Arya runs from the brotherhood into worse: the Hound takes her for his prize, to sell to her mother at the Twins — the last unbroken thread of her family. Arya VIII Under storm-swollen skies the Hound drives hard for the Twins, racing her uncle's wedding with Arya on his saddle and no good plan at the end of the road. Arya IX Rain, mud and a swollen river: the Twins rise out of the storm, wedding torches burning, and something in the music makes even the Hound's skin crawl. Arya X So close to her mother that she can see the castle: Arya and the Hound circle the wedding camps as the feast begins, and the portcullises quietly close. Arya XI Arya reaches the Twins as the slaughter spills outward; the Hound's axe-flat puts her down before the camp can — and that night she wears Nymeria's skin and finds a body in the river. Arya XII Fever and rain on the road to nowhere: the Hound weakens while Arya recites her list of names each night like a prayer — his is still on it. Arya XIII At the crossroads inn Polliver is wearing Needle: the Hound takes his wine, Arya takes her sword back, and leaves Sandor dying under a tree — off her list, unlisted.

A Feast for Crows 3 chapters of their own

Arya I The Titan of Braavos straddles the harbor; a copper coin and a word open the House of Black and White, where all men must serve: valar dohaeris. Arya II Cat of the Canals sells cockles and gathers secrets; when a deserter of the Night's Watch sings for coin in the Happy Port, the Many-Faced God is given his due. Arya III Each night the girl confesses: Arya of House Stark, Needle under a stone. After Dareon dies, the kindly man gives her warm milk — and she wakes blind.

A Dance with Dragons 2 chapters of their own, and 1 more that name them

Reek III Winterfell hosts a wedding in the snow: Ramsay Bolton takes “Arya Stark” — Jeyne Poole in a stolen name — and Reek gives the bride away into horror. Arya I A blind girl begs on Braavos's canals and learns to see with skin and ears; each night the kindly man asks who she is, and each night the lie tastes safer: no one. Arya II The ugly little girl gets her sight back and her first gift to give: an old man on the docks who sells death sheets — and a new face from the hall beneath the temple.

Where the page and the screen part

Book Arya is younger — nine when it begins — and her Braavos chapters run deeper and stranger than the show's: the blind beggar Beth, the canal-cat Cat of the Canals, the Mercy chapter where she kills a name from her list while acting in a stage play. Everything after Braavos, though, is the show's invention. The published books have not brought her home; the Frey massacre, the Winterfell reunion and the godswood dagger have no book counterpart yet, and the Night King himself does not exist on the page. Readers know the girl; only viewers, so far, know the legend.

The show also pruned her companions and reassigned her encounters — book Arya never serves Tywin at Harrenhal (her cupbearer duty is to Roose Bolton, a far colder room), never meets Melisandre, and parts from the Hound by leaving him dying rather than robbed. The Tywin scenes are pure invention and among the show's best additions, a writers' room finding gold in a pairing the books never imagined. Her wolf, meanwhile, matters more in the books: Nymeria leads a huge pack through the riverlands, and Arya wolf-dreams her way into it — a thread the show reduced to a single roadside farewell.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
