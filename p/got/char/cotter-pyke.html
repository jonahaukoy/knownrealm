Cotter Pyke

The Night's Watch

A baseborn ironborn risen to command Eastwatch-by-the-Sea, a rough, fierce, and able captain of the Night's Watch (book).

The fuller tale

Cotter Pyke is what the Night’s Watch does with talent that arrives without a name. Baseborn on the Iron Islands — his surname is the one the ironborn stamp on bastards — he came to the Wall rough and stayed rough, and rose anyway: command of Eastwatch-by-the-Sea, the Watch’s port and fleet, held by a man who cannot comfortably read the letters his maester writes for him. The brothers who sneer at his birth do it quietly and from a distance, because Cotter Pyke has never lost a fight he could reach.

The books pair him with his opposite number for one of the Watch’s best running jokes: Ser Denys Mallister of the Shadow Tower, silver-haired, courteous, impeccably born — and the two commanders detest each other with a completeness that turns every council into a diplomatic incident. During the drawn-out election of a new Lord Commander, their rival candidacies deadlock the Watch for days, a stalemate broken only when Sam Tarly plays the two old enemies against each other to clear the path for Jon Snow. Pyke takes the outcome the way he takes everything: profanely, and then back to work.

His great assignment is the saga’s most dreaded errand. When word comes that thousands of free folk are starving at Hardhome with the dead closing in, it is Cotter Pyke who takes eleven ships north to bring them off — the Watch doing its true work at its farthest reach. What comes back is not a fleet but a correspondence: terse, misspelled, increasingly grim letters that read like a horror story told by a man too stubborn to raise his voice, ending with a plea for help by land and the line readers quote with a shiver — dead things in the woods, dead things in the water.

The page and the screen

The show cut him and split the inheritance. Eastwatch-by-the-Sea survives on screen — handed to Tormund to garrison, and demolished by the ice dragon — but its foul-mouthed commander does not, and his Hardhome mission is transferred wholesale to Jon Snow and Tormund so the audience can sail with it; Karsi and the massacre episode occupy the space the books fill with Pyke’s letters. The election farce with Ser Denys is likewise gone, folded into a quicker show of hands. Book-readers keep a wary eye on his story: as the published saga stands he is alive — or at least unaccounted for — somewhere north of the Wall at Hardhome, at the end of a paper trail that stops mid-plea. The Watch has heard nothing since, and neither have we.
