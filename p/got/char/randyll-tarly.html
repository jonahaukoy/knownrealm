How it ends for them

On the screen. Fell after the Reach campaign (S7·E5)

Sam's iron father chose Cersei over the queen who beat him at the Blackwater Rush, and refused the knee with his son beside him. Daenerys gave the order; Drogon's fire left two suits of armor in the ash.

The show ends him on principle — the only currency he ever respected. Captured after Daenerys's Dothraki and her dragon annihilate the Lannister-Tarly column at the Goldroad, Randyll refuses the knee with his heir at his side: he will not trade his loyalties at spear-point, he tells the foreign queen, and declines the Wall's mercy on the grounds that she has no authority to send him there. Dickon steps forward to share the sentence over his father's one flicker of protest. Daenerys gives the order; Drogon's fire takes them both, and the ashes blow across a field that used to be an army. Tyrion, who argued for mercy, and Samwell, who learns of it later from a kindness-blunted Jorah, both carry the scene forward as a question about their queen. In the published books Randyll Tarly is alive and consequential, a sword still hanging over several plots at once.

Where the page and the screen part

Book Randyll never faces the choice the show built for him: in the published pages the sack of Highgarden has not happened, and Randyll — master of laws on the boy king's council, with custody of the imprisoned Margaery — is positioned as the Reach's kingmaker rather than its casualty, his army camped where it can decide several futures at once. His justice at Maidenpool and his flaying contempt for Brienne are book-only textures; the Horn Hill dinner is show-only staging of an ultimatum the books deliver years earlier and crueler. The show's field execution has no book counterpart; in the novels the hardest man in the Reach is alive, armed, and holding hostages the plot badly wants.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
