Missandei

A young scribe and translator of the peaceable island of Naath, fluent in the tongues of the world, freed from slavery in Astapor.

A soul born at Naath.

The fuller tale

Season Three

A slave translator working for Kraznys mo Nakloz in Astapor, softening his contempt into something more diplomatic each time she renders it into the Common Tongue for Daenerys — sparing her, or trying to. It turns out not to matter: Daenerys has understood the Astapori Valyrian all along and has heard every insult unfiltered, and uses that same language to turn the Unsullied against their masters in front of the whole city, Missandei included. Freed along with the army when Astapor falls, she has nowhere obvious left to go and stays anyway, choosing Daenerys over a home she hasn't seen since she was a child.

Season Four

In Meereen now, teaching Grey Worm and the other freed Unsullied to read and speak the Common Tongue in whatever hours the war doesn't take, and finding in him someone as careful with words as she is. She advises Daenerys on the business of ruling former slaves who don't agree among themselves on what freedom should look like, translating not just language now but temperament — whose anger is real, whose grief is real, whose flattery isn't. It's slow, unglamorous work, and she does it without complaint, steadier than almost anyone else around the queen.

Season Five

Meereen curdles around her — Sons of the Harpy in the alleys, masters plotting in daylight, a fighting pit Daenerys reopens against her own better judgment. When Grey Worm is carried back near death from an ambush, she sits with him and admits, plainly, that she would have grieved him, which is as close as either of them has come to saying more. She holds the city together with Tyrion and the rest of the small council once Daenerys vanishes on dragonback, running a government that, for a while, has no queen in it.

Season Six

Holds Meereen with Grey Worm and Tyrion while the masters' fleet burns the harbor, cool under pressure in a room full of men losing theirs, and helps talk Daenerys back from torching the city's remaining enemies without a plan first. When the fleet finally sails for Westeros she goes with it, translator no longer strictly needed but advisor very much still.

Season Seven

At Dragonstone as the councils get larger and the arguments get sharper, still the one person Daenerys trusts to say a hard thing plainly rather than prettily. Before the Unsullied sail for Casterly Rock, she and Grey Worm finally say aloud what the last few years have only implied — unhurried about it, because there has never been a good time and they've stopped waiting for one.

Season Eight

North to Winterfell for the fight against the dead, then south again with the fleet toward King's Landing — and the fleet is the trap. Euron's ships take her before she ever reaches shore, and Cersei has her walked out onto the Red Keep's walls in chains and beheaded in front of Daenerys and Grey Worm both, her last word — Dracarys — less a plea than an instruction.

How it ends for them

On the screen. Executed at King's Landing (S8·E4)

Captured at sea and paraded in chains on the walls of King's Landing, the freedwoman of Naath was beheaded by the Mountain at Cersei's nod, in full view of her queen and Grey Worm. Her last word was Dracarys.

The show’s Missandei dies in the eighth season, in the war’s cruelest staging. Captured at sea in Euron Greyjoy’s ambush off Dragonstone, she is held in King’s Landing and brought out in chains onto the city walls — before Daenerys, Grey Worm, and the assembled parley — as Cersei’s answer to the demand for surrender. Offered last words, the freedwoman of Naath chooses the queen’s own word of fire: Dracarys. The Mountain beheads her where she stands, in her chains, in front of the man who loves her and the queen who freed her — the chains a deliberate, calculated obscenity for a woman whose whole story was the removal of them.

The death functions exactly as Cersei intended and rather more than she intended: it is the moment the campaign for King’s Landing stops being a war of thrones and becomes, for Daenerys and for Grey Worm, a matter of grief — the last counterweight to fire gone. In the published books Missandei is alive in Meereen, a child at a queen’s side, her story still unwritten; the show’s walls-of-King’s-Landing ending belongs to the adaptation alone.

Their chapters, book by book

Where the page and the screen part

Book Missandei is a child — about ten when Daenerys frees her, a solemn, uncannily clever little scribe whom the queen half mothers, and whose brothers, taken with her, were made Unsullied; one of them, Mossador, the show borrowed by name for a Meereen subplot. She has no romance with Grey Worm on the page — the show aged her up and invented the relationship whole — and she remains alive in the published books, serving in Meereen through the queen’s absence. The show’s Missandei, in Nathalie Emmanuel’s performance, became one of the adaptation’s most significant expansions: a background functionary grown into a principal, whose death was written specifically to detonate the final act — a purpose the books, with their living, eleven-year-old Missandei, cannot and likely will not mirror.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
