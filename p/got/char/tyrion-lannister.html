Tyrion Lannister

House Lannister

The Imp: a dwarf, and the cleverest of the Lannisters, which his father has never forgiven him. He drinks, he reads, and he knows things ÔÇö the only weapons his family left him.

A soul of House Lannister, born at Casterly Rock.

The fuller tale

Season One

Rides north with the king's party to see the Wall and piss off the edge of the world, and builds a crippled boy a saddle before anyone has given him a reason to be kind. Taken on the road by Catelyn Stark for a murder attempt he had nothing to do with, he talks his way out of a sky cell, wins a trial by combat by hiring somebody better than him, and is sent to King's Landing by his father to be acting Hand ÔÇö a post given to him as a punishment.

Season Two

His masterpiece, and nobody notices. He runs the city, removes Cersei's people from the small council one by one without ever raising his voice, ships Myrcella to Dorne to keep her alive, and saves King's Landing at the Blackwater with a chain across the river and a bay full of wildfire. He takes a sword across the face doing it, and wakes up in a small room, demoted, disfigured and written out of the song.

Season Three

Married to Sansa Stark on his father's order, which neither of them wants, and refuses to touch her ÔÇö which is the most decent thing anybody does to her all year and earns him nothing from anyone. He watches Tywin distribute titles like a man settling accounts and is handed the office of Master of Coin as a joke about his height.

Season Four

Joffrey dies at his own wedding with a cup in Tyrion's hand, and the trial his family has wanted for thirty-nine years finally has a courtroom. Every witness is true and every word is a lie. He demands trial by combat, watches Oberyn Martell win and then lose in the same second, and escapes through his brother's mercy and the tunnels ÔÇö by way of Shae in his father's bed and a crossbow on his father's privy.

Season Five

Crated to Pentos, drinking, and then dragged east by Jorah Mormont as a gift for a queen. Stone men in the Sorrows, a slaver's pen, a fighting pit ÔÇö and then Daenerys, who has every reason to kill a Lannister and instead listens to him for an hour and makes him her adviser.

Season Six

Left to rule Meereen while she is gone, and does it the way he does everything: by making a deal nobody likes. He buys seven years of peace from the masters, which half works and looks like betrayal to the freedmen, and then walks into a pyramid basement and unchains two dragons because somebody has to.

Season Seven

Hand of the Queen, and increasingly a man whose advice is either overruled or overtaken. He argues for a careful war and watches it lose; he arranges the meeting at the Dragonpit and gets a truce out of it that lasts about a minute; he goes under the Red Keep to reason with his sister and is lied to.

Season Eight

Sees everything clearly and always slightly too late. He frees Jaime and is arrested for it, finds his brother and sister in the rubble, and throws his badge of office down a flight of steps. Then from a cell he talks the lords of Westeros into choosing a king by asking them what unites people ÔÇö and is sentenced, with perfect irony, to spend the rest of his life doing the job he is best at and never wanted.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul ÔÇö they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Where the page and the screen part

The Tysha cut is the great divergence, and it detonates twice. In the books, Jaime frees Tyrion personally and ÔÇö as a parting honesty ÔÇö confesses that Tysha was never a whore: the marriage was real, the love was real, and the lesson was Tywin's lie. Tyrion answers by telling Jaime a lie of his own out of pure spite, poisoning the brothers' farewell, and goes up the ladder to his father with the truth burning in him; his last exchange with Tywin is not about Shae but about where whores go. The show, which erased Tysha after a single first-season mention, plays the escape as tender and the murder as grief ÔÇö and consequently ships a gentler Tyrion into exile. Book five's Tyrion is a far darker passenger: drunk, suicidal, cruel to nearly everyone, wandering the Rhoyne with a legitimate hidden Targaryen claimant the show never cast, a dwarf girl named Penny whose kindness shames him, and a nose he no longer has ÔÇö the books took the whole thing at the Blackwater, where the show settled for a scar.

His book arc is also, as of the published novels, unfinished business: he has never actually met Daenerys. He ends the fifth book newly signed to the Second Sons, maneuvering outside a plague-ridden Meereen under siege, his queen still a rumor on a hilltop. Everything the show gives him from the fighting pits onward ÔÇö the Handship, the wars, the verdicts ÔÇö is television's own invention, built on the books' promissory note that the cleverest Lannister and the last Targaryen belong in the same room.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
