Jon Snow

House StarkHouse TargaryenThe Night's Watch

Ned Stark's bastard son, raised at Winterfell and always a half-step apart from it. Skilled with a blade, hungry to prove himself, and bound for the Wall. His direwolf is the albino Ghost.

A soul of House Stark, born at Winterfell, sworn to The Night's Watch.

The fuller tale

Season One

Ned Stark's bastard, raised inside the family and seated below the salt, and quicker than his trueborn brothers to notice the people the world overlooks. He takes the black expecting the order of the songs and finds thieves, poachers and old men holding seven hundred feet of ice; he is made a steward instead of a ranger and is furious about it until he understands why. He pulls the Old Bear out of a burning room and is given Longclaw for it. When word comes that his father is dead and his brother has raised the banners he rides south in the night, and his friends ride after him and talk him back.

Season Two

Beyond the Wall with the Great Ranging: Craster's Keep, where he learns what the Watch is willing to overlook for a warm hall, and then the Fist of the First Men. Paired with Qhorin Halfhand and sent scouting, he cannot bring himself to cut a wildling girl's throat, and it costs them everything. Qhorin's last order is the hardest one in the Watch's book ÔÇö turn your cloak, and make them believe it ÔÇö and the only way to make them believe it is to kill Qhorin himself.

Season Three

Among the free folk as a spy who keeps forgetting that he is one. He climbs the Wall with Ygritte, sees the world from the top of it, and finds that the wildlings are not an army of monsters but a hundred quarrelling peoples running from something worse. At Queenscrown he refuses to kill an old horse-breeder to prove himself, and the pretence ends there. He rides back to Castle Black with three arrows in him, having broken faith with everybody in the story except himself.

Season Four

Castle Black holds against a hundred thousand for one night, and the man who has been telling everybody he is not in charge finds himself in charge. Ygritte dies in the yard with an arrow in her back and his name in her mouth. Then he walks out through the gate alone to murder Mance Rayder under a parley ÔÇö a plan with no version in which he survives it ÔÇö and is interrupted by Stannis Baratheon's cavalry coming out of the north.

Season Five

Elected Lord Commander by one vote, and spends the office on the one thing the Watch will not forgive. He sails to Hardhome to bring the free folk south of the Wall, and sees on that beach exactly what he has been arguing about: the Night King raising every corpse in the village with his arms out. He brings the survivors through the gate anyway. His own officers gather in the yard, tell him it is for the Watch, and put their knives in him one at a time.

Season Six

Brought back by a red priestess who did not expect it to work, and comes back angrier and colder and no longer bound by anything. He hangs the men who killed him, hands his cloak to Edd, and quits. Then Sansa arrives, and the North has to be taken back from the Boltons with an army that does not exist. He very nearly dies under the bodies at the Battle of the Bastards. He is proclaimed King in the North by a hall of lords who spent the whole campaign refusing him men ÔÇö and in a vision at a tower in Dorne, his brother learns who his mother was.

Season Seven

Goes to Dragonstone with nothing to offer and asks a queen for dragonglass, and ends up bending the knee to her ÔÇö for the war, he says, and by then not only for the war. He leads seven men beyond the Wall to drag a corpse back as an argument, which works, and costs Daenerys a dragon that gets up again on the wrong side. Sam and Bran, in different rooms, put together the thing nobody has ever asked: whose son he actually is.

Season Eight

He tells his sisters the truth about his name, which is the same as telling the realm. He rides a dragon, holds the left at Winterfell through the Long Night, and watches a city that had already surrendered burn from the ground. The last thing he does in the war is walk up to the woman he loves in the throne room and put a knife in her, because nobody else could get close enough ÔÇö and the realm, unable to decide whether that was murder or mercy, sends him back to the Wall and lets him keep going past it.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul ÔÇö they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Where the page and the screen part

Book Jon's story ends earlier and harder: A Dance with Dragons closes with the daggers ÔÇö Bowen Marsh weeping as he strikes, the word 'Ghost' in Jon's mouth, and no resurrection on the page. Everything after is, for readers, still a promise: Melisandre is at the Wall and Ghost is nearby, and the books have seeded warging heavily enough that many expect Jon's death to detour through his wolf, but the show's clean revival is unconfirmed text. Likewise R+L=J: the books have never said it outright ÔÇö it lives in Ned's fever-dreams and a thousand close readings ÔÇö and 'Aegon' is a name the books have already given to another claimant entirely, a young man marching on Westeros as the show never mentions.

The politics around book Jon are also thicker: his rebuilding of the Watch is a chapter-by-chapter grind of garrisons, loans and hostages; Stannis is at the Wall for much of it, offering to legitimize him as Jon Stark and getting refused; and the provocation for the mutiny differs ÔÇö the show's Jon dies for loving wildlings, the book's Jon announces he will ride south against Ramsay Bolton, arguably breaking his vows in earnest. And the Pink Letter that provokes it remains one of the books' great authorship puzzles. The show streamlined all of it into cleaner martyrdom ÔÇö fewer moving parts, same knives.

Their journey
