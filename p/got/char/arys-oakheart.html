On the page. Cut down in Dorne (AFFC, ch. 22)

The white knight let Arianne Martell talk him into crowning Myrcella, and rode at Areo Hotah's line on the Greenblood when the plot broke. The captain's longaxe took him almost gently ÔÇö Hotah liked him, and made it quick.

His death is book-only and takes less than a page. When the queenmaker conspiracy is betrayed and Areo Hotah's men close the trap on the Greenblood, Arys ÔÇö seeing the plot dead, his vows twice-broken, and no version of the morning he can survive with honor ÔÇö sets spurs and charges the captain's line alone, sword up, against a score of spears. The books are careful to make it neither madness nor accident: it is a chosen end, the last decision left to a man who could no longer live as either a true knight or a false one. Hotah's longaxe meets the charge and takes his head half from his shoulders ÔÇö quickly, the captain notes, because he had liked the man, and quick was the only kindness left on the river that dawn. Myrcella is maimed in the same chaos; Arianne carries the guilt of both into every chapter she has left.

Where the page and the screen part

The show cut Arys along with the entire queenmaker plot; its Myrcella-in-Dorne arc needed no compromised guardian, and his narrative office ÔÇö the outsider's eyes on Sunspear ÔÇö went unfilled. The cut is quietly expensive: his chapter is one of the books' key windows on Arianne, on Dornish law, and on what the Kingsguard's vows have curdled into by the war's middle years, a theme the books route through Arys, Jaime and Barristan as a running argument the show only samples. Old Oak's knight remains, for show watchers, a man who never existed.
