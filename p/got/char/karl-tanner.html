Karl Tanner

The Night's Watch

A cutthroat of King's Landing turned black brother, ringleader of the mutineers who seize Craster's Keep.

The fuller tale

Karl Tanner introduces himself as a legend — a fookin’ legend, of Gin Alley, in Flea Bottom — and the alarming thing is that the boast checks out. A paid knifeman from the capital’s worst streets, he brought to the Night’s Watch the skills of the Flea Bottom cutthroat: speed, viciousness, and a total, liberating absence of scruple. The Wall took him as it takes everyone, and discovered too late that some men treat vows the way they treat locks — as an inconvenience between them and the goods.

The mutiny at Craster’s Keep is his masterpiece and his coronation. It is Karl who turns the starving remnant’s grievance into murder — taunting Craster to his feet and putting a dirk up through his skull mid-boast — and Karl who rules the keep afterward, drinking wine from the Old Bear’s skull and presiding over a court of deserters with Craster’s wives as its currency. His speech about being better than wellborn commanders — he was killing for pay in King’s Landing while they played with wooden swords — is the mutiny’s whole philosophy: the Watch’s discipline was only ever fear, and beyond the Wall the fear points the other way.

He matters as the tale’s demonstration of what the Watch is holding back inside its own walls. The order recruits from dungeons and calls the result brothers; Karl Tanner is the residue that oath-making cannot dissolve, the reminder that some of the swords guarding the realms of men were pointed at men for money the week before. When the Watch returns to burn the keep, it is not avenging Mormont so much as auditing itself.

The page and the screen

Karl Tanner as the screen knows him is a show creation — the name, the Gin Alley legend, the skull goblet. The books’ mutiny has no single ringleader with his profile: Craster dies to the ranger Dirk’s knife in a spontaneous brawl over hidden food, the Old Bear to a tangle of hands, and the mutineers who squat at the keep afterward are a rabble — Dirk, Ollo Lophand, Clubfoot Karl (the name’s book-cousin), Garth of Greenaway and the rest — with no court and no philosophy. Nor does the Watch return to punish them: in the books that reckoning belongs to a stranger errand, as Coldhands hunts the mutineers through the haunted forest on Bran Stark’s behalf and leaves what remains for the crows. The show consolidated the rabble into one memorable villain and gave the Watch the vengeance the books outsourced.

The show: Killed at Craster's Keep (S4·E5)

The mutineers' 'fookin' legend of Gin Alley' ruled Craster's Keep on rum and cruelty until the Watch came back for its own. Jon Snow fought him hand to hand, and a daughter of Craster's put the final knife in his back.

He dies in the retaking of Craster’s Keep, in the best fight the show’s Wall arc had staged to that point. When Jon Snow comes through the door, Karl meets him with Flea Bottom rules — two knives, spat insults, dirty feints — and is winning, cutting the swordsman apart at close quarters, when one of Craster’s wives buries a blade in his back for every night of his reign. He turns on her, and Jon finishes it: a sword driven through the back of Karl Tanner’s skull and out his mouth, ending the legend of Gin Alley mid-sneer. The keep is burned with his body in it. Having never existed in the books, he has no fate there to await.
