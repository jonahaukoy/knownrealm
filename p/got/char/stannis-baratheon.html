Stannis Baratheon

House Baratheon

Robert's dour middle brother, Lord of Dragonstone: humorless, rigid, and unbending in his sense of what is owed him by right and law.

A soul of House Baratheon, born at Storm's End.

The fuller tale

Season Two

In The North Remembers — The rightful heir, with the charm of a ledger. Stannis Baratheon proclaims his claim and burns the Seven on Dragonstone's shore for the red god. Davos recruits the pirate Salladhor Saan to Stannis's cause. In Garden of Bones — Offers his brother one night to reconsider. One.

Renly and Stannis parley and part as enemies; Davos rows Melisandre into a cave beneath Storm's End. The shadow kills Renly in his own tent; his hundred thousand scatter to Stannis and Highgarden. Stannis's fleet sails; Tyrion and Bronn count wildfire jars and chain links. In Blackwater — Dragged from the walls still swinging.

Stannis's fleet enters the bay; one empty ship meets it, leaking wildfire.

Where they stand. Dragonstone and King's Landing.

Season Three

In Second Sons — Three leeches in a fire, three kings named.

Season Four

In The Laws of Gods and Men — The Iron Bank backs grim arithmetic over golden promises. Stannis and Davos ask the Iron Bank for gold; the ledger votes Stannis. In The Children — The only king who answered the Watch's call. Stannis's cavalry breaks the wildling camp; Mance yields; the debt to the realm is paid at the Wall.

Where they stand. Braavos, Dragonstone and Castle Black.

Season Five

Stannis offers Mance Rayder his life for a knee; Mance chooses the fire, and Jon's arrow ends it early. In Sons of the Harpy — The stone doctors said to give up on Shireen. He didn't. Melisandre tempts Jon; Stannis explains greyscale to Shireen the only way he can: I fought for you. Stannis marches from Castle Black for Winterfell, family and red woman in train.

In The Dance of Dragons — Sacrificed the only person who loved him for a melted snowdrift. Ramsay's twenty men burn Stannis's camp; the march to Winterfell strands in snow and hunger. Stannis burns Shireen. The princess who taught men to read screams her father's name. Stannis's host deserts; Brienne finally gets her sentence — and Ramsay's men get the field.

How it ends for them

On the screen. Fell at Winterfell (S5·E10)

The last of his army broken before Winterfell's walls, his daughter's ashes behind him, Stannis was found wounded against a tree by Brienne of Tarth. She named her charge — the murder of Renly — and he answered 'Do your duty,' and she did.

In the show, the end comes as a ledger being balanced. His sacrifice of Shireen buys a thaw and costs him half his army by desertion and his wife by her own hand; Melisandre abandons him last. He marches the remnant on Winterfell anyway — because the alternative is that the price was paid for nothing — and the Bolton host breaks it in the field. Wounded against a tree in the snow, he is found by Brienne of Tarth, who has waited years to name the charge: the murder of Renly, by blood magic and a shadow. Stannis does not dispute it. 'Do your duty,' he tells her, and she does. The camera looks away at the last — a famous ambiguity the show later closes by confirming his death.

In the published books Stannis is alive — entrenched in the snows outside Winterfell, planning battle, with Shireen unburned at Castle Black and Melisandre far from his side. A letter reaching Jon Snow claims Stannis is dead and broken; the letter's author is Ramsay Bolton, and nothing in it is verified. The books' readers hold their breath on a battle not yet fought.

Tyrion VIII Stannis has sailed: Tyrion works the city's defenses — a chain for the river, wildfire for the fleet, and a nephew-king who is worse than useless. Davos III The Blackwater becomes green fire: the trap closes on Stannis's fleet, and Davos loses his ship, four sons, and nearly the sea itself. Sansa VII The tide turns: riders in green armor under a dead king's banners shatter Stannis on the shore — Tywin and the Tyrells, and the city is saved for the lion.

A Storm of Swords 4 chapters that name them

Davos III Brought up from the dark, Davos expects the fire and speaks truth anyway; Stannis has questions the red woman cannot hear, and an onion knight's honesty to answer them. Davos VI Davos gambles his head to read Stannis a letter from the Wall — and wins: the king who must save the realm to win the throne will sail north. Jon IX Sent under a peace banner to kill Mance Rayder, Jon is saved from his own conscience when Stannis's host falls on the wildling camp like a hammer. Jon XI Stannis offers Jon Snow everything: Winterfell, legitimacy, a name — for a knee bent to a red god and a godswood put to the torch. The bastard hesitates.

A Dance with Dragons 6 chapters that name them

Jon I The new Lord Commander divides spoils and grudges with King Stannis; Melisandre offers Jon her fires and a warning — daggers in the dark. Asha I At Deepwood Motte the kraken's daughter reads letters from a broken home — then Stannis's host comes out of the trees, and Asha Greyjoy is the king's prize. Where the page and the screen part

No principal character divides book readers and show watchers more sharply. The books' Stannis — dry, precise, bleakly funny in a way he himself never notices — is a serious contender for the best military mind in the story, and his Dance with Dragons campaign is a study in adaptation and grit. The show compresses him toward his worst hour: the humor is pared away, the northern strategy shrinks to a doomed slog, and choices the books frame as Melisandre's temptations become his own decisions, made onscreen with open eyes. Book partisans call the show's version character assassination; the show's defenders point out that the books load the same gun — the tellings disagree about whether Stannis fires it.

Smaller divergences ripple outward. Book Stannis never burns men for apostasy at Dragonstone quite as the show stages it; his brother's shadow is dealt with at Storm's End in front of witnesses the show trims; Renly's peach, Proudwing the hawk, and the bleak wedding-night arithmetic with Selyse are all book textures the show never touches. And the show's Stannis loses his campaign before Winterfell entirely, while the books leave him mid-move on the board — with a letter claiming his defeat that the fifth book pointedly declines to confirm.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
