Areo Hotah

House Martell

Captain of Prince Doran's guard, a Norvoshi bought as a boy and bound by his longaxe and an oath to serve, protect, and obey. He watches, and misses nothing.

The fuller tale

Areo Hotah was made in Norvos, and the books let him tell it with a soldier's economy: a big lowborn boy, one mouth too many, sold by his family to the bearded priests, who burned the axe into his chest and trained him from childhood to the great weapon of his order — a longaxe with a shaft of mountain ash, six feet of it, which he has carried, cleaned and slept beside for most of a lifetime. When a Norvoshi noblewoman named Mellario wed the Prince of Dorne, Hotah was her wedding escort; the marriage frayed and the lady sailed home, but the captain stayed, passed from her service to her husband's, and has stood at Doran Martell's shoulder ever since. Serve, protect, obey — he recites the trinity like a heartbeat, and means it.

The books make him their Dornish window: the point-of-view man in the corner of every scene that matters at Sunspear, watching the Sand Snakes rage and Arianne scheme and his prince endure, missing nothing and volunteering nothing. He is the saga's purest study of the sworn instrument — a man who has outsourced every choice to his oath and found something like peace there — and his quiet dread runs under all his chapters: that the day is always coming when the prince will say the word, and the longaxe will have to fall on someone the captain likes. When that day comes at the Greenblood, the axe does not hesitate. Hotah does, privately, forever.

The page and the screen

The gulf between the tellings is nearly total. Book Hotah is a narrator — three books of Dornish politics arrive through his watchful, wintry voice — and a survivor: alive in the published pages, dispatched with Obara Sand to hunt the rogue knight Darkstar, his competence never once caught napping. The show reduced him to a bodyguard with two scenes and a fatal case of standing in the wrong spot, his vigilance — the books' defining trait, the man who watches everyone — overturned in a single edit for the coup's convenience. Even his weapon shrank in translation. Book readers regard his show death less as an adaptation than as a clerical error.

The show: Fell at Sunspear (show); watches on in the books (S6·E1)

Prince Doran's captain, who missed nothing for decades, was taken from behind with a dagger in the back by Tyene Sand in the very moment his prince was murdered — too quick even to lower his longaxe.

In the show he dies in the same breath as his prince, which is at least thematically exact. As Ellaria Sand drives her dagger into Doran in the Water Gardens, Tyene Sand — smiling, close, family — slips her blade into Hotah's back before the longaxe can so much as turn: the man whose whole life was watching, taken from the one angle a lifetime of service had taught him not to guard. He falls beside the chair he defended, the coup complete around him. In the published books Areo Hotah is alive and entirely unambushed — riding out from Sunspear on his prince's orders, axe across his saddle, with the books' Dorne still turning around the things he has seen and kept to himself.
