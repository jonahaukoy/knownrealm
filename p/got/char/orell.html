Orell

The Free Folk

A wildling skinchanger who wears the skin of an eagle, scouting the lands south of the Wall for Mance's host.

The fuller tale

Orell is a skinchanger — one of the free folk’s wargs, born with the old blood that lets a man slip his skin and ride a beast’s — and his second self is an eagle. That choice of bird tells you the man: not a wolf’s pack-loyalty or a hound’s devotion but a raptor’s cold altitude, watching everything, belonging to nothing. In Mance Rayder’s host he serves as the eyes of the war, his eagle riding the winds over the Wall and the Gift, counting crows and mapping the kill.

In the wildling column he is Jon Snow’s most persistent doubter — with reason, as events prove. Orell reads the turncloak the way his eagle reads ground: he sees the crow under the sheepskin, says so early and often, and finds himself overruled by Ygritte’s heart and Mance’s gamble. The show sharpens the triangle by giving Orell his own designs on Ygritte, so that his hatred of Jon runs on two rails at once — the spy he distrusts and the rival he resents — and lets him force the issue at the climb, cutting the rope line and leaving Jon and Ygritte to fall, which they conspicuously decline to do.

Orell’s deeper function is to carry the tale’s warg lore into the wildling arc: through him the story establishes what skinchanging is, what it costs, and what the free folk believe waits at the end of it — that a warg who dies in his man’s body lives on inside the beast, for a while, as a fading passenger. It is a doctrine the story states almost casually over a campfire and then spends, with terrible interest, elsewhere.

The page and the screen

Timing is the great divergence. Book-Orell is dead before the reader ever properly meets him — killed by Jon Snow in the fight in the Skirling Pass in the second book — and exists thereafter as a legacy: his eagle, inherited in part by the warg Varamyr Sixskins, hates Jon with a dead man’s hatred and marks him for the host. The show resurrected him into a full speaking role — a season of scouting, suspicion and rivalry with a face and a name — essentially dramatizing in person the grudge the books conduct through a bird. His campfire warg-lore likewise absorbs material the books assign to Varamyr’s prologue. One detail survives every version: an eagle with a man inside it, circling Jon Snow, wanting him dead.

Their fate — if death has found them, it is written here. Open at your peril.

The show: Killed at the Queenscrown; his eagle flew on (S3·E9)

The books: Slain in the Skirling Pass (ACOK, ch. 68)

The eagle-warg fought Jon Snow at the Queenscrown tower when the raiding party turned on him. Jon's sword ended the man; his last act was to fly his eagle at Jon's face and mark him for life.

Both tellings kill the man by Jon Snow’s hand and let the bird carry the grudge. In the books he dies in the Skirling Pass skirmish, cut down by Longclaw before Jon is ever taken into the host — and his eagle flies on with a remnant of Orell riding it, screaming over every battlefield of the wildling war until (the fifth book’s prologue reports) Melisandre’s fires burn it out of the sky beneath the Wall, killing him a second time and searing the warg who had taken up the reins. The show stages his death at the Queenscrown: when Jon refuses the Thenns’ test and the raiding party turns on him, Orell attacks and dies on Jon’s sword — slipping into his eagle as he falls, and spending his last seconds of manhood raking Jon’s face with the bird’s talons, the scars of which Jon carries for the rest of the tale. Skinchangers die twice, the free folk say; Orell manages to make both count.
