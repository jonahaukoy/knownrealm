Podrick Payne

A shy, tongue-tied young squire of distant Lannister blood, quietly braver and more capable than anyone gives him credit for.

A soul born at The Westerlands.

The fuller tale

Season Two

Assigned to Tyrion as squire by Tywin, who means it as an insult — a Payne of the disgraced, landless branch of the family, about as far from an honor as the name can manage. He turns out to be quietly, almost accidentally excellent at the job: attentive, unbothered by Tyrion's tongue, and present with a spear at exactly the moment a Lannister knight is about to finish off his master on the riverbank during the Battle of the Blackwater. He never tells anyone how he did it, which somehow makes it more impressive rather than less.

Season Three

Tyrion tries to reward him for Blackwater with a night — several nights — with the finest brothel King's Landing has to offer, and is baffled to hear the girls refuse payment afterward, unable to get a straight explanation out of Podrick as to what exactly he did to earn that. He keeps close through Tyrion's forced betrothal to Sansa Stark and the wedding that follows, pouring wine and saying nothing, which by now everyone understands is simply how he shows up for people.

Season Four

At Joffrey's wedding when the king dies choking at his own feast, and one of the few people who still wants to help Tyrion once the whole capital decides he did it. He digs for witnesses to build a defense and comes back with black eyes and no answers, having clearly been warned off by people with more power than he has. Tyrion, out of gratitude and dread in roughly equal measure, refuses to let him testify or dig further and sends him out of King's Landing entirely with nothing but a letter and a horse, buying his safety by putting him beyond anyone's reach.

Season Five

Squire to Brienne of Tarth now, passed into her care on Tyrion's word, an odd pairing by every outward measure that nonetheless works. They track Sansa to the woods outside Winterfell and keep a cold, patient watch for the one signal Brienne was promised — a candle lit in the highest window — while Podrick spends the season mostly cooking, keeping watch, and getting steadily better with a sword under Brienne's repetitive, unglamorous instruction. The candle finally appears, but only after Brienne has ridden off to deal with a dying Stannis, and they miss their chance to reach Sansa that year.

Season Six

At Riverrun with Brienne, sent to talk the Blackfish into lending his remaining men to Sansa's cause, before the negotiations run into a complication neither of them expected: Jaime Lannister, commanding the besieging army on the other side of the walls. Podrick watches the two of them work through a history he only half understands and gets nothing for the effort — the Blackfish refuses to bend, and Brienne and Pod leave by boat before the castle falls, still bound for Winterfell and Sansa with empty hands and Jaime's continued goodwill instead.

Season Seven

Drilling Winterfell's green recruits at Brienne's side, taking hit after hit from men twice his size with a patience that slowly starts looking like its own kind of skill, while Tormund Giantsbane hangs around the training yard making no secret of who he's really there to watch. Podrick travels south with Brienne as part of the party bringing Jon and Tyrion's fragile truce to King's Landing, one more sworn sword standing quietly at the edge of a room where dragons and queens are doing all the talking.

Season Eight

Sings 'Jenny of Oldstones' around the fire the night before the dead arrive at Winterfell, one small unguarded moment from someone who has spent years being nothing but reliable, and then survives the battle itself to be teased about the singing afterward. He remains at Brienne's side through the last uneasy months of the war, one more quiet constant in a story that has, by now, killed off almost everyone flashier than he is.

How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Where the page and the screen part

Book Pod is younger, smaller and still mid-journey. The novels keep his Blackwater heroics and his refusal to abandon condemned masters — he follows Brienne uninvited across the Riverlands because Ser Jaime's lady is his best road back to the lord he lost — but his arc through the published pages ends in genuine peril: taken with Brienne by the Brotherhood Without Banners in its merciless late incarnation, he is under a noose in her final chapters, and his survival hangs on the word Brienne shouts as the rope draws — a cliffhanger the books have never resolved on the page, though the story's grammar and Brienne's later movements imply the boy was cut down in time. The show, having no patience for ambiguity about its most likable squire, carries him clear of the Riverlands entirely — no Brotherhood, no noose — and invents his whole later flowering: the training montages, the song, the white cloak. The famous brothel mystery is the show's too; book Pod's talents remain, as far as the record runs, strictly military.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
