Where the page and the screen part

The show reduced him to a background face and reassigned his knife. On the page it is Bowen Marsh, weeping, who leads the officers of the Watch in the assassination of Jon Snow — the fifth book’s final convulsion, driven by the stewards and the old guard, men whose grievances are institutional rather than personal. The screen gave that conspiracy to Alliser Thorne’s bitterness and Olly’s grief, faces the audience knew, and left Marsh unnamed in the crowd. The change trades the books’ chillier point — that Jon is killed not by his enemies but by the Watch’s reasonable middle — for a more legible betrayal. Marsh has no death in either telling: he is alive in the published books, holding Castle Black in the aftermath of what he did, with Wun Wun’s roars and the free folk’s numbers arrayed against the arithmetic of his survival.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
