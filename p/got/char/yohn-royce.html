How it ends for them

The chronicle records no death for this soul — they are living when the tale leaves them, or their ending is a page the maesters have yet to turn.

Their chapters, book by book

Where the page and the screen part

Show and book Royce are the same man at different altitudes. The show makes him Sansa’s gruff marshal and gives him the on-screen victories; the books make him the great lord of the Vale’s opposition, whose Lords Declarant storyline — six proud banners against one smiling upstart — is among the threads the adaptation cut entirely. The books also draw the family lines the show leaves unspoken: the doomed ranger of the saga’s first page is Bronze Yohn’s own son, which means the story’s very first casualty was a Royce of Runestone, and his father never learns how it happened.

Their journey

Where the two tellings send them to the same place the road runs whole; where they part, the screen keeps the left and the books the right.
