S4·E8 — The Mountain and the Viper

Tommen I Baratheon holds the throne Hand: Lord Tywin Lannister

The hour retold

The duel the season promised, staged in sunlight with the whole capital watching. Oberyn Martell fights the way he talks — spinning, showboating, gorgeous — and the spear does its work: the Mountain armored and enormous, cut down by degrees, flat on his back with Dornish steel in his chest. It should end there. But Oberyn did not come for a verdict; he came for a confession, and he circles the dying giant demanding the words — say her name, say you killed her, tell me who gave the order — because a win that leaves Tywin unindicted is no win at all. Elia Martell’s name in the arena air is worth more to him than Tyrion’s life or his own, and the Mountain, granted one last moment of lucidity, gives him both answers and death: a sweep of the leg, thumbs in his eyes, the confession roared as a boast while he crushes the prince’s skull with his hands.

It is the show’s cruelest structural joke — a trial by combat decided after the combat was over, a champion killed by his own need for the truth — and the episode is honest about the cost radiating outward: Ellaria’s scream, Tyrion condemned to death by his father from the box, and in Dorne, though the season only gestures at it, a war being conceived. Around the arena the hour arranges its quieter judgments. Sansa, at the Eyrie’s inquest, lies for Littlefinger with a performance that saves him and banks him — then descends the stairs transformed, a girl done waiting for rescuers. Jorah Mormont’s old sin surfaces in Meereen by way of a royal pardon with Robert’s signature, and Daenerys exiles the man who loves her most rather than hear the case for forgiveness.

The title’s pairing is exact: the Mountain and the Viper, force and finesse, and the episode’s verdict is that finesse dies when it stops to gloat — but also that force wins nothing either, since Gregor Clegane leaves the arena poisoned on the spear’s edge, a detail the capital will learn slowly and regret at leisure. Moments before the duel, the show files its saddest scene almost as an aside: Grey Wind’s— no, this hour it is the Hound and Arya arriving at the Bloody Gate to ransom her to an aunt three days dead, and Arya laughing — actually laughing — at the sheer, exhausted comedy of her luck. The gods of this story have a sense of humor. It is not a kind one.

What happens

  • Mole's Town falls to the wildlings; Ygritte spares Gilly and the babe.
  • Ser Barristan hands Jorah a royal pardon signed by Robert Baratheon: the spying is out; Dany exiles him.
  • Sansa lies for Littlefinger at the inquest — and descends the Eyrie's stairs a different creature.
  • The Viper dances, wins, demands a confession — and the Mountain's thumbs answer for Elia Martell.
