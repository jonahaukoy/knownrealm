S4·E4 — Oathkeeper

Tommen I Baratheon holds the throne Hand: Lord Tywin Lannister

The hour retold

An episode named for a sword, organized around what oaths are worth. Jaime — freshly returned, publicly useless, and privately reassembling himself — gives Brienne the Valyrian blade his father forged from Ned Stark’s greatsword, plus armor, plus Podrick, and a commission that is really a conscience: find Sansa Stark, keep her safe. Brienne names the sword Oathkeeper, which is either irony or repair depending on the light — Stark steel, Lannister guilt, and the two most oath-burdened people in the realm dividing one promise between them. It is the season’s cleanest transaction: for once, something of value goes to someone who deserves it.

The rest of the hour is oath-breaking in ascending order. In Meereen, Daenerys answers the crucified children of the road with a hundred and sixty-three crucified masters and calls it justice while Barristan urges mercy — the first time her arithmetic openly frightens her own council. At Craster’s Keep, the Watch’s mutineers drink from the Old Bear’s skull, having broken every vow the order owns, and Bran’s party blunders into their hands. And in the far north, the episode quietly detonates its biggest reveal: a White Walker carries Craster’s last son to an altar of ice, and a crowned figure lays a nail of a finger on the child’s cheek — blue eyes opening on a transformation the books had only whispered about. The dead are not just rising; they are being made. Somebody, it turns out, has been keeping the oldest bargain in the story all along.

What happens

  • Meereen rises from within; the Great Masters are nailed up as answer to the children on the road.
  • Jaime gives Brienne his Valyrian sword and a quest: find Sansa, keep her safe. She names it Oathkeeper.
  • The Night's Watch mutineers at Craster's drink from the Old Bear's skull; Bran's party stumbles into them.
  • Beyond it all, a White Walker carries Craster's last son to a cold altar — and blue eyes open.
