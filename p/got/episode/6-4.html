S6·E4 — Book of the Stranger

Tommen I Baratheon holds the throne Hand: Ser Kevan Lannister

The hour retold

The season’s first genuine reunion arrives without fanfare: Sansa Stark reaches Castle Black and finds her half-brother alive against every reasonable expectation, the first time two Stark children have stood in the same room since Winterfell burned. What follows is not a triumphant homecoming so much as two survivors comparing wounds — Sansa is done running, Jon is tired of fighting other people’s wars, and Ramsay’s letter, demanding her return and threatening Rickon’s life in the same breath, is what finally moves Jon from reluctance to resolve. Littlefinger, meanwhile, slides back into the Vale unbothered by any of it and aims Robin Arryn’s knights north, setting up an intervention no one at Castle Black knows is coming.

In Vaes Dothrak, Daenerys turns a death sentence into a coronation. Sentenced to the temple of the dosh khaleen for the crime of widowhood, she instead locks the khals in with the burning braziers and walks out of the flames unburnt for the second time in her life, and the surviving Dothraki — faced with a choice between a dead god and a living one — kneel by morning. It is the show’s cleanest demonstration of how Daenerys actually accumulates power: not by asking, but by making refusal look like a worse bet than surrender. On Pyke, Theon returns from his long ordeal not to reclaim a crown of his own but to kneel for his sister’s, the season’s quietest and most complete character reversal, finally putting someone else’s survival ahead of his own ambition.

What happens

  • Sansa reaches Castle Black: the first Stark reunion since Winterfell burned.
  • Ramsay's letter demands Sansa and threatens Rickon; Jon reads it and stops saying no.
  • Littlefinger returns to the Vale and aims its knights north.
  • In Vaes Dothrak the khals debate Dany's fate; she burns the temple with them in it and walks out unburnt. Again.
