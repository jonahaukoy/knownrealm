S1·E3 — Lord Snow

Robert I Baratheon holds the throne Hand: Lord Eddard Stark

The hour retold

Everyone arrives where they were going, and everywhere turns out to be worse than advertised. Ned reaches King’s Landing to find the crown six million in debt — half of it to the father of the queen — and a small council where the whisperer, the moneylender and the old maester all smile with too many teeth. At Castle Black, Jon expects the storied brotherhood of the songs and gets thieves, rapers and grey old men holding seven hundred feet of ice; his sulk about it lasts precisely until Tyrion points out that sneering at broken men is a luxury for boys raised in castles. The episode is a matched pair of disillusionments — the realm’s two great institutions, throne and Watch, both running on fumes and reputation.

The quiet scenes carry the freight. Catelyn, smuggled into a brothel by Littlefinger, leaves the capital with a name for the dagger — Tyrion Lannister — which happens to be a lie that will start a war. Old Nan tells a broken boy about the Long Night, when the White Walkers came the first time, and the show files the story away in plain sight. And in a sunlit room, a Braavosi fencing master takes a small girl through her first lesson while her father watches, smiling — until the wooden swords start sounding, to Ned’s ears, like steel. Syrio’s water dance is the one gift Ned gives that outlives everything: swift as a deer, quiet as a shadow, and not knitting.

What happens

  • Ned reaches King's Landing and finds the crown six million in debt — half of it to Tywin Lannister.
  • Catelyn arrives in secret; Littlefinger tells her the assassin's dagger belonged to Tyrion Lannister.
  • At Castle Black, Jon learns the Watch is thieves and poachers — and starts teaching instead of beating them.
  • Arya begins lessons with Syrio Forel, First Sword of Braavos: not knitting.
  • Old Nan tells Bran of the Long Night, when the White Walkers came the first time.
