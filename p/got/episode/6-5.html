S6·E5 — The Door

Tommen I Baratheon holds the throne Hand: Ser Kevan Lannister

The hour retold

Sansa spends her return to the North settling a score she has been carrying since her wedding, confronting Littlefinger with the plain cost of the bargain he sold her into — not gratitude for the rescue but an accounting of what the rescue required first. On Pyke, the kingsmoot crowns Euron Greyjoy over his niece by acclaim and violence in equal measure, and Yara and Theon, refusing to serve under an uncle who murdered their father, answer the vote by stealing the best ships of the Iron Fleet in the dead of night — a coup answered by a heist, in the ironborn style.

Beneath a weirwood far north, Bran’s education finally outstrips its own safety net: left alone for a moment too long, he reaches for the Night King in a vision and the Night King reaches back, and the cave’s ancient wards fail all at once. What follows is the show at its bluntest about cost — Summer dies buying seconds, the Three-Eyed Raven dies after a thousand years of watching, the last of the children of the forest dies undoing what her own kind once made — and at the center of the collapse, Hodor holds a door against the entire onslaught while a vision from decades earlier reveals, in a single devastating stroke, exactly why he has only ever been able to say that one word. Hold the door, warped by a boy’s desperate warg across time into a single collapsing syllable, is the show’s cruelest use of its own magic: a tragedy engineered, unknowingly, by the person it destroys.

What happens

  • Sansa faces Littlefinger and names what his marriage bargain cost her.
  • The kingsmoot crowns Euron; Yara and Theon steal the best ships of the Iron Fleet the same night.
  • Bran, unsupervised, is touched by the Night King in a vision — and the cave's wards fail.
  • The dead pour in. Summer dies, the Raven dies, Leaf dies — and Hodor holds the door as a boy's whole life collapses into one word.
