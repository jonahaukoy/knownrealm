S2·E8 — The Prince of Winterfell

Joffrey I Baratheon holds the throne Hand: Tyrion Lannister, acting Hand

The hour retold

The held breath before the green fire. Stannis’s fleet is at sea, and King’s Landing prepares in its characteristic way: Joffrey plans heroics, Cersei plans hostages, and Tyrion counts wildfire jars and chain links with Bronn, conducting the defense of the city out of a workshop and a brothel. The episode’s craft is in the countdown — every scene in the capital is timed against the sails on the horizon, and the only man doing his job is the one the city will never thank.

The rest of the map is a study in prices. Yara arrives at Winterfell with ironborn sense — the castle is indefensible, the sea is home, come away — and Theon refuses, because coming away would mean the boys died for nothing, which they did. Robb, learning his mother has freed the Kingslayer on her own authority to ransom the girls, puts Catelyn under guard while Brienne rows Jaime downriver on the war’s least promising diplomatic mission. Arya spends Jaqen’s last owed death not on a name but on an exit, and walks out Harrenhal’s front gate past dead guards — a man keeps his word, precisely. And beneath Winterfell, the episode plays its gentlest card: Bran and Rickon never fled at all, and are hiding in the crypts below Theon’s feet, closer to home than anyone dares be.

What happens

  • Yara comes for Theon with ironborn sense: the castle is indefensible, come home. He refuses.
  • Stannis's fleet sails; Tyrion and Bronn count wildfire jars and chain links.
  • Robb learns his mother freed the Kingslayer and puts her under guard; Brienne rows Jaime south.
  • Arya spends her last death buying an exit: Jaqen empties Harrenhal's gate instead.
  • Bran and Rickon never left: they're in the crypts below Theon's feet.
