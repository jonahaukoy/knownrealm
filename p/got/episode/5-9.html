S5·E9 — The Dance of Dragons

Tommen I Baratheon holds the throne Hand: Vacant

The hour retold

Stannis Baratheon’s march on Winterfell was always a wager against the season, and the hour opens with the bill coming due: twenty of Ramsay’s riders slip past his pickets in the night and burn his baggage train to ash, leaving the self-declared king stranded in the snow with a starving host and no way forward or back. It is the least glamorous kind of military disaster — not a battle lost but a campaign strangled by weather and logistics — and it strips Stannis down to the one resource he has always trusted more than food or men: his certainty that the red god wants him on the throne.

What that certainty costs is the hour’s darkest passage and one of the show’s most argued-over scenes. Melisandre convinces Stannis that Shireen’s king’s blood can melt the snow the way Renly’s and Robb’s and Joffrey’s once seemed to end their claims, and the show refuses to look away from what follows — a father walking his daughter to the pyre while she screams his name, believing until the very last second that this, too, is a kind of duty. It is the point at which Stannis stops being a rigid but honorable man and becomes something the audience cannot forgive, and the show is exact about making sure no music, no framing, softens the murder into anything other than what it is.

Half a world away, in the golden light of Daznak’s Pit, the season stages its other extreme — wonder instead of horror, arriving in the same hour as a corrective. The Sons of the Harpy spring their ambush during the fighting-pit games, cutting down soldiers and nobles alike in a locked arena, and just as the trap closes on Daenerys herself, Drogon answers a mother’s scream for the first time since Astapor, landing in a burst of fire and carrying her up and out over the rooftops of Meereen — the first Targaryen to fly in a hundred years, escaping a massacre on the same night her rival immolates a child for the exact opposite reason. The episode’s cruelty and its awe are not opposites; they are the same coin, the season’s recurring argument about what fire and blood actually buy.

What happens

  • Ramsay's twenty men burn Stannis's camp; the march to Winterfell strands in snow and hunger.
  • Stannis burns Shireen. The princess who taught men to read screams her father's name.
  • Arya sees Meryn Trant in Braavos and abandons the thin man for an older prayer.
  • In Daznak's Pit the Harpies spring their trap — and Drogon answers his mother's scream. She flies.
