S5·E10 — Mother's Mercy

Tommen I Baratheon holds the throne Hand: Vacant

The hour retold

Winter finishes what the burning started. Stannis’s host deserts him overnight — soldiers do not forgive what he did to buy good weather — and Brienne of Tarth finds him alone against a tree with nothing left to command, and delivers, at last, the sentence she swore years ago in a tent at Storm’s End for a king she loved and lost to a shadow with his own brother’s face. It is one of the season’s few deaths that plays as satisfaction rather than tragedy, an oath honored on schedule after two seasons of patient waiting, and the show grants it almost no ceremony — a sword, a nod, and the snow closing back over the story of a rightful heir undone by his own certainty.

In King’s Landing, the season’s cruelest engine of comeuppance finishes its work on the queen who built it: Cersei confesses to a fraction of her sins, is shorn in a cell, and walks the mile from the sept to the Red Keep naked, pelted with filth while the city she has spent years terrorizing chants Shame at her like a bell tolling. The sequence is filmed with an unflinching, almost clinical patience — the camera studies Cersei’s face as much as the crowd’s cruelty — because the show wants the audience to feel two contradictory things at once: that she has earned every step, and that watching a person broken in public, regardless of who they are, is its own kind of horror. At Winterfell, in the episode’s gentler mirror image, Sansa and Theon choose the jump off the battlements into the snow together rather than one more night under Ramsay’s roof — a small, cold, hopeful ending stitched into the same hour as Cersei’s public ruin.

The season closes on two falls that are really the same fall told twice. Arya, given Meryn Trant at last, takes his eyes and his life in a Braavosi brothel — vengeance she was explicitly forbidden — and the Many-Faced God’s house answers by taking her sight, the price for stealing a debt that belonged to no one. And at Castle Black, Jon Snow’s mercy toward the free folk, the very choice that made him a leader worth following, gets him a dagger from a boy he saved and then blades from officers who never forgave him for it — For the Watch, they say, and Olly’s knife goes in last and worst. The season began by asking what fills a vacuum of power; it ends by demonstrating that doing the right thing, in this story, has never once been the same as doing the safe one — a lesson every major character in the finale learns at exactly the same, unbearable moment.

What happens

  • Stannis's host deserts; Brienne finally gets her sentence — and Ramsay's men get the field.
  • Sansa and Theon jump from Winterfell's walls into the snow, hand in hand.
  • Arya takes Meryn Trant's eyes and life; the Faceless Men take her sight for it.
  • Cersei confesses, is shorn, and walks the city naked to the bells of 'Shame'.
  • "For the Watch." Olly's knife is the last of many; Jon Snow bleeds out in the snow.
