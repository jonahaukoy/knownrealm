S6·E3 — Oathbreaker

Tommen I Baratheon holds the throne Hand: Ser Kevan Lannister

The hour retold

Jon Snow’s return comes with an immediate, brutal piece of housekeeping: he hangs the mutineers who murdered him, Olly last of all, and then does something none of his predecessors ever managed — walks away from the job entirely. Death released him from his vows, he reasons, with the precise, lawyerly logic of a man who has just been given the rarest gift in Westeros and intends to use it; my watch is ended lands as both liberation and a quiet renunciation of everything the Watch asked of him. Beyond the Wall, Bran’s visions carry him to the Tower of Joy for the first time, watching a younger Ned Stark tell a story about how a fight was won that the vision itself is already contradicting — the show’s most patient long game finally showing its hand, one careful frame at a time.

At Winterfell, the Umbers arrive bearing gifts calculated to prove loyalty to the wrong side: Osha the wildling, a captured Rickon Stark, and the head of his direwolf, handed over to Ramsay Bolton as tribute — the last free Stark child delivered, without ceremony, into the household that broke his family. In Braavos, Arya’s long punishment ends the moment she stops needing an ending: beaten and blind for weeks, she finally answers a girl has no name and means it completely, and the fountain water she drinks afterward restores her sight on precisely the day the show has decided she no longer requires it to prove anything.

What happens

  • Jon hangs the four mutineers — Olly last — then lays down his cloak: "My watch is ended."
  • Bran watches young Ned at the Tower of Joy and hears his father lie about how the fight was won.
  • Umbers deliver a gift to Ramsay: Osha, Rickon Stark, and the head of his direwolf.
  • Arya, blind and beaten, finally answers "a girl has no name" like she means it — and drinks from the fountain.
