Craster's Keep

A wildling's hall beyond the Wall

The daubed hall of Craster, a free folk man who keeps his daughters as wives and stays on cold terms with harder things.

The fuller tale

Craster’s Keep is a midden heap with a hall on top — one smoky longhouse behind a dirt wall, north of the Wall and outside every law but its owner’s. Craster styles himself a friend to the Watch, and the rangers use his roof and hate themselves for it, because the price of his hospitality is agreeing not to notice things: the daughters he marries, the wives who are his daughters, and the sons who are never, ever seen. The keep is the story’s first lesson that some bargains keep you alive and cost you everything else.

The truth of the missing sons is the tale’s coldest secret — left in the woods for the things in the dark, a tithe paid to powers the Watch has stopped believing in. And it is under Craster’s roof that the Watch itself breaks: the survivors of the Fist, starving and mutinous, turn on their own Lord Commander, and the Old Bear dies in a pigsty of a hall at the hands of his sworn brothers. Gilly — one daughter-wife, one unsanctioned son — walks out of that ruin into the story’s keeping, which is more escape than the place ever allowed anyone before.

The page and the screen

The show returns to the keep for an invented chapter: the mutineers linger there under Karl Tanner, Bran’s party is briefly caught, and Jon leads a raid from Castle Black to put the traitors down. In the books nobody goes back — the mutineers scatter or rot where they sit, and Craster’s hall simply drops out of the world, which is somehow the bleaker ending.
