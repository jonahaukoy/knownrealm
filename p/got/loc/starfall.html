Starfall

Seat of House Dayne

The Daynes' seat on the Torentine, home of the sword Dawn and the memory of Ser Arthur, the Sword of the Morning.

The fuller tale

Starfall stands where the Torentine meets the Summer Sea in Dorne's far west, a pale castle on an island at the river's mouth, and House Dayne has held it since the Dawn Age ÔÇö which at Starfall is not a figure of speech. The family treasure is Dawn, a greatsword pale as milkglass, forged (the tale insists, and for once no maester argues) from the heart of a fallen star; and the house's peculiar honor is that the blade is not the lord's by right. Dawn waits, sometimes for generations, until a knight of the house is judged worthy of it, and only then is he named the Sword of the Morning ÔÇö the realm's one title that must be earned twice, by blood and by soul.

The tale's shadow over Starfall is the last man to bear it. Ser Arthur Dayne was the finest knight of Aerys's Kingsguard and, by the accounting of the man who killed him, the finest sword he ever faced; he died at a tower in the Red Mountains defending a dying king's secret, and Ned Stark ÔÇö bleeding, grieving, and owing his life to a crannogman's knife in the back of the duel ÔÇö rode to Starfall afterward to return Dawn to the family. What else passed at Starfall that day the books keep folded: Lady Ashara Dayne, beauty of a generation and a name entangled with the Starks at Harrenhal's great tourney, threw herself into the sea from the castle's seaward tower, and the hows and whys of it are among the saga's most carefully hoarded silences. The sword came home; the Sword of the Morning has not been named since; and Starfall keeps its griefs the way it keeps Dawn ÔÇö waiting for someone worthy of them.

The house endures in the tale's margins with a poignancy few great houses manage. Its young lord, another Ned, squires in the riverlands for the lightning lord's broken men and speaks of milk-brothers and old loyalties that hint how tangled the Dayne and Stark stories truly are; a cousin called Darkstar carries the family's edge without its honor through the books' Dornish intrigues. Starfall itself never hosts a battle or a wedding on the page ÔÇö it does not need to. It is the tale's chapel of the old chivalry: the place where the best knight came from, where his sword sleeps, and where the story keeps its unpaid debts to the age of honor filed under a falling star.

The page and the screen

The show never sails to Starfall; House Dayne exists on screen only as Ser Arthur at the tower ÔÇö famously restaged with a sword in each hand, where book readers expected the pale greatsword Dawn, an omission partisans still relitigate. The books keep the house threaded through everything: Edric Dayne's quiet revelations about Wylla and the Stark connection, Ashara's death as an open mystery with rival explanations, Darkstar loose in the Dornish plot, and Dawn itself waiting at Starfall ÔÇö an unclaimed sword that readers, noting what the blade is made of and what is coming from the north, refuse to believe the author has finished with.
