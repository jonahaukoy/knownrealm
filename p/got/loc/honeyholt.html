Honeyholt

Seat of House Beesbury

The Beesburys' seat amid orchards and hives on the Honeywine.

The fuller tale

Honeyholt drowses on the Honeywine amid hives and orchards, and House Beesbury has kept it — and the beekeeping that named them both — for longer than most royal lines lasted. Theirs is the gentlest wealth in Westeros: honey, mead, wax for the septs, and a seat close enough to Oldtown to marry into learning as often as land. The family's most famous son was an old man at a council table — master of coin to a dying king, the one voice raised for law and a girl's inheritance on the morning the greens stole a crown, and the first casualty of the Dance of Dragons before it had a name. The accounts disagree on exactly how he died in that chamber; they agree no sword was in his hand. Honeyholt has kept bees and counted honestly ever since, which is perhaps its own kind of memorial.
