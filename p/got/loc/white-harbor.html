White Harbor

City of House Manderly

The North's only true city, a clean white port at the mouth of the White Knife, held by House Manderly for the Starks.

The fuller tale

White Harbor is the North’s only true city, and it looks like nowhere else in the North — whitewashed walls, clean cobbled streets, a hill crowned by the New Castle and a sept as well as a godswood, because the Manderlys brought the Seven with them when they came. And they did come: driven from the Reach a thousand years ago, the Manderlys were taken in by the Starks and given the mouth of the White Knife, and have spent ten centuries proving that adopted loyalty can outlast the born kind. Their mermen banners, their fat purses and their genuine devotion make them the strangest thing in the North — southern in manner, northern to the bone.

Lord Wyman Manderly is the war’s great performance artist. Too fat to sit a horse, mocked at every court, he plays the craven collaborator — feasting Freys, mouthing loyalty to the Boltons — while running the North’s quietest counter-conspiracy: ships built in secret, a smuggler knight sent hunting a lost Stark heir, and a vengeance served, if the darkest reading of his wedding pies is right, at the Boltons’ own table. His whispered creed to a man he pretends to condemn — the North remembers — becomes the watchword of everything that follows.

In the story’s architecture White Harbor is the proof that the Stark compact was never about fear. The Boltons can seize Winterfell; they cannot seize this — a thousand years of chosen loyalty, compounding interest in a city the wolves once gave to strangers. When the North rises, it rises because places like White Harbor decided the debt was still owed.

The page and the screen

The city is effectively a books-only setting. Davos’s embassy to White Harbor — the mocking court, the false execution with a criminal’s head shown in his place, the secret charge to find Rickon Stark on Skagos — is one of the saga’s best late arcs, and the show omits the city, the mission and very nearly the Manderlys themselves, reducing ten centuries of merman loyalty to a banner in a crowd scene.

The chronicle here

  • The onion knight comes ashore at Sisterton in a smuggler's boots; Lord Borrell serves him sister's stew, old grudges, and a warning about White Harbor. (ADWD 10)
  • In the Merman's Court Lord Wyman Manderly condemns Davos loudly for the Freys' benefit; the head that goes up over White Harbor's gates belonged to a criminal with convenient hands. (ADWD 16)
  • A dead man dines well in the Wolf's Den: the head on White Harbor's spike wore another face, and Lord Manderly's grief has been playing a longer game. (ADWD 20)
  • Manderly's price for White Harbor's knights is a smuggler's errand: Rickon Stark lives, on an island of cannibals and unicorns — bring the boy home, and the North rises. (ADWD 30)
