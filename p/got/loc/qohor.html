Qohor

City of sorcerers

The Free City at the edge of its great forest, famed for dark arts and smiths who can rework Valyrian steel.

The fuller tale

Qohor sits at the western edge of the vast forest that bears its name, a dark-stoned city grown rich on timber, furs and the caravan trade — the last civilized stop before the long road east to Vaes Dothrak. Its reputation runs darker than its ledgers. The city worships the Black Goat, whose altars take blood daily — calves and horses on ordinary days, condemned men on holy ones — and its name is a byword across Essos for sorcery, necromancy and arts best not enumerated. What is certain is the skill of its forges: the smiths of Qohor alone remember how to rework Valyrian steel without ruining it, a craft they guard so jealously that men whisper the secret involves the Black Goat’s favorite currency. Tobho Mott, the master armorer of King’s Landing who reforges Ice into two Lannister blades, learned his trade there — the Qohorik arts, it turns out, reach even into the story’s most bitter inheritance.

Qohor’s great hour belongs to history, and the story retells it every time the Unsullied are sold. Four centuries past, with its own cavalry annihilated, the city hired three thousand Unsullied to face a khalasar fifty thousand strong. Eighteen times the Dothraki charged; eighteen times they broke on the spears; and when barely six hundred eunuchs still stood among the heaped horses, the surviving riders cut off their braids and threw them at the victors’ feet before turning away. The Three Thousand of Qohor are the sales pitch Daenerys hears in Astapor — proof of what obedience perfected can do, offered by men who never mention what it costs to manufacture.
