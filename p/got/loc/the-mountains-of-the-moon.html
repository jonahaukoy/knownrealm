The Mountains of the Moon

The wall around the Vale

The grey peaks that make the Vale a fortress and its hill tribes a menace — Stone Crows, Burned Men and Black Ears who bow to no lord and rob every traveler the Bloody Gate doesn't save.

The fuller tale

The Mountains of the Moon are the wall around the Vale — grey peaks that make Jon Arryn’s country a fortress with one door and give the high road from the riverlands its reputation as the most dangerous stretch of civilized Westeros. Civilized being the courtesy: the mountains belong to the clans — Stone Crows, Burned Men, Black Ears, Moon Brothers — descendants of the First Men the Andals drove uphill, who have spent six thousand years declining to acknowledge the arrangement and taxing the road in blood and boots.

The story’s best mountain chapter is Tyrion’s: captured on the high road, freed at the Eyrie, and promptly recruiting the clans on the way down — trading Lannister steel for Stone Crow swords and telling Shagga son of Dolf which parts to cut off. It is the tale in miniature: the despised outsider finding common cause with the people the realm pretends are scenery. The clans fight at the Green Fork for the Imp’s promise, and the mountains resume their long war with the Vale’s knights — a conflict older than the kingdom and, on current form, likelier to outlast it.
