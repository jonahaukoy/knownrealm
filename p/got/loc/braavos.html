Braavos

The Secret City

Greatest of the Free Cities, hidden in a fog-bound lagoon behind its Titan, ruled by the Sealord and famed for its water dancers.

The fuller tale

Braavos is the city slaves built to hide in. When ValyriaÔÇÖs ships swept the seas for rowers and miners, one fleet of captives rose, killed their masters, and sailed not for home ÔÇö every home was watched ÔÇö but for the end of the map: a fog-bound lagoon at the top of the narrow sea where the dragonlords would never think to look. There they raised a city of a hundred islets stitched together by bridges and canals, and kept its very existence secret for a century, trading through intermediaries while the mightiest empire in the world never learned it had misplaced several thousand of its possessions. Every other Free City is a daughter of Valyria; Braavos calls itself the bastard child, and wears the name with a bravoÔÇÖs grin.

The secret is long out, and the city now announces itself with the least subtle landmark on earth: the Titan, a fortress in the shape of a striding warrior, so vast that ships pass between its legs and its roar marks the hours. Beyond it lie the Purple Harbor for Braavosi keels and the RagmanÔÇÖs Harbor for everyone else, the SealordÔÇÖs palace with its menagerie, the temples of every god the founders carried into exile ÔÇö the Moonsingers foremost, for it was their prophecy that led the fleet to the lagoon ÔÇö and the damp alleys where bravos in flamboyant slashed finery duel over the honor of courtesans whose barges outshine the SealordÔÇÖs. Because the founders had all worn chains, the cityÔÇÖs first law endures: no slave may set foot in Braavos. It is the one Free City where the adjective is not a joke.

Two Braavosi institutions bend the whole story from a distance. The Iron Bank, grown from a hoard the founders locked in an iron mine, is the power that wars run on ÔÇö thrones borrow, thrones repay, and when a throne will not, the Iron Bank funds its replacement, a policy the Lannisters discover the way debtors usually discover things. Their services cost more than gold; what they sell is the most reliable commodity in the world.

The city reaches into the tale before anyone sails there. Braavos matters to the tale as its great counterexample. Every other city in the story was built by conquest and runs on someoneÔÇÖs servitude; the secret city was built by the conquered and runs on contracts, and it has quietly outlasted the empire that hunted its founders. The city of escaped slaves gave the last Targaryen her image of home; the story does not underline the irony, which is how you know it is intended.
