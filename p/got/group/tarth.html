House Tarth

The Evenstar's house on the Sapphire Isle off the stormlands coast — old and honorable, if no longer rich, and mother to one very singular daughter.

The fuller tale

House Tarth holds the Sapphire Isle off the stormlands coast, an old and honorable line long since past its wealthiest days, better known by the story's present for the singular daughter it produced than for anything else about its history. Brienne of Tarth grew up unusually tall, unusually plain by the court's own cruel standards, and unusually determined to be a knight in a world with no formal path for a woman to become one — mocked at tourneys, proposed to as a joke, and dismissed by nearly everyone she meets until they actually watch her fight.

Her career is a long series of oaths kept at real cost. Sworn first to Renly Baratheon and unable to save him from the shadow that kills him, she carries that failure into an oath to Catelyn Stark to protect her daughters, a promise Catelyn's own death does nothing to release her from — and Brienne spends years honoring it anyway, escorting an unlikely, one-handed companion across a burning countryside, fighting a bear pit barehanded rather than break faith, and eventually finding and protecting the Stark daughters she was never quite able to locate when it would have been easy.

What Brienne represents, more than any single deed, is the story's argument that chivalry's old language — honor, service, the sworn word — can still mean something even when the world that invented it has mostly stopped believing its own rules. She ends the tale as living proof of the argument: knighted at last, in the old way, by the very oathbreaker she spent years distrusting, and recognized, finally, by an institution that spent her whole life insisting she could never belong to it.

The page and the screen

The books leave Brienne's fate on a genuine cliffhanger the show resolves completely. Her final published chapter ends mid-action, captured by Lady Stoneheart's brotherhood and apparently forced to choose whether to bring Jaime Lannister to be hanged for oathbreaking — the chapter closes on a single shouted word, deliberately ambiguous, and readers have argued for years over what she actually decided. The show, having cut the Stoneheart storyline entirely, instead gives Brienne a clean, decisive arc: survival, a knighthood earned in full view, and an ending as the Kingsguard's Lord Commander in all but name, recording Jaime's deeds honestly in the White Book rather than being forced to end them.
