A Clash of Kings — Catelyn IV

In Renly's tent at dawn a shadow with Stannis's face slides through the king's gorget; Brienne is blamed, and Catelyn flees with her.

The chapter retold

In Renly’s tent at dawn, something slides through the king’s gorget that no blade should have been able to reach — a shadow wearing Stannis’s face, there and gone in an instant, and Renly Baratheon dies before anyone in the tent can raise an alarm. Brienne, closest to the body and holding a sword, is immediately and wrongly blamed for the murder she witnessed and could not prevent.

Catelyn, present in the tent, is the only other witness who saw what actually happened, and she flees with Brienne rather than let the disgraced knight hang for a magic she has no way to prove. The scene is the book’s starkest demonstration that Melisandre’s power is not theater — Renly’s death by shadow-assassination reshuffles the entire war overnight, dissolving the largest host in Westeros and scattering its lords in every direction, most of them toward Tywin Lannister or Stannis by default. Renly Baratheon, King of Summer, dies with the war barely begun and his crown never once tested in a real battle.
