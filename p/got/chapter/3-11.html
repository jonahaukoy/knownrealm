A Storm of Swords — Davos II

In the dark under Dragonstone, Davos hardens his purpose against king and god alike; Melisandre comes to the bars to explain her fires — and his.

The chapter retold

In the dark beneath Dragonstone, Davos Seaworth does not break so much as set. Denied light and mostly denied hope, he uses the isolation to harden a purpose that has nothing left to soften it — not loyalty to Stannis, not fear of Melisandre's god, just a plain intention to see the red woman answer for the sons the wildfire took. Melisandre comes to the bars herself, unbothered by his hatred, and offers something stranger than an apology: an explanation of what she actually sees in her fires, and why she believes every death she causes serves a purpose too large for Davos's grief to outweigh.

It is the first time the reader gets Melisandre's own account of her convictions rather than other characters' fear of her, and it is unsettling precisely because she is not lying — she believes every word, which makes her more dangerous than a cynic would be. Davos leaves the conversation no less determined to stop her, but with a clearer, colder picture of exactly what he is up against: not malice, but faith, which does not negotiate.
