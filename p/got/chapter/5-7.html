A Dance with Dragons — Quentyn I

Three Dornishmen bury a friend and take ship in Volantis under false names; Quentyn Martell carries a secret pact east toward a queen with dragons.

The chapter retold

Three young Dornishmen — Quentyn Martell and his companions Gerris Drinkwater and Archibald Yronwood — bury a friend lost to fever on the long road east and take ship out of Volantis under names that aren’t their own. Quentyn, Prince Doran’s heir apparent in all but the ordinary sense, is carrying a secret his father entrusted to almost no one: a marriage pact with Daenerys Targaryen, struck years before the dragons hatched, meant to bind Dorne to the Iron Throne through a queen instead of a king. He is a diffident, unglamorous young man asked to do a very glamorous thing, and the mismatch worries him more than the danger does.

The chapter quietly reintroduces Dorne’s long game — Doran Martell playing decades ahead of everyone else at the table, sacrificing his own son’s comfort, and possibly his life, on a bet placed before Robert’s Rebellion’s ashes had even cooled. Quentyn’s road toward Daenerys is framed from the start as a mismatch between plan and person: everything about the scheme is clever except the man sent to close it, who has spent his whole life being second choice and knows it. Nothing about this errand is going to be simple, and the book lets the reader feel that dread a full book before it matters.
