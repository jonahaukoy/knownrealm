A Dance with Dragons — Davos IV

Manderly's price for White Harbor's knights is a smuggler's errand: Rickon Stark lives, on an island of cannibals and unicorns — bring the boy home, and the North rises.

The chapter retold

Manderly names his price for backing House Stark’s cause openly, and it is not gold or fealty but an errand fit for a smuggler: word has reached White Harbor that Rickon Stark, the last trueborn Stark son thought lost since Winterfell’s sack, is alive on Skagos, an island the mainland regards as home to cannibals and worse. Davos is to sail there, find the boy, and bring him back — the single act that could rally the North behind something more solid than grief and rumor.

The chapter turns Manderly’s earlier theater into strategy at last: a lord too shrewd to commit openly is instead spending Davos, an expendable and deniable envoy, on the one mission that could actually change the North’s arithmetic. For Davos it is a return to what he has always been best at — an impossible, unglamorous errand undertaken out of loyalty rather than ambition — and it sends him, and the reader’s hope for the Starks, toward the edge of the map on the strength of a fat lord’s rumor and a dead king’s memory.
