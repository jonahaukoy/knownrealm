A Clash of Kings — Arya IX

Roose Bolton's Harrenhal is no kinder than the Mountain's; Arya hears the northmen's plans and weighs Jaqen's lesson — a girl can free herself.

The chapter retold

Roose Bolton’s Harrenhal proves no gentler a home than the Mountain’s garrison before it — different banners, same casual cruelty, the castle’s reputation for misfortune apparently earned regardless of who holds it. Arya listens to the northmen’s plans with the practiced invisibility of a servant nobody bothers to notice, weighing everything Jaqen taught her about names and debts against a new thought taking root: a girl doesn’t need a faceless god’s permission to free herself.

It’s a hinge chapter for her arc, quieter than the ones around it — Arya beginning to understand that the power Jaqen offered her (three deaths, a coin, a door across the sea) is less valuable to her right now than simply getting out and going home. The list keeps its names; what’s changing is Arya’s sense of what she’s owed and what she’s willing to take for herself rather than wait to be given.

This movement: The March on the City

  • Stannis's fleet and host converge on King's Landing; Tyrion counts chain links and wildfire pots.
  • Robb wins the Crag in the west while his cause rots behind him; Catelyn frees the Kingslayer for two girls' lives.
  • Arya spends her third death to buy Harrenhal's gate — the man offers more, and Jaqen gives her a coin and a phrase.
  • Jon, taken in the Frostfangs, kills the Halfhand at his order and kneels to Mance Rayder with clean hands and a black heart.
