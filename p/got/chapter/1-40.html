A Game of Thrones — Eddard X

Milk of the poppy dreams: the tower of joy, three white cloaks, and “promise me, Ned”; he wakes to Robert restoring the badge and riding to hunt.

The chapter retold

Bedridden and fevered from his shattered leg, dosed with milk of the poppy against the pain, Ned drifts through dreams of the Tower of Joy — the fight at the end of Robert's Rebellion where he found three of the Mad King's finest knights of the Kingsguard waiting for him, and where, dying, one of them extracted a promise from Ned that the book still refuses to name outright. He wakes from the dream to find Robert at his bedside, guilt-ridden over Ned's injury and the political fallout of Tyrion's imprisonment, restoring the Hand's badge Ned had thrown down and declaring he means to go hunting to clear his head.

The Tower of Joy dream is one of the book's most carefully rationed pieces of information — repeated in fragments across multiple books before its full meaning becomes clear, it is the closest thing the story gives, this early, to a direct hint about the secret Ned has carried since before the novel began. That he surfaces from it straight into ordinary political business, badge restored and a king riding off to hunt as if nothing has changed, is the book's quiet way of showing how thoroughly Ned's private grief stays sealed off from the public man conducting the realm's business around it.
