A Game of Thrones — Sansa III

Sansa dreams of golden-haired princes; told they are going home, she runs weeping to the queen — carrying more than she knows.

The chapter retold

Sansa, still nursing her storybook fantasy of golden princes despite everything the tourney and the road south have already shown her, learns that her father intends to send the family home to Winterfell now that his investigation is winding toward a dangerous conclusion. Horrified at the thought of losing Joffrey and the life she still imagines waiting for her at court, she runs weeping to Queen Cersei rather than to her own parents — and in doing so, without fully understanding what she's handing over, reveals Ned's plans to the one person in the castle most dangerous to know them.

It's a gutting scene precisely because Sansa's motives are so understandable and so innocent — a homesick, love-struck girl confiding in an adult she still trusts, with no way of grasping the stakes of what she's just given away. The betrayal costs nothing in malice and everything in consequence: Cersei now knows Ned means to move against her timeline, and Sansa's fairy tale, which has already taken so much damage, is about to cost her family more than she will ever be told to her face.
