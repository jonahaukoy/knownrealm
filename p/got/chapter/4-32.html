A Feast for Crows — Brienne VI

At the crossroads inn the orphans shelter with a broken man; Rorge comes hunting in the Hound's helm, and Biter's teeth meet Gendry's spear too late to save her face.

The chapter retold

At a crossroads inn, Brienne and her small party find shelter alongside a group of war orphans and a broken, damaged man among them — and violence finds them there too. Rorge, one of the vicious criminals released from King's Landing's black cells earlier in the war, arrives wearing the stolen helm of the Hound, hunting for exactly the kind of vulnerable prey the inn's orphans represent. In the fighting, young Gendry — Robert Baratheon's blacksmith bastard, unexpectedly present — drives a spear through the cannibalistic killer Biter, but not before Biter's teeth have already torn into Brienne's face, leaving her permanently, brutally scarred.

The 'broken man' sheltering among the orphans is left more felt than described — big, silent, moving like a soldier despite his wounds — an echo, deliberate or not, of Septon Meribald's sermon on broken men delivered several chapters earlier, as much as it is a specific character the text stays coy about naming outright. Rorge, still wearing the Hound's stolen helm from a run of terrorizing the countryside, and Biter, feral and cannibalistic even by the standards of the war's worst survivors, are exactly the wreckage that sermon described — men the fighting broke past any peace's ability to mend. Their attack on a room of orphans and one wounded stranger is opportunism, not strategy: the last scraps of the Brave Companions, still hunting whatever looks weakest within reach.

This is one of the book's pivotal chapters, closing out two of the war's ugliest loose threads (Rorge and Biter, remnants of the Brave Companions and King's Landing's freed criminals) in a burst of sudden, costly violence. Brienne survives, but the scarring is not incidental set dressing — it's the physical mark the book leaves on a character whose entire life has already been defined by other people's judgments about her looks, ensuring the wound will follow her, and the reader's sense of her, for the remainder of her arc.
