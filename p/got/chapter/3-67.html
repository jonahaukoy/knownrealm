A Storm of Swords ÔÇö Tyrion IX

The trial of the Imp: bought witnesses parade rehearsed lies until Shae's is the knife that finds him; Tyrion demands trial by combat and damns them all.

The chapter retold

Tyrion Lannister's trial for Joffrey's murder unfolds as pure theater with a predetermined verdict: witnesses paraded before the court recite rehearsed testimony, bought or coerced, each one twisting some real memory of Tyrion's cleverness or cruelty into evidence of regicide. The cruelest blow comes last, from Shae ÔÇö his lover, testifying that he confessed the murder to her in bed, dressed up with humiliating invented detail that turns their entire relationship into evidence against him in open court.

Something in Tyrion breaks with Shae's testimony that his father's contempt and his sister's accusations never quite managed to break. He abandons any hope of a fair hearing and demands trial by combat instead, then turns on the entire assembled court ÔÇö father, sister, kingdom ÔÇö with a fury that damns him politically even as it is, finally, completely honest. The trial ends with Tyrion having lost the room and gained, for the first time in the book, nothing left to lose.
