A Clash of Kings — Daenerys IV

In the House of the Undying the warlocks show Daenerys three fires, three mounts, three treasons — and reach for her; Drogon burns them in their dust.

The chapter retold

In the House of the Undying, Daenerys is shown three visions that read like riddles and feel like prophecy — three fires, three mounts, three treasons — images of a ruined throne room, a wolf and a lion feasting, a blue-eyed king at Winterfell, none of them explained, all of them clearly important. The warlocks who lured her here reveal their true purpose only once she’s deep inside: they mean to feed on her power, or trap her forever among the visions, and their hospitality curdles into an ambush.

Drogon answers where nothing else could — turning on the warlocks with dragonfire and burning the House of the Undying’s masters in their own hall, freeing his mother from a trap built specifically for someone without a dragon. It’s the book’s clearest statement of what Daenerys’s children are actually worth beyond spectacle: not just symbols of Targaryen return, but weapons that make her, for the first time since Drogo’s death, someone who cannot simply be captured by whoever is cleverest in the room. The visions she carries out with her — unexplained, unresolved — will shadow the rest of her story for books to come.

This movement: The March on the City

  • Stannis's fleet and host converge on King's Landing; Tyrion counts chain links and wildfire pots.
  • Robb wins the Crag in the west while his cause rots behind him; Catelyn frees the Kingslayer for two girls' lives.
  • Arya spends her third death to buy Harrenhal's gate — the man offers more, and Jaqen gives her a coin and a phrase.
  • Jon, taken in the Frostfangs, kills the Halfhand at his order and kneels to Mance Rayder with clean hands and a black heart.
