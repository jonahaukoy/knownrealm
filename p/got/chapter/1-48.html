The chapter retold

On a hunt meant to clear his head, Robert is gored by a boar while drunk on strongwine ÔÇö a wound that will prove fatal within days. Bedridden and finally facing his own mortality after seventeen years of treating kingship as an obligation to be endured between hunts and feasts, he dictates a will naming Ned Protector of the Realm until his heir comes of age. Writing the document at the dying king's own request, Ned makes one small, enormous alteration: where the will should name Joffrey, Ned writes only 'my heir,' unwilling to put his signature to a lie about the boy's parentage even in a dead man's final wishes. When Renly Baratheon offers Ned a hundred swords and a chance to seize Joffrey and the throne by force before Robert has even finished dying, Ned refuses ÔÇö the crown is not his to take by ambush, whatever he now knows about who deserves it.

It's the clearest single moment where Ned's honor becomes a form of self-sabotage the reader can see coming and he can't: the honest word choice on the will is scrupulously correct and functionally useless without armed men to enforce it, and turning down Renly's offer of exactly those armed men, on principle, leaves Ned holding the moral high ground and nothing else. Every advantage he might have seized here, he sets aside in favor of doing things properly ÔÇö a virtue everywhere else in his life, and about to become the single costliest choice of it.
