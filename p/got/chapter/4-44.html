A Feast for Crows ÔÇö Cersei X

The Faith comes for the queen: accused by her own catspaw, Cersei paces a cell beneath the Great Sept, writing letters no one hurries to answer.

The chapter retold

The machinery Cersei built with her own hands finally closes on her. Accused by her own catspaw ÔÇö Osney Kettleblack's scourged, garbled confession naming her alongside every sin the Faith cares to punish ÔÇö she is taken from the Red Keep by the Faith Militant she personally re-armed, and marched down into a cell beneath the Great Sept, stripped of guards, title and the small council she stocked with men too weak to help her now. She paces. She writes letters ÔÇö to Jaime, to anyone who might still owe her enough to move quickly ÔÇö and sends them out into a city that is, for the first time in her life, in absolutely no hurry to answer a Lannister's summons.

The cell itself becomes the chapter's real subject: cold stone, a single window too high to reach, the specific indignity of a woman who has spent the whole book insisting she rules better than any man discovering exactly how much of her power lived in other people's fear rather than in anything she actually controlled. The High Sparrow visits, unhurried and unmoved by rank, and makes plain that a trial is coming ÔÇö not the theater of a council chamber, but the Faith's own justice, which does not bend for gold or blood.

This is the book's clearest act of poetic justice, delivered without a trace of the mercy Cersei withheld from everyone she ever outmaneuvered. Every choice that built her isolation arrives at once ÔÇö the flatterers promoted over competent men, the estrangement from Jaime after his refusal of the Handship, Tyrion long gone and blamed for everything, the Faith Militant restored for leverage over Margaery and now turned wholesale on its own architect ÔÇö and there is, genuinely, no one left positioned or willing to intervene. Cersei mistook control for security across her whole regency; this chapter is the bill for that mistake, presented in full.
