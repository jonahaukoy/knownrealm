A Game of Thrones — Daenerys III

On the Dothraki sea Daenerys stops the khalasar to walk in the grass, faces down Viserys for the first time, and learns she carries Drogo's child.

The chapter retold

Riding with the khalasar across the Dothraki sea, Daenerys asks Drogo to halt the column so she can walk out into the endless grass on her own — a small assertion of will that would have been unthinkable for the frightened girl of the book's early chapters. When Viserys, drunk and diminished, tries to bully her in front of her own khalasar handmaids, she stands her ground against him for the first time in her life, refusing the old fear-response that has governed every interaction between them since childhood. In the same stretch she confirms what her body has already told her: she is carrying Drogo's child.

The chapter marks the clearest turning point yet in Daenerys's transformation from property to person — she isn't just enduring her circumstances anymore, she's beginning to actively shape them, testing the authority a khaleesi is owed and finding, to her own surprise, that it holds. Viserys's diminishment runs in exact inverse to her rise: every chapter that makes Daenerys stronger makes her brother correspondingly more desperate and less relevant, a trade the book is already setting up to end badly for him.
