A Game of Thrones — Daenerys VII

The khalasar marches and Drogo's wound festers under a poultice torn away too soon; Daenerys watches her sun-and-stars begin to fall.

The chapter retold

The khalasar breaks camp and marches on, and the small wound Drogo took during the Lhazareen raid, treated with a poultice by Mirri Maz Duur, one of the captured godswomen Daenerys spared, begins to fester after the dressing is torn away too soon in the rush of travel. What should have healed cleanly instead turns, over the course of the ride, into something visibly, frighteningly wrong, and Daenerys watches — helpless in a way she has not been helpless in many chapters — as her husband's strength starts, for the first time since she's known him, to fail.

The chapter turns the small detail planted several chapters back into the central crisis of Daenerys's married life, and it does so with a cruel irony baked into the cause: Mirri Maz Duur's care, offered after Daenerys spared her people from slavery, becomes tangled up in Drogo's decline in ways that raise more questions than the text answers outright. Everything Daenerys has built in this marriage — status, safety, a home of sorts among a people who were never meant to be hers — is suddenly as fragile as the wound closing badly across her husband's chest.
