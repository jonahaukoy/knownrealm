A Game of Thrones — Bran III

In his long sleep Bran falls through darkness while a three-eyed crow shows him the whole realm — and the heart of winter; he wakes, broken but alive.

The chapter retold

Deep in his coma, Bran falls endlessly through darkness in a dream that feels realer than waking life, guided by a three-eyed crow that shows him glimpses of the whole broken realm — his father in a dungeon, his brother marching to war, a wall of ice under a sky gone entirely to winter. When he finally wakes, weeks after his fall, he is alive but changed, his legs forever useless beneath him, though something in him — the crow's visitor's-eye view of the world — has started to open in compensation.

The vision chapter is the book's clearest early signal that its supernatural machinery, mostly kept off-page since the prologue, runs through Bran specifically. His crippling is presented at first purely as tragedy — the active, climbing boy reduced to a body he can no longer trust — but the crow's visit reframes the injury as also a doorway, the first hint that Bran's story will run on entirely different rules than his siblings', watching the realm from angles no map can show.
