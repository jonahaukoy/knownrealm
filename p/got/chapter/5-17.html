A Dance with Dragons — Daenerys III

Yunkai buys sellswords and allies while Astapor dies behind its walls; the queen's peace hangs by threads, and the pale mare has begun to ride.

The chapter retold

Slaver’s Bay tightens around Meereen: Yunkai rebuilds its army with hired swords and old grudges while Astapor, freed months before, collapses behind its own walls into a rotating cast of self-proclaimed kings who rarely outlive their crowns by more than weeks. Daenerys’s peace, already thin, gets thinner with every rider bringing news of massing enemies, and a new horror rides in alongside the political ones — the pale mare, a plague of bloody flux, has begun working its way through the camps outside her city.

This is scale-setting disguised as a status report: the chapter widens the lens from Meereen’s internal politics to the whole disintegrating region, making clear that Daenerys’s dream of a settled, well-governed Slaver’s Bay was always going to be squeezed between enemies she can fight and an epidemic she can’t. The pale mare in particular becomes one of the book’s most effective slow-building threats — an enemy with no throat to cut, spreading through besieging armies and city alike, indifferent to anyone’s claim to a crown.
