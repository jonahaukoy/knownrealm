A Dance with Dragons — Reek III

Winterfell hosts a wedding in the snow: Ramsay Bolton takes “Arya Stark” — Jeyne Poole in a stolen name — and Reek gives the bride away into horror.

The chapter retold

Winterfell hosts a wedding staged entirely for northern eyes: Ramsay Bolton marries the girl the whole North believes to be Arya Stark, though she is in truth Jeyne Poole, the steward’s daughter, dressed and drilled into a dead queen’s daughter’s likeness by people who need the North to believe the Boltons have married into the old blood legitimately. Reek, hollowed out and obedient, is made to give the bride away — the last of Theon Greyjoy’s old name spent on delivering another captive into a horror he knows intimately.

This is one of the book’s cruelest set-pieces, staged as a celebration and functioning as a public act of psychological and physical violence, all the more horrifying for how procedurally the text handles it — a wedding night the narration will not look away from, filtered through Reek’s own broken, self-protective flatness. The chapter cements exactly why Ramsay’s marriage matters politically — a fabricated Stark bride to launder Bolton rule — while making unmistakably clear how much human damage that fabrication is being paid for in.
