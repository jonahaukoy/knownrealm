A Dance with Dragons — Davos II

In the Merman's Court Lord Wyman Manderly condemns Davos loudly for the Freys' benefit; the head that goes up over White Harbor's gates belonged to a criminal with convenient hands.

The chapter retold

In White Harbor’s Merman’s Court, Lord Wyman Manderly receives Davos Seaworth with theatrical fury — loud condemnation of Stannis’s cause, of onion knights, of anyone fool enough to back a losing king, staged for the benefit of the Frey delegation watching from the gallery. It is, unmistakably, a performance, and Davos, no stranger to reading a room for what it’s hiding, understands he’s being kept alive precisely because Manderly needs witnesses to believe the opposite. A criminal’s severed head goes up over the city gates soon after, presented as justice done — except the hands on the corpse don’t match the crime it’s supposed to answer for.

The chapter is a small masterclass in Manderly’s double game: a fat, jolly, seemingly harmless lord using spectacle to hide a rebellion in plain sight, buying time and cover while he decides how far he can trust a smuggler-knight bearing a dead king’s seal. The mismatched hands on the displayed head are the chapter’s quiet alarm bell — a detail too specific to be an accident, planting the suspicion that Manderly’s justice and his grief over his own murdered kin are running on a much longer, much colder clock than anyone in the hall lets on.

This movement: Kings at the Wall

  • Stannis takes the mountain clans and Deepwood Motte, gathering the North that hates the Boltons.
  • Davos is sent to White Harbor to win fat Lord Manderly — and is reported executed, head and hands tarred.
  • In the crypts of Winterfell, the Boltons stage a wedding: 'Arya Stark' — Jeyne Poole in a stolen name — given to Ramsay.
  • Melisandre reads her fires at the Wall: snow, a wooden face, a girl on a dying horse. Jon listens with half an ear.
