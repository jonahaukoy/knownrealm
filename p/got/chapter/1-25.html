A Game of Thrones — Bran IV

Tyrion stops at Winterfell on his way south and gives Bran the gift of riding again — a saddle design — and gets frost from Robb for his kindness.

The chapter retold

Tyrion breaks his journey home at Winterfell and, remembering the crippled boy he met on his first visit north, presents Bran with a design for a special saddle that will let him ride again despite his ruined legs — a genuinely thoughtful gift from a man his hosts still regard with deep suspicion. Robb's response is coldness rather than gratitude; still raw over his mother's accusations against House Lannister and unable to separate Tyrion the individual from Tyrion the family name, he treats the kindness as another Lannister trick rather than what it plainly is.

It's a quietly important character beat for Tyrion, reinforcing the pattern the book keeps returning to: he is, again and again, the Lannister who actually helps the people his family's reputation would suggest he'd ignore or exploit, and he is, again and again, thanked for it with suspicion rather than warmth. The saddle itself becomes a real gift regardless of how it's received — Bran's mobility, and the independence it buys him, will matter for the rest of his arc, engineered by the one Lannister nobody at Winterfell trusts.
