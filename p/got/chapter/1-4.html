A Game of Thrones — Daenerys I

In Illyrio's manse in Pentos, Viserys dresses his sister for sale; Daenerys meets Khal Drogo and learns her wedding will buy her brother an army.

The chapter retold

Across the narrow sea in Pentos, Daenerys Targaryen is dressed by handmaids for her own sale. Her brother Viserys — vain, threadbare, calling himself a king with no kingdom left to rule — has struck a bargain with the magister Illyrio: Daenerys's hand in marriage to the Dothraki warlord Khal Drogo, paid for in advance with a khalasar's worth of swords that Viserys means to use to retake the Iron Throne. She is thirteen, friendless, and entirely without a vote in the transaction; when Drogo finally looks at her at the welcoming feast, it is the first time in the chapter anyone looks at her as anything other than an instrument.

This is the whole shape of Daenerys's early story compressed into one scene: a girl priced, packaged and handed across a table by the one relative who should be protecting her. Viserys's casual cruelty — the promise that he'd let a thousand Dothraki ride through her body if that's what it took to get his throne back — sets the floor she has nowhere to go but up from, and Illyrio's oily hospitality is the first hint that everyone in this transaction has an angle Daenerys hasn't been let in on.
