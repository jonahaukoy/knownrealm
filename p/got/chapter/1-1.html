The chapter retold

The book opens past the edge of the map the rest of the story will live on: three rangers of the Night's Watch, sent to find a missing wildling band, find them all dead in a clearing that should not be so cold. Ser Waymar Royce is young, lordly and contemptuous of Gared's forty years of experience, and his pride keeps the party in the open after dark instead of riding for the Wall. What comes for them out of the trees is not wildlings ÔÇö pale, silent shapes with eyes like burning blue stars, moving in a stillness that human beings do not move in. Royce dies first, cut to ribbons by a blade of what looks like ice.

It is a strange, sideways door into a story that will spend most of its length on courts and castles hundreds of leagues south of here, and that is exactly the point ÔÇö Martin opens with the one truth that outranks every claim, betrothal and murder to follow, then lets the whole realm ignore it for a very long time. Will's report reaches Winterfell as a deserter's tale, believed by no one who matters, and the man who hears it responds the only way the North knows how: with a headsman's block. The reader alone starts the book knowing what the characters spend it forgetting.
