A Feast for Crows — Prologue · Pate

In Oldtown's shadowed taverns the novice Pate trades a stolen Citadel key for a golden dragon — and dies with the coin in his mouth while a stranger picks up his face.

The chapter retold

The book opens nowhere near a throne. A maester named Alleras and the drunk archmaester Pate stand watch over Oldtown's rooftops for a comet, and through them the reader gets its first glimpse of the Citadel as an institution — a place of forging chains, hoarding secrets and arguing about the ravens' true purpose, run by old men who distrust magic even as glass candles start burning in locked rooms. A second Pate, a poor novice who has failed his exams and hates every fat, comfortable maester above him, meets a stranger in a tavern who offers a fortune — a golden dragon coin — for a stolen Citadel key and a night with a girl too pretty for the price. He takes the bait and dies for it, throat opened, the coin left cooling in his own mouth as the stranger walks off wearing a face that is not quite the one it started with.

The chapter is stitched from a single night's worth of overlapping vantage points — Pate's fatal bargain, the older novice Alleras tracking the comet's omens across the rooftops with a friend, and the doomed boy's own dawning, too-late realization that the price of his stolen key was never going to be a coin. The stranger who pays him is sketched only in fragments — a face that seems to shift in torchlight, a voice too pleasant for the errand — and readers who have already met the Faceless Men through Arya's Braavosi chapters will recognize the method a full book before Oldtown's own maesters ever think to look for it.

It is a strange, sideways door into a book that will spend forty-five more chapters almost entirely in the south, and that is the point: A Feast for Crows announces from its first page that this is a story about institutions outliving the people who run them — the Citadel, the Faith, the ironborn's driftwood crown, the Iron Throne itself — and about faceless things wearing familiar skins. No one in King's Landing will hear about a dead novice in Oldtown, and the reader is meant to file the name Pate away and forget it, which is exactly the trick the chapter is built to pull.
