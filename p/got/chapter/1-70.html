A Game of Thrones — Tyrion IX

Word of Ned Stark's death reaches Tywin's camp and ruins every plan; Tywin sends Tyrion to rule King's Landing as Hand — without his whore.

The chapter retold

News of Ned Stark's execution reaches Tywin Lannister's war camp and lands like a second, unplanned battle — the confession-for-mercy bargain Cersei and Varys struck in the capital was never supposed to end in a headsman's block, and Joffrey's impulsive order has just handed the North a martyr and Robb Stark a reason to fight to the last man rather than negotiate. Tywin, furious at the strategic damage his own grandson has caused, makes a decision that startles Tyrion more than almost anything else in the book so far: he is sending Tyrion to King's Landing to rule as acting Hand in his stead, to clean up the mess Cersei's court has made — on the explicit condition that Shae stays behind in the camp.

It's a rare moment of Tywin extending real responsibility to the son he's spent a lifetime treating as an embarrassment, though even this vote of confidence arrives wrapped in contempt — Tyrion is trusted with the capital but not with keeping the woman he loves nearby, a condition that tells him exactly how much personal happiness his father believes a Lannister heir is entitled to. The appointment sets up the next long stretch of Tyrion's story: running a hostile city on behalf of a family that has never once thanked him for solving their problems.
