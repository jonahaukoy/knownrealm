A Clash of Kings — Sansa IV

On the rooftop as the fleet burns closer, the Hound tells Sansa the truth about killing; she sings to herself the songs he says are lies.

The chapter retold

On a rooftop as Stannis’s fleet burns closer to the city, Sandor Clegane finds Sansa and, in his blunt, unsentimental way, tells her the truth about killing — what it actually looks like, what it costs, why the songs she loves lie about all of it. It’s one of the most intimate scenes the two of them share, the Hound’s usual contempt for gentility replaced, briefly, with something like honesty offered as a gift.

Sansa responds the only way she knows how, humming a song to herself even as he insists the songs are lies — not defiance exactly, more a refusal to let him take away the last comfort she has left. The chapter captures the strange, asymmetrical bond between them at its clearest: a frightened girl and a violent, self-loathing man, each offering the other something neither quite knows how to receive.
