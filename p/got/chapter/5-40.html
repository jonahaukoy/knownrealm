A Dance with Dragons — Jon VIII

A banker of Braavos arrives frost-bitten at the Wall; Jon signs the Iron Bank's paper for grain against winter — and lends Tycho Nestoris to find Stannis in the snows.

The chapter retold

A banker from the Iron Bank of Braavos, half-frozen and entirely businesslike, reaches Castle Black with an offer Jon can’t afford to refuse and can barely afford to accept: grain and gold against the Watch’s dwindling stores, at interest, because the Iron Bank does not extend charity, only credit. Jon signs, mortgaging the Night’s Watch’s future to keep it fed through the winter, and sends the banker, Tycho Nestoris, further north still — to find Stannis, whose survival or death the bank apparently has its own reasons to want confirmed.

The chapter is a rare, welcome dose of realpolitik amid the Wall’s supernatural dread — an institution older and colder than any crown, extending exactly enough help to keep its investment alive, no more. Jon’s willingness to sign away the Watch’s finances for grain says everything about how dire the winter math has become, and sending a banker to hunt for a missing king is the kind of quietly absurd errand this book specializes in, dry comedy riding right alongside genuine desperation.
