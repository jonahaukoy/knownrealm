A Dance with Dragons — Daenerys VI

The peace is signed in ceremonies and hostages; Daario goes to the Yunkai'i as surety, and Daenerys learns what a queen may keep and what she must put away.

The chapter retold

The peace with Yunkai is formalized in ceremony after ceremony, hostages exchanged to guarantee good faith on both sides — and Daario Naharis, Daenerys’s lover and the coalition’s least trustworthy variable, is sent to the Yunkai’i camp as surety, removed from her side at the precise moment she most needs someone she actually trusts. The queen who once burned a khal’s corpse to hatch dragons signs papers instead, and feels every signature as a small defeat dressed up as diplomacy.

The chapter is about the gulf between what a queen wants and what a queen is permitted to keep, staged through the mechanics of hostage diplomacy rather than any single dramatic confrontation — Daenerys learns here, in granular, bureaucratic detail, that peace has its own price schedule, and that love is one of the first line items cut. Sending Daario away removes both a complication and a comfort in the same gesture, and the chapter leaves her more isolated at the exact moment her position looks, on paper, most secure.
