A Clash of Kings — Davos II

Davos rows Melisandre beneath Storm's End; in the dark under the castle she births a shadow, and Ser Cortnay Penrose falls from a tower onto nothing.

The chapter retold

Davos rows Melisandre beneath Storm’s End under cover of darkness, uneasy about the errand and unable to refuse his king’s red priestess anything. In a hidden cave carved into the rock beneath the castle, she gives birth — the only word for it — to a shadow with Stannis’s face, a thing conceived of fire and royal blood and set loose to do what an army outside the walls could not.

Ser Cortnay Penrose, castellan of Storm’s End, has held the castle against every threat and refused Stannis’s demand to yield a boy hostage even under siege; he is found broken at the base of a tower by morning, fallen from a height no man pushed him from. It’s one of the book’s most quietly horrifying scenes — Davos, decent and superstitious in equal measure, becomes an unwilling accessory to murder by magic, and the chapter makes plain that Melisandre’s power is not confined to omens and prophecy. It kills, directly, when asked.
