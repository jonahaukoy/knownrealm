A Dance with Dragons — Theon I

The Prince of Winterfell sits below the salt at Ramsay's wedding feast; Abel the bard and his washerwomen keep asking Theon how the castle's walls are watched.

The chapter retold

Theon — the narration finally restoring his name in the chapter heading, though not yet in his own head — sits below the salt at Ramsay’s wedding feast, present but unseated, watching a celebration built on a lie he helped stage. A traveling entertainer called Abel plies the hall with songs while his washerwomen move quietly among the guests, and one of them keeps finding excuses to talk to Theon about the castle’s walls, its watches, its weak points — questions dressed as gossip that are plainly nothing of the kind.

The chapter plants the rescue plot that will consume the rest of Theon’s arc without yet naming its shape: Abel’s troupe is working an angle inside Ramsay’s own wedding, and Theon — broken, terrified, still more Reek than not — is the one man in the castle who could actually answer their questions usefully, if he can remember how to be dangerous instead of obedient. It is the first flicker, faint and unreliable, of Theon Greyjoy stirring back to life inside what Ramsay built.
