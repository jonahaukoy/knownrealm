A Dance with Dragons — Daenerys VII

Daenerys weds Hizdahr zo Loraq in the Temple of the Graces, buying Meereen's peace with a Ghiscari crown — and burying what she wants beneath what is needed.

The chapter retold

Daenerys marries Hizdahr zo Loraq in the Temple of the Graces, a Ghiscari ceremony conducted with all the old city’s pageantry, and becomes, in the same hour, both Meereen’s queen by conquest and its queen by marriage — a legitimacy she never particularly wanted, purchased at the cost of every private wish the wedding quietly buries. The peace it buys is real, on paper: an end, however uneasy, to nightly killings and a truce with the coalition camped outside her walls.

The chapter reads Daenerys’s own wedding as an act of self-suppression rather than romance, the queen choosing the marriage that keeps her city breathing over the desires — for Daario, for unfettered rule, for something simpler than diplomacy — that she has spent the whole book learning to set aside. It’s a quiet, sad triumph: peace achieved, at exactly the price the book has been signaling all along that peace like this always costs a person like her.
