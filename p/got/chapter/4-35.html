A Feast for Crows — Arya III

Each night the girl confesses: Arya of House Stark, Needle under a stone. After Dareon dies, the kindly man gives her warm milk — and she wakes blind.

The chapter retold

In the House of Black and White, Arya's nightly ritual of confession continues — each night she must recite who she truly is: Arya of House Stark, with Needle hidden beneath a stone rather than surrendered to the House along with everything else she's been asked to give up. After Dareon's death is discovered and its author suspected, the kindly man who serves as her principal teacher gives her a cup of warm milk that is not what it appears to be — and Arya wakes to find herself blind.

This is a brutal, deliberately ambiguous punishment: the novel never states outright whether the blinding is retribution for Arya's unsanctioned killing of Dareon, a test of her commitment to becoming no one, or simply the House's method of stripping away the last vestige of self-interest a novice clings to. What's unambiguous is the cost — Arya's refusal to fully surrender her identity (Needle under a stone, her name still hers in the dark) has just been answered with the loss of her sight, the sharpest possible statement that the Faceless Men's bargain is not negotiable.
