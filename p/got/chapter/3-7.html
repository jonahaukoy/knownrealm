A Storm of Swords — Sansa I

The Queen of Thorns asks Sansa plainly what Joffrey is; the truth — “a monster” — buys her a Highgarden dream: gentle Willas, and roses instead of lions.

The chapter retold

Sansa Stark is interrogated with total gentleness by the sharpest woman in King's Landing. Olenna Tyrell, the Queen of Thorns, wants to know — plainly, over needlework, in a tone that makes lying feel unnecessary — exactly what kind of boy Joffrey Baratheon is before her family marries him to Margaery. Sansa, who has spent a year learning that survival means saying nothing true out loud, gives Olenna the single honest word she has been holding back from everyone else in the capital: a monster. It buys her, for one glowing afternoon, the fantasy of an escape — a betrothal instead to gentle, crippled Willas Tyrell, heir of Highgarden, roses instead of lions.

The chapter is Sansa's first real taste of being useful rather than ornamental, and it is also a trap she cannot see the shape of yet: the Tyrells are gathering intelligence, not offering charity, and the dream of Highgarden exists only until it becomes convenient for someone with more power than either Sansa or the Tyrells to notice her value and cancel it. No one dies here, but a hope is born that the rest of the book will spend systematically killing.
