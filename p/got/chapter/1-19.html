A Game of Thrones — Catelyn IV

Catelyn reaches King's Landing in secret and is fetched to Littlefinger, who names the dagger's owner: Tyrion Lannister — a lie with a long tail.

The chapter retold

Catelyn arrives in King's Landing in secret, trusting no one, and is intercepted by Petyr Baelish — Littlefinger, an old friend from her Riverrun girlhood who once loved her enough to duel her betrothed for the privilege and lost. He offers her what she came south for: the name of the dagger's owner, the one used against Bran. He tells her it belongs to Tyrion Lannister, won, he says, in a wager at the tourney celebrating Prince Joffrey's nameday. Catelyn, already primed to believe the worst of the Lannisters, takes the information as confirmation rather than as the claim of one man with his own tangled history and motives.

It's a masterstroke of dramatic irony: the reader has already watched Jaime, not Tyrion, push Bran from the tower, so Littlefinger's information — whether he believes it himself or is lying outright — sends Catelyn's investigation down entirely the wrong branch of the family tree. Her trust in an old friend's word, offered so smoothly and so conveniently, becomes the single decision that turns a private assassination attempt into a public war between two great houses.
