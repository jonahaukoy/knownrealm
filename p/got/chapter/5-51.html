A Dance with Dragons — Daenerys VIII

Peace by ceremony: the pits will open, the king smiles, the Harpy's murders have stopped — and at a feast, someone seasons the honeyed locusts for the queen who never eats them.

The chapter retold

Meereen’s peace gets its public unveiling: the fighting pits reopen under royal sanction, Hizdahr smiles through a ceremony designed to prove the Harpy’s murders have finally stopped, and for one afternoon the city plays at being healed. At the celebratory feast that follows, someone seasons a dish of honeyed locusts meant for the queen — an assassination attempt that lands, or doesn’t, depending on habits nobody but Daenerys’s own household would know to exploit.

The chapter’s surface calm is the whole point — Meereen performing normalcy for its own benefit as much as anyone watching from outside — and the poisoning attempt puts a hairline crack through it that the text refuses to resolve cleanly, leaving Hizdahr’s role, and his innocence, deliberately unsettled. Whatever peace this marriage bought is shown here to be exactly as fragile as everything else Daenerys has built in this city: real enough to celebrate, thin enough that one dish of locusts can nearly undo it.
