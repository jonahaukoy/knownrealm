A Storm of Swords — Davos V

The Hand who can barely read learns his letters beside Edric Storm — and reads in Melisandre's fires exactly what she wants of king's blood.

The chapter retold

On Dragonstone, Davos Seaworth — a smuggler who never had cause to learn his letters — sits beside young Edric Storm, Robert Baratheon's acknowledged bastard, and finally starts learning to read, a small, humanizing thread of ordinary life running alongside the war's much larger machinery. The lessons matter more than they first appear: reading lets Davos start piecing together, in Melisandre's own fire-visions and writings, exactly what she intends to do with king's blood — and Edric Storm's blood in particular.

The chapter quietly arms Davos with the specific knowledge he will need to act later, turning a tender scene between an unlikely tutor and student into a slow-fuse plot point. Melisandre's plans for Edric are never stated outright here, but the shape of the danger is clear enough that the reader, like Davos, starts counting down.
