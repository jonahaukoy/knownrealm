A Game of Thrones — Jon III

Castle Black hardens Jon; he turns the recruits who hated him into something like brothers, and Benjen rides north on a ranging without him.

The chapter retold

At Castle Black, Jon discovers the Night's Watch is nothing like the songs promised — a garrison of thieves, poachers and debtors doing time rather than storied heroes holding a legendary line — and his first instinct, wounded pride at being lumped in with criminals, curdles into something better when Ser Alliser Thorne, the master-at-arms, singles him out for cruelty in the training yard. Jon starts quietly teaching the recruits who hate him — Pyp, Grenn and the rest — the swordcraft his highborn education gave him for free, turning resentment into something closer to brotherhood. In the same stretch, his uncle Benjen Stark rides out on a ranging beyond the Wall without taking Jon along, a rejection Jon feels more sharply than he lets anyone see.

The chapter does the quiet work of building Jon's found family before the plot needs it — Sam Tarly hasn't arrived yet, but the recruits Jon wins over here will matter for the rest of his story. It also plants Benjen's ranging as an open thread: rangers who go beyond the Wall in this book do not always come back on schedule, and the uncle who might have explained Jon's parentage rides off into the one place no letter can follow him.
