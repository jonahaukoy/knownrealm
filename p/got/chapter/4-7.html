A Feast for Crows — Arya I

The Titan of Braavos straddles the harbor; a copper coin and a word open the House of Black and White, where all men must serve: valar dohaeris.

The chapter retold

Arya Stark arrives in Braavos with nothing but a coin, a name — Jaqen H'ghar's parting gift, valar morghulis — and a list of enemies she recites like a prayer. The Titan of Braavos straddles the harbor, immense and alien, and Arya's arrival in the free city is rendered as culture shock: canals instead of roads, a language she does not speak, a girl utterly friendless in the most theatrical city in the known world. She finds her way, eventually, to a plain building with a door of ebony and weirwood, presents the coin, and speaks the password — valar dohaeris, all men must serve — and is taken in by the House of Black and White, the temple of the many-faced god of death.

This chapter formally opens Arya's apprenticeship among the Faceless Men, a religious order built entirely around the idea that death is a gift and identity a costume to be shed. It reframes her entire arc — assassin's list, sword skill, the survival instincts hammered into her since Ned's execution — as training she was already halfway through before she ever knew the school existed. The House's servants who greet her (kindly, opaque, unnamed) establish the order's central bargain immediately: everything is available here, at the price of everything she is.

This movement: The Crows Gather

  • The war winds down and the scavengers move in: the riverlands starve, the Faith arms, the Citadel schemes.
  • Cersei rules for Tommen and stuffs the council with flatterers; Jaime refuses to be one of them.
  • In the Iron Islands, Balon's death calls a kingsmoot — Aeron Damphair means to drown the succession in salt.
  • Brienne rides out with Oathkeeper hunting a girl of three-and-ten with auburn hair.
