A Clash of Kings — Arya I

Yoren's ragged column creaks out of King's Landing: Arya is “Arry” now, an orphan boy among orphan boys, with Gendry, Hot Pie, and three chained men in a wagon.

The chapter retold

Arya’s war starts as a costume change. Fleeing King’s Landing in Yoren’s charge, she has cropped her hair, taken a boy’s name — Arry — and folded herself into a wagon of orphans, thieves and two chained prisoners bound for the Wall. It is a demotion she chooses on purpose: better an anonymous boy on the road than a highborn girl worth a bounty, and the disguise costs her nothing she isn’t glad to shed, needlework and skirts included. Gendry, the armorer’s apprentice with a face he doesn’t know is royal, and Hot Pie, more baker than fighter, become her first traveling companions in a life that will run through a great many more.

What the chapter really establishes is method: Arya survives by disappearing into whoever she’s pretending to be, and the practice starts here, badly and out of necessity, long before Braavos gives it a name. The column creaking north is also the book’s clearest snapshot of what the war looks like from underneath — not banners and battles but hungry children being moved like cargo, with Yoren’s rough decency the only thing standing between them and worse. Every name Arya will later add to her list has its grammar taught to her on this road.
