A Dance with Dragons — Davos I

The onion knight comes ashore at Sisterton in a smuggler's boots; Lord Borrell serves him sister's stew, old grudges, and a warning about White Harbor.

The chapter retold

Davos Seaworth, sent north by Stannis on a quiet errand, comes ashore at Sisterton in a smuggler’s old boots and a lord’s borrowed authority, looking for allies for a king whose fortunes have thinned to almost nothing. Lord Godric Borrell of the Sisters receives him with fish stew, old grudges against the mainland houses, and no particular hurry to commit to anyone — the Sisters have survived every war in living memory by picking sides late, and Borrell isn’t about to break the habit for a knight of onions and a claim that looks more hopeless by the week.

The chapter reintroduces Davos as the story’s most useful kind of nobody — a smuggler’s son whose low birth lets him go places and ask things a highborn envoy never could, exactly the reason Stannis keeps sending him on the missions that matter most. Borrell’s warning about White Harbor, delivered almost as an afterthought, seeds the far more dangerous errand waiting for Davos ahead: Wyman Manderly, the fat lord of a rich port, is a bigger and much more dangerous unknown than a minor island lord’s fish stew and grievances.
