A Dance with Dragons — Daenerys II

Xaro Xhoan Daxos comes with thirteen ships and one price — that she sail away and be a queen somewhere else; Daenerys chains her dragons after Hazzea, and refuses him war instead.

The chapter retold

Xaro Xhoan Daxos, the Qartheen merchant prince who once tried to buy Daenerys with promises and thirteen ships, arrives in Meereen still selling the same pitch with a new price tag: sail away, be a queen somewhere convenient, and let Meereen sort out its own mess. Daenerys, freshly grieving Hazzea and newly aware of what her free dragon can do to a farmer’s child, answers him not with fire but with chains — she has Viserion and Rhaegal, the two dragons she can still reach, shackled deep beneath the Great Pyramid, a decision that costs her something every reader can feel even through the page.

The scene is a masterclass in Daenerys refusing the easiest version of herself twice over: she turns down Xaro’s offer of an escape from responsibility, and she turns down the instinct to answer horror with more horror, choosing restraint over vengeance for the sake of children she will never meet. Chaining her own dragons is presented as an act of conscience that will not read as one to history — a queen who tames herself instead of her enemies, in a story that will spend the rest of the book testing whether restraint like that can survive contact with power.
