A Dance with Dragons — Tyrion VI

Chained to Ser Jorah in Volantis, Tyrion meets the widow of the waterfront, who sells passages and prophecies — and a dwarf girl named Penny, who does not want to kill him. Much.

The chapter retold

Chained to Jorah Mormont on the long road to Volantis, Tyrion talks his way into an unlikely bond with the widow of the waterfront — a woman running a trade in ships, secrets and forged papers for anyone with coin and the right introductions — and into an even less likely friendship with Penny, a dwarf performer traveling with her own small troupe, who takes an immediate, unshakeable liking to a fellow little person she has no idea just killed his own father.

The chapter softens Tyrion just enough to matter: Penny wants nothing from him but company, the first relationship in the whole eastward journey not built on leverage, and it costs Tyrion visibly to accept kindness he can’t immediately turn into an angle. Jorah watches the two of them with the flat patience of a man who has already decided what Tyrion is worth to him in Meereen, and the tension between Penny’s warmth and Jorah’s transaction sets up exactly the kind of moral discomfort this stretch of the book specializes in.
