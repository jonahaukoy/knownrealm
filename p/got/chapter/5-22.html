A Dance with Dragons — Jon V

The Wall's larders shrink as the free folk swell; word comes of thousands more starving at Hardhome, where the dead walk the shore — a rescue no one can afford.

The chapter retold

Word reaches Castle Black of a catastrophe unfolding at Hardhome, a squalid refugee town on the northern coast where thousands of free folk who never followed Mance are now trapped and starving — and where, the reports insist with increasing hysteria, the dead have begun coming up out of the sea itself. Jon has no ships to spare, no food to spare, and no political capital to spend on a rescue that will only deepen his officers’ conviction that their Lord Commander cares more for wildlings than for his own brothers.

The chapter forces the Hardhome question onto the table without yet forcing an answer — a rescue no one can afford set against a death toll that keeps this book’s supernatural threat honest, reminding the reader that every chapter spent on succession politics and marriage pacts is happening while an actual army of the dead recruits new soldiers from the starving and the desperate. Jon’s problem is no longer whether the wildlings deserve saving; it’s whether the Watch can save anyone at all with the little it has left.
