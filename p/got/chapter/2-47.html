A Clash of Kings — Bran VI

By night Bran and Rickon come out of the crypts they never left; Winterfell believes its princes fled, and Theon must produce bodies or be laughed at.

The chapter retold

By night, Bran and Rickon slip quietly out of the crypts they never actually left — the story of their escape from Winterfell has been a fiction maintained since Theon’s conquest, the two boys hidden below ground with Hodor, Osha and the direwolves while the castle above believes them fled. Theon, desperate for a story that saves face, needs bodies to prove his missing princes are dead rather than admit two children have simply vanished from under his nose.

The deception the chapter builds is precarious and morally costly even in its cleverness — hiding successfully means someone else may have to pay for the lie’s plausibility. Theon’s mounting panic, unable to produce what he needs and unwilling to admit failure to his own men, sets up one of the book’s darkest turns, still a few chapters off but already visible in the shape of his desperation here.
