A Clash of Kings — Arya VIII

Harrenhal under Bolton: Arya becomes the lord's cupbearer; Jaqen pays his last debt, offers her the iron coin, and changes his face. Valar morghulis.

The chapter retold

At Harrenhal, now Roose Bolton’s, Arya has become the lord’s cupbearer, close enough to real power to be genuinely useful and dangerous both. Jaqen H’ghar, having paid the last life owed on his debt, offers her something stranger than a favor — an iron coin and the words that will open a door across the Narrow Sea, and then, in front of her, changes his own face entirely, proving what he is beyond any more argument.

Valar morghulis — all men must die — is the phrase he leaves her with, the first time the book’s most quietly enormous piece of worldbuilding surfaces on the page. Arya doesn’t yet understand what she’s been offered, only that the strange, quiet killer who owed her three deaths has just shown her that everything she thought she knew about faces and names and killing was smaller than the truth.
