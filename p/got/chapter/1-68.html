A Game of Thrones — Sansa VI

Joffrey shows Sansa her father's head on the walls and promises her Robb's next; the Hound's rough counsel keeps her alive on the battlements.

The chapter retold

Joffrey summons Sansa to the battlements to show her a fresh horror: her father's head, mounted on a spike among the traitors, and he promises her — with the casual cruelty that is by now entirely familiar — that Robb's will join it soon enough. Septa Mordane, who raised Sansa in every courtly grace that has proven useless against exactly this kind of viciousness, has already been executed and her own head set beside her lord's on the wall. It is the Hound, of all people, who talks Sansa through the ordeal without her fainting or breaking in front of the boy who would enjoy watching her do either — rough, unsentimental counsel that keeps her alive on the battlements in the only currency that matters there: composure Joffrey can't take credit for breaking.

The scene is the book's cruelest inversion of the courtly world Sansa was raised to expect: the man showing her mercy, if it can even be called that, is the same scarred, violent knight whose burn story horrified her chapters ago, while the boy she once dreamed of marrying delivers atrocity as a kind of courtship gift. Sansa's survival here is not passive endurance so much as the first real exercise of the skill that will define the rest of her arc — keeping her face still and her voice steady while everything underneath it screams, because in this castle, that composure is the only armor left to her.
