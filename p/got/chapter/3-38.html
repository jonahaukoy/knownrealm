A Storm of Swords — Jaime V

In Harrenhal's bathhouse the Kingslayer bleeds out the truth at last: wildfire under the city, a mad king's last command, and the oath that broke his oaths.

The chapter retold

In Harrenhal's bathhouse, half-delirious with fever and looser-tongued on wine than he has been in seventeen years of practiced silence, Jaime Lannister finally tells someone the truth about the night he killed Aerys II Targaryen. Brienne of Tarth, scrubbing the grime and stink of captivity off him because there is no one else to do it, becomes the first person he has ever trusted with the real story — not confession exactly, but something closer to a man setting down a weight he has carried so long he has forgotten how much it costs to hold.

The story itself reorders everything the reader thought they knew about the Kingslayer: Aerys, mad past reasoning, had wildfire caissons hidden throughout King's Landing by the pyromancer Rossart, enough to incinerate the entire city — half a million people, friend and enemy alike — rather than surrender it to Robert's rebels. Jaime's white cloak meant nothing against that order. He killed his king from behind, seconds before the command could be carried out, and the realm rewarded the act that saved it with a name — Kingslayer — that erased every reason behind it, because no one thought to ask why a Kingsguard knight would break the oldest oath he had.

Jaime finishes the story and faints in Brienne's arms, spent by fever and by the sheer weight of finally being believed rather than judged. The chapter is the emotional hinge of the entire book: it recasts seventeen years of contempt as a tragedy of silence rather than a character flaw, cements the bond between Jaime and Brienne on a foundation deeper than captivity, and gives Jaime, for the first time since the reader has known him, a reason to want to be understood rather than merely feared.
