A Clash of Kings — Jon II

Whitetree and its hollow weirwood, then more empty villages; the Watch rides deeper into a silence where eight thousand wildlings should be.

The chapter retold

The Watch’s ranging pushes deeper into the haunted forest, past Whitetree’s hollow weirwood with its carved red face, and finds more of what chapter seven promised: villages emptied without a fight, no bodies, no signs of struggle, just absence. Two hundred rangers move through country that should hold eight thousand wildlings by Mormont’s own count, and the silence gets less explicable with every mile.

It’s a chapter built entirely out of accumulating wrongness rather than incident — no battle, no revelation, just the growing certainty among seasoned rangers that something has cleared this land of people, and that whatever did it wasn’t Mance Rayder’s army passing through peacefully. The dread here is procedural, which is exactly what makes it effective: professionals who don’t spook easily are spooked.
