A Dance with Dragons — Reek II

Reek rides to Moat Cailin under a peace banner to talk the ironmen out of an unwinnable ruin; Ramsay flays the garrison anyway, and Lord Bolton comes home to the North.

The chapter retold

Ramsay sends Reek to Moat Cailin under a peace banner to talk the last starving ironborn holdouts into surrender — a task Theon, or what’s left of him, can still perform because he remembers exactly how desperate men reason. He succeeds, more or less: the garrison lays down arms on the promise of safe passage. Ramsay flays them anyway, banner and word be damned, and rides north to formally hand the North back to his father, Lord Roose Bolton, who returns to Winterfell to claim, in cold legal language, what his son took in blood.

The chapter shows exactly what Ramsay’s tools are for and exactly what they cost: Reek’s remaining usefulness lies in the fact that broken men are excellent liars because they’ve stopped believing promises mean anything themselves, and Ramsay exploits that with total indifference to the garrison’s trust. Roose Bolton’s arrival reframes the horror in political terms — House Bolton no longer needs to hide its methods, because it has already won the North on paper, and paper, this book keeps insisting, is worth exactly as much as the sword standing behind it.
