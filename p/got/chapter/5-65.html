A Dance with Dragons — Arya II

The ugly little girl gets her sight back and her first gift to give: an old man on the docks who sells death sheets — and a new face from the hall beneath the temple.

The chapter retold

With her sight returned as reward for surviving her blind apprenticeship, the girl in Braavos is given her first true assignment by the Faceless Men: an old man on the docks who sells insurance against death at sea, a small, ordinary fraud whose particular scam has apparently offended someone enough to pay for his ending. She completes the task and, in the same visit to the House of Black and White, is offered something new — a face from the hall of the dead, a mask she can wear to become someone else entirely.

The chapter marks a genuine graduation in Arya’s training, moving her from passive observer to active instrument for the first time, and the ease with which she carries it out is the chapter’s real point of unease — she is good at this, disturbingly good, and the House’s reward system is designed to keep rewarding exactly the qualities that are slowly erasing the girl underneath. Every gift the Faceless Men give her — sight, a face, competence — is also, quietly, a further payment toward the price they’re still trying to collect: her name.
