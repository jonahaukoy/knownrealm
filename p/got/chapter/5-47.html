A Dance with Dragons — Theon III

A ghost walks Winterfell by night killing Bolton men one by one; Little Walder Frey is found dead, the lords' knives come out — and Abel's plan needs Theon whole.

The chapter retold

Someone is killing Ramsay’s men inside Winterfell’s walls at night, methodically and without leaving a trace worth following, and the mystery finally claims a name the whole castle recognizes: Little Walder Frey, Ramsay’s own squire, found dead beneath the snow with his skull broken in. Suspicion turns the garrison against itself exactly as intended, and Abel’s people — spearwives working under cover of a bard’s traveling show — signal to Theon that whatever they’re planning needs him whole, functioning, and ready sooner than he’s prepared for.

This is the book’s most effective slow-burn thriller sequence, a closed-castle murder mystery layered over a rescue operation layered over Theon’s fragile psychological recovery, each thread pulling the others tighter. Little Walder’s death is a real, named casualty rather than an abstract rumor, proof the ghost stalking Winterfell is neither legend nor accident — and it forces Ramsay’s paranoid garrison into exactly the kind of chaos a rescue attempt needs to succeed.
