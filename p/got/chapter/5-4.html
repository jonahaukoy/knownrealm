A Dance with Dragons — Jon I

The new Lord Commander divides spoils and grudges with King Stannis; Melisandre offers Jon her fires and a warning — daggers in the dark.

The chapter retold

Jon Snow, a few weeks into his lord commandership, is dividing the practical wreckage of two armies — Mance Rayder’s defeated wildlings and Stannis Baratheon’s stalled invasion — while trying not to let either king notice how thin the Watch’s resources really are. Stannis wants soldiers and grain and Jon’s blessing for a northern campaign; Jon needs the Wall held and the free folk fed, and neither need has much patience for the other’s war. Melisandre, drawn to him in a way she can’t quite explain even to herself, offers her fires and her counsel, and gets an answer that is respectful, wary, and entirely unconvinced — until she tells him she has seen daggers in the dark, meant for him.

It’s a chapter about the impossible math of command: Jon inherited a Watch too poor to be neutral and a war he cannot afford to lose, wedged between a king who might win and a dead army that certainly will. Melisandre’s warning lands and does not land — Jon has enemies enough among his own brothers already, men who never forgave him the wildlings, without needing a red priestess’s visions to tell him so — but the line is dropped early and precisely, the kind of warning a book likes to let a reader forget until it matters unbearably.
